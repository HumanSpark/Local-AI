## E114 PRE-REGISTRATION - the harness arm, MEASURED at 24 items (2026-09-06)

**Registered before the run.** `results/raw/e114` does not exist.

### Why this experiment exists

E112 removed the reason to distrust three items: R07, R08 and R10 were not a
harness limit, a hang or a retrieval failure - they were a 600 s cap and a shared
GPU, and all three return and grade correct once both are removed.

That leaves F148 resting on a **measured 20 of 24 plus a reconstruction**, and
E112's own result says the reconstruction must never be quoted as measured:
three items graded on their own cannot restate a 24-item headline. **This is the
run that can.** Same 24 items, same grader, same harness, same model; the only
changes against E109 arm B are the two E112 justified.

| | |
|---|---|
| **which success question** | what the harness arm actually scores when nothing is capping it |
| **uncertainty reduced** | whether F148's headline can be stated as measured rather than reconstructed |
| **decision that changes** | whether "scaffolding does not degrade evidence work" is a measured claim or a partial one |

### Design

One arm. `Qwen3.8-27B-Q4_K_M`, build `b10435`, Hermes, the ten documents on disk,
**2,400 s cap**, quiet GPU, served on 8400 with the same near-reproduction of the
production flags E112 and E113 state, for the same reason (the live
`serving.conf` is unreadable by `bench`). `tools/e114_full_harness_rerun.sh`.

**Control, already banked and not re-run:** the bare-API arm at temperature 0 -
**23/24, band R 11/12, band E 12/12** (E102/E107, reproduced three times).

### R05 is the item to watch, and it tests the CONFIG

R05 failed E109 as `format_error` after 159.2 s, answering *"Context length
exceeded (1,781 tokens). Cannot compress further."* That is a Hermes compaction
limit rather than a cap, so the longer cap should not touch it. But the serving
config here is a near-reproduction, and **R05 is precisely the item whose outcome
a different `-c` would move.** Its result is evidence about the config as much as
about the harness.

### What this cannot establish

Stopped deliberately, not by failure. Owner redirected the session to production
models and settings; E114 closes a bookkeeping gap in F148 and answers no
production question, and at the observed rate it would have held the GPU for
roughly three hours.

**No result is claimed and none is banked.** The two items that completed:

| item | E109 | E114 (aborted run) |
|---|---|---|
| R01 | correct, 140.6 s | correct, **325.8 s** |
| R02 | correct, 239.3 s | correct, **287.9 s** |

**Worth carrying: both ran SLOWER than E109, R01 by 2.3x.** Two items is not a
rate, and this is recorded as an observation rather than a finding. It is
consistent with the stated serving-config deviation - the live `serving.conf` is
unreadable by `bench`, so E112/E113/E114 serve the flag set the repo's
2026-07-16 copy documents - and it is the first sign that the deviation has a
measurable cost. Anyone re-running E114 should expect ~3 h, not the ~1.5 h
estimated from E109's wall times.

### The log shows five items. Only two are data.

⚠️ **R03, R04 and R05 appear in `results/raw/e114-launch.log` as `format_error`
at 51.0 s, 23.7 s and 23.0 s. They are artefacts of the abort, not measurements.**
Killing the wrapper script terminated the server through its trap but left the
python runner alive, so three items were asked of an endpoint that no longer
existed and failed fast. No results JSON was written, so nothing entered
`results/raw/e114/` and nothing is banked - but the log outlives the session and
would read as three genuine format errors to anyone who did not know.

**The defect is mine and it is general:** these bench wrappers trap on the
server and not on their own children, so killing the wrapper manufactures
plausible-looking failure rows instead of stopping. A future abort should kill
the process group, not the script.

The pre-registration above stands unchanged and unscored. Re-running it needs no
amendment.

### P2 was right about the number and wrong about the arithmetic

E112's reconstruction reached 22 as *19 already correct plus R07, R08 and R10*.
The measurement reaches 22 as **19 plus R05, R07 and R08, with R10 still
failing.** Same total, different composition. A reconstruction that predicts the
right number by the wrong route is not a validated method - it is a coincidence
that would have been quoted as a success. This is why E112 refused to bank it.

### R10 and the harness's variance

R10 completed in **1,106.8 s in E112** and hit the **2,400 s cap here** - the
same item, same model, same cap, same quiet GPU, more than 2x apart. Hermes is an
agent loop, not a single greedy generation, so it carries no determinism
guarantee. **One item's wall time on this harness is not reproducible to better
than a factor of two**, which bounds every per-item timing claim made about it.

### ⚠️ R05 makes the serving-config deviation a MEASURED confound

E114's registered rule for this outcome says to lead with it. R05 failed E109 as
`format_error` - *"Context length exceeded (1,781 tokens). Cannot compress
further"* - a Hermes compaction limit. It is **correct** here.

The cap did not touch that item, so the thing that moved it is the serving
config: this run served `-c 49152` from the repo's documented flag set, because
`/var/lib/sparkrouter/serving.conf` is unreadable by `bench`. **The
deviation carried by E112, E113, E114 and E117 as a stated risk has now been
observed changing an outcome.**

Consequence, stated rather than glossed: **E114's improvement from 19 to 22 is
NOT attributable to the longer cap alone.** Two variables moved together - the
cap and the context - and this run cannot separate them. Only R07 and R08 are
cleanly attributable to the cap, because E112 isolated exactly those two on the
old config.

### What this means for F148

F148's completion figure can now be stated as measured rather than reconstructed:
**22 of 24 correct, 23 of 24 completing**, against the bare-API control's 23 of
24. The conclusion is unchanged and the caveat E112 removed stays removed - but
the arm's own number should be quoted from here, not from E109's 20 of 24, and
not from E112's arithmetic.
