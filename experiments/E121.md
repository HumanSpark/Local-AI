## E121 PRE-REGISTRATION - the WORKHORSE at matched effort, against Nemotron (2026-09-07)

**Registered before the run.** `results/raw/e121` does not exist.

### Why this experiment exists

The production question the owner asked is which models and settings belong in
the slots. **The workhorse slot is the one that matters most: it is the model
that stays loaded.** It is currently `Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507-Q4_K_M`.

F118 measured that workhorse at **0 of 8 walked periods on l4 where
Nemotron-3-Nano scored 7 of 8** - same architecture class, ~3.5B active,
slightly larger on disk. E118 has now measured Nemotron at matched `low` effort
and found it unchanged. **The workhorse has never been measured at `low`**, so
the comparison that would justify or refuse a workhorse swap does not exist.

E118's own conclusion is why this is worth running rather than assuming: matched
effort moved NOTHING for Nemotron. If effort also moves nothing for the
workhorse, the two are cleanly comparable at last.

| **uncertainty reduced** | whether the workhorse's l4 collapse is a capability limit or an effort artefact |

### THE ANSWER, and it reframes F118

> **Against the model actually in the slot, Nemotron is more than twice as
> accurate on both tiers and no less safe.** It declines correctly where the
> workhorse over-claims (l4 `U1`), and both over-claim identically on l3 `Z1`.

F118 wrote that Nemotron "is not safer than the workhorse, it is differently
unsafe". **That sentence compared Nemotron to the CEILING, not to the
incumbent.** F118's "both capable models" are the ones scoring 12/12 on l3;
the workhorse scores 4/12 and was never one of them. A swap decision compares
the candidate to what is deployed, and on that comparison the liability argument
does not hold: **the workhorse over-claims on BOTH unanswerable items, Nemotron
on one.**

### ⚠️ "Matched effort" is probably a misnomer for this arm, and I registered it wrongly

`Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507` is an INSTRUCT model with no thinking mode. Its
per-item wall times here are **0.36-2.85 s against Nemotron's 9-268 s**, and it
scored **identically to F118's `default` arm on both tiers**. The economical
explanation is that `reasoning_effort` is INERT for this model and the flag did
nothing.

**So P2 held trivially rather than informatively.** It was registered as
"effort moves nothing for it either, as it moved nothing for Nemotron", implying
an active setting that failed to bite. If the setting was never active, the
prediction tested nothing, and E121 is not a matched-effort comparison - it is
**"the workhorse as it is" against "Nemotron at low"**. That is still the right
production comparison, but it is a different claim from the one registered and
the difference is not cosmetic.

**Registered check, not an assertion:** E115b's technique settles it cheaply -
send this model a level its template cannot know and see whether it returns
HTTP 500 (the level is parsed and rejected) or answers normally (the level is
ignored). Until that runs, "matched effort" must not be claimed for this arm.

### What this cannot establish

- One run per tier. Nothing about throughput or footprint, which E122 measures

- **Nothing about the abstention bank**, where a 21-item test would settle the
  over-claim question properly. It is unrunnable: E120 found the bank failing
  its own integrity gate.
- Nothing about the smart slot, which is a different model and a different
  question.
