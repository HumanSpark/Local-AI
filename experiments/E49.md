## E49: separating `-b` from `-ub` - which flag actually moved the prefill wall? (2026-08-17, pre-registered before any run)

**Why this exists, and it is a flaw in my own arm B.** E48 arm B cleared the
F72 prefill wall, but it changed **two** flags at once: `-b` 2048 -> 512 and
`-ub` 512 -> 128. So "the wall is a tunable" is established and **"`-ub` is the
tunable" is not** - arm B cannot attribute its own result. This is the
comparison-integrity rule applied to my own work: one variable, or you have
measured a combination and named it after one of its parts.

**A second thing arm B did not predict and cannot explain.** Prefill ran at
**~132 tok/s against the default configuration's 49.57** - about 2.7x FASTER,
where I registered 15-45 tok/s on the reasoning that smaller dispatches cost
efficiency. Being wrong about the direction means the mechanism is not what I
assumed. Faster AND more stable together points at memory pressure rather than
a dispatch-duration watchdog, but that is a hypothesis and this experiment is
what would support or kill it.

### Design: a 2x2 on the two flags, plus two cells to locate the boundary

Same pack throughout - L3 at 59 distractors, 118,911 prompt tokens, the exact
configuration that died at 96,286. `-c 262144`, `thinking=low`,
`max_tokens 4096`, temperature 0, b10435. Only `-b` and `-ub` change.

| cell | `-b` | `-ub` | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| default | 2048 | 512 | **MEASURED (E48)**: device lost at n_tokens 96,286 |
| arm B | 512 | 128 | **MEASURED (E48b)**: survived, ~132 tok/s prefill |
| **E49-A** | **2048** | **128** | isolates `-ub` (batch left at default) |
| **E49-B** | **512** | **512** | isolates `-b` (ubatch left at default) |
| **E49-C** | 2048 | 256 | locates the boundary between 128 and 512 |
| **E49-D** | 2048 | 64 | tests whether the effect keeps going below 128 |

The four corners of the 2x2 are default, arm B, A and B. Two are already
measured, so this costs four runs rather than six.

### Predictions (registered before any cell runs). The FIELD is named in each.

1. **PRIMARY: `-ub` is the causal variable, `-b` is not.** Cell A's count of
   `outcome == "transport_failed"` is **0** (survives); cell B's is **12**
   (crashes). Reasoning: `n_ubatch` sets the actual compute dispatch submitted
   to the GPU, while `n_batch` is a logical grouping that gets split into
   ubatches, so `n_batch` should not change what any single dispatch does.
2. **Prefill throughput tracks `-ub`, not `-b`.** Read from the serverlog's
   `prompt processing ... tokens per second` at the last progress line:
   **cell A >= 100 tok/s, cell B <= 70 tok/s.**
3. **Cell C (`-ub 256`) survives**, `transport_failed` == 0. Least confident of
   the four; it is the cell that locates the boundary rather than tests the
   hypothesis.
4. **Cell D (`-ub 64`) survives, and its prefill rate is NOT materially higher
   than cell A's.** Registered band: **90-145 tok/s**. If throughput keeps
   climbing as the micro-batch keeps shrinking, the memory-pressure reading is
   incomplete and I would say so.
5. **`correct` is 10-12 in every surviving cell.** Not pinned at 12: F70
   establishes that changed prefill chunking changes floating-point
   accumulation and can flip a token at temperature 0, and every cell here
   changes the chunking.

**Not predicted:** which individual questions change answer between cells.

**Calibration note, stated because the pattern is now consistent.** Registered
bands in this programme have sat BELOW the measured result seven times,
including arm B's prefill rate two hours ago. Bands 2 and 4 above are set wider
on the upside than instinct suggests, and if they are still too low that is
itself the finding.

**Falsifier: if cell A crashes and cell B survives, `-ub` is not the mechanism
and `-b` is** - which reverses prediction 1 and would mean the relevant
quantity is total in-flight logical batch rather than dispatch size. **If BOTH
survive**, either flag alone is sufficient and the mechanism is a property of
the combination, which no single-flag story explains and which I would report
as unresolved rather than pick a winner from.

### E48 arm B RESULT - the wall is a TUNABLE. 12/12 at 118,911 tokens, and prefill got FASTER.

> **The "2.7x" figures in this entry are SUPERSEDED.** They divided rates
> taken at two different depths. See the correction below: the like-for-like
> figure is **2.51x at matched depth**, and the advantage is depth-dependent.

`-b 512 -ub 128`, everything else identical to the rung that died. 1,736.9s
total.

| | default `-b 2048 -ub 512` | **arm B `-b 512 -ub 128`** |
|---|---|---|
| Prompt tokens | 118,911 | **118,911** (identical pack) |
| Fate | device lost at n_tokens 96,286 | **completed** |
| `correct` | - (all 12 `transport_failed`) | **12/12** |
| `citation_valid` | - | **12/12** |
| Prefill rate | 49.57 tok/s | **~132-144 tok/s** |

**PREDICTION 1 HELD - the primary one.** `transport_failed` is **0**. F72's
ceiling is not a property of the hardware; it is a property of the default
micro-batch.

**PREDICTION 2 HELD at the top of its band.** Registered 10-12, measured
**12**, with every citation correct.

**PREDICTION 3 FALSIFIED, and in a direction I did not consider.** I registered
prefill throughput as LOWER than 49.57 tok/s, band 15-45, reasoning that
smaller dispatches cost efficiency. Measured **~132-144 tok/s - about 2.7x
FASTER**. There is no throughput-for-survival trade here. The smaller
micro-batch is both more stable and substantially quicker at this depth, which
means my model of the failure was wrong rather than merely mis-scaled: a pure
dispatch-duration watchdog would not also make the work go faster. Memory
pressure at `-ub 512` is the obvious candidate, and E49 is registered to test
it rather than assert it.

**This is the eighth time a registered band has sat below the result.**

### CORRECTION to the E48 arm B result: the speedup is 2.51x at matched depth, not 2.7x, and it is DEPTH-DEPENDENT

Recorded 2026-08-17 under the owner's standing instruction to cross-check every
number. The "2.7x faster" figure I published this morning is **wrong**, and the
error is a like-for-like failure of exactly the kind
`development-discipline.md` names.

**What I did wrong.** llama-server's `tokens per second` in the prefill progress
line is a **cumulative average, not an instantaneous rate**, so it declines as
depth grows. I took arm B's rate at one depth (~132) and the default run's rate
at a deeper one (49.57) and divided them. Two different depths, one ratio.

**The correct comparison, at matched `n_tokens`**, both runs on the identical
118,911-token pack:

| n_tokens | default tok/s | arm B tok/s | ratio | default sec | arm B sec |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24,606 | 222.54 | 216.62 | **0.97x** | 110.6 | 113.6 |
| 45,086 | 142.64 | 194.03 | 1.36x | 316.1 | 232.4 |
| 65,566 | 92.26 | 174.93 | 1.90x | 710.7 | 374.8 |
| 86,046 | 66.47 | 156.20 | 2.35x | 1,294.4 | 550.9 |
| **94,238** | **59.20** | **148.84** | **2.51x** | **1,591.8** | **633.1** |

94,238 is the deepest point both runs reached, because the default one died
shortly after it.

**So: no advantage at all below about 25,000 tokens, and a monotonically
growing one after that, reaching 2.51x where the comparison runs out.** Every
document quoting 2.7x has been corrected.

**This makes the result MORE interesting, not less, and it is a mechanism
clue.** A fixed per-dispatch overhead would produce a constant ratio. An
advantage that is absent when the KV cache is small and grows steadily as it
fills is what **memory pressure** looks like. That is the hypothesis E49 was
registered to test, and this curve is independent support for it that arrived
before E49 ran. It does not replace E49: this is still one run against one run,
and it cannot separate `-b` from `-ub`.

**The falsification of prediction 3 stands and is unaffected.** I registered
prefill as SLOWER at 15-45 tok/s; it is faster at every depth above ~25K and
never slower than 0.97x. Only the magnitude was wrong.

### E49 RESULT - `-ub` is the whole story, `-b` does nothing, and 128 is an OPTIMUM. All five predictions held.

Same 118,911-token pack in every cell, `-c 262144`, `thinking=low`. Throughput
compared at **matched depth** (n_tokens 94,238, the deepest point all six
configurations reached) using `tools/prefill_curve.py`, not by dividing
last-line rates.

| cell | `-b` | `-ub` | survived | `correct` | tok/s @94,238 | vs default |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| default | 2048 | 512 | **NO** - lost at 96,286 | - | 59.20 | 1.00x |
| **B** | **512** | **512** | **NO** - lost at 109,598 | - | 63.13 | 1.07x |
| C | 2048 | 256 | yes | 12/12 | 105.60 | 1.78x |
| **A** | **2048** | **128** | **yes** | **12/12** | **147.97** | **2.50x** |
| arm B | 512 | 128 | yes | 12/12 | 148.84 | 2.51x |
| D | 2048 | 64 | yes | 12/12 | 127.53 | 2.15x |

**PREDICTION 1 HELD on both halves - this was the whole experiment.** Cell A
(`-ub` lowered, `-b` left alone) has `transport_failed` **0**. Cell B (`-b`
lowered, `-ub` left alone) has `transport_failed` **12** and died with the same
device-lost fingerprint. `-ub` is the causal variable and `-b` is not.

**`-b` does nothing, and the numbers are stark.** Holding `-ub` fixed and
varying `-b` by 4x moves throughput **0.6%** at `ub=128` (147.97 vs 148.84) and
**6.6%** at `ub=512` (59.20 vs 63.13, and both still crash). Holding `-b` fixed
and varying `-ub` moves it **2.50x**. Arm B's two-flag change was therefore a
one-flag change wearing a disguise, which is exactly what E49 existed to
determine.

**PREDICTION 2 HELD.** Registered cell A >= 100 tok/s and cell B <= 70.
Measured **147.97** and **63.13**.

**PREDICTION 3 HELD.** Cell C at `-ub 256` survives, 12/12. So the survival
boundary sits between 256 and 512.

**PREDICTION 4 HELD, and it is the most interesting cell.** Registered that
cell D would survive and would NOT be materially faster than A, band 90-145
tok/s. Measured **127.53 - slower than A, not faster.** Throughput peaks at
`-ub 128` and falls off on BOTH sides:

```
ub 512:  59.20    ub 256: 105.60    ub 128: 147.97    ub 64: 127.53
```

**128 is an optimum, not a "smaller is safer" monotone.** Below it, per-dispatch
overhead starts to dominate; above it, something else does.

**PREDICTION 5 HELD.** Every surviving cell scored 12/12, at the top of the
registered 10-12 band.

**All five predictions held.** That is the first time in this programme, and it
is worth saying against the record that my bands sat below the result eight
times before today. The difference is that these predictions came from a
mechanism (`n_ubatch` sizes the dispatch, `n_batch` only groups) rather than
from calibrating a number.

### What the mechanism now looks like, and what is still not established

An inverted-U in throughput with a cliff on the high side is consistent with a
**working-set** account: prefill activations scale with `n_ubatch`, so a larger
micro-batch means a larger transient allocation on top of an already-large KV
cache. That predicts three things the data shows - the advantage is absent when
the KV cache is small (0.96x at 24,606 tokens), grows monotonically as it fills
(2.50x at 94,238), and turns into a device-lost once the total exceeds what the
driver will service. It also explains why the two `ub=512` deaths landed at
**different** depths, 96,286 and 109,598: a pressure threshold moves with
whatever else is resident, where a fixed limit would not.

**Not established, and I am not going to assert it.** No allocation was
measured. The account above is a hypothesis consistent with five configurations;
confirming it needs VRAM/GTT instrumentation during prefill, which this harness
does not collect. What IS established is the operational fact, and it needed no
mechanism: **serve long context with `-ub 128`.**

**A caution against over-generalising.** Everything here is one model, one pack,
one build, one driver. `-ub 128` is the optimum measured on Qwen3.8-27B-Q4_K_M
at `-c 262144` on b10435 with this Mesa/RADV stack. It is not established as a
general llama.cpp tuning constant, and the corridor-rule discipline in F10 says
architecture-dependent numbers do not transfer.
