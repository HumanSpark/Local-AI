## E38: the L2 tier - can anything in this pack actually stop Qwen3.8? (2026-08-15, pre-registered before any run)

**Why this exists.** E37 ended 20/20. An instrument the subject exhausts
measures the instrument, so the routing question "how capable is this model
at knowledge work" is still open. E38 raises the difficulty on the SAME
capabilities rather than swapping in a different, easier-to-fail task -
otherwise a lower score would be evidence about the new task, not about the
model.

**The design principle, and it is the falsifiable part of the whole tier:
L1 rewarded a HEURISTIC and L2 tests whether that is all the model has.**
Every L1 supersession item was solved correctly by "use the most recent
amendment". Three of the six L2 categories punish exactly that move:

- `date_effective` - the amendment exists but is NOT YET IN FORCE on the
  date asked about, so the older figure is correct. Applying it early is a
  new named outcome, `premature`, and it is the exact inverse of
  `stale_value`.
- `revocation` - Amendment 3 REVOKES a paragraph of Amendment 2 and
  reinstates the original 24-month retention. The newest amendment restores
  an older value, and a partial supersession (three words deleted from
  clause 4.2, the rest surviving) tests wholesale-replacement reasoning.
- `precedence` - a side letter gives the Policy precedence over the
  Agreement, SCOPED to data matters and explicitly not to service levels.
  One question is resolved by applying it; a second must NOT be. Applying it
  out of scope is the new outcome `over_applied`, and it is the more
  expensive of the two precedence errors because it produces a confident
  answer that contradicts the contract.

Plus `multihop` (three questions whose answer needs two or three chained
facts - an effective date, an extended engagement period, and a rate),
`exhaustive` (one negative question requiring all five candidates to be
checked), and `underspecified` (the topic is in the pack, the attribute is
not - a harder decline than L1's, because adjacent text makes a confident
answer feel supported).

**Config.** `tools/run_ps_eval.py --tier l2`; corpus `spikes/ps-eval/
corpus_l2.py` (eight documents, 13,221 chars, ~3.3K tokens); questions and
graders `spikes/ps-eval/questions_l2.py`. Otherwise identical to E37: `-c
16384`, `-np 1`, temperature 0, `max_tokens 4096`, one request per question.

**This is STILL not a long-context test.** The L2 pack is ~3.3K tokens
against L1's ~2.7K. The difficulty is in the reasoning demanded, not in
retrieval load. Length is E35's question and must not be conflated with it.

**The L1 pack is byte-identical and that is asserted mechanically.**
`build_pack()` remains 10,741 chars, sha256 `eb5630b5f7378476`, checked by
`tools/validate_ps_eval_l2.py` on every run. Seven completed cloud arms are
scored against it.

**The graders are validated, not assumed.** Seven synthetic respondents,
each with a required signature: perfect 12/12; a respondent applying
amendments regardless of effective date produces exactly 3 `premature`; one
running the "latest amendment wins" heuristic produces 1 `stale_value` and
1 `wrong`; one never applying the precedence rule produces 1
`precedence_missed`; one applying it out of scope produces 1 `over_applied`;
one answering the underspecified item produces 1 `over_claim`; an
unformatted respondent produces 12 `format_error`. All seven pass at time of
registration, and every named trap is asserted reachable.

**Predictions (registered before any arm runs). The FIELD is named in each,
and the bands are set HIGHER than instinct because E37 falsified four of
five in the same direction - under-predicting this model.**

1. **`correct` /12.** Qwen3.8-Q4_K_M **6-10**; workhorse control **2-6**;
   `qwen/qwen3.8-27b` cloud **7-11**.
2. **`premature` count >= 1 for Qwen3.8.** The date-effective trap is the
   one L1 could not set, and a model that scored 4/4 on L1 supersession has
   demonstrably learned "apply the amendment" - D1 and D2 punish it. If
   `premature` is 0 across all three date_effective items, the model is
   reading effective dates and not pattern-matching, which is the single
   most impressive thing it could do on this tier.
3. **`over_applied` == 1 on P2 for at least one of the two local arms.**
   The side letter's scope limitation is one sentence long and contradicts
   the sentence before it.
4. **Z1 (`underspecified`) is graded `correct` for Qwen3.8.** It scored 4/4
   on L1's unanswerable items, and this prediction says that generalises to
   the harder decline where the topic is present and only the attribute is
   missing.
5. **The gap `correct(Qwen3.8) - correct(workhorse)` is >= 3.** On L1 it was
   6 of 20. If L2 compresses the gap to under 3 the two models are closer on
   hard reasoning than L1 implied, and the workhorse's L1 deficit was
   concentrated in arithmetic rather than judgement.

**Not predicted:** which individual questions fail. The category bands are
the falsifiable part.

**Falsifier for the whole tier, and it is the one that matters most: if
Qwen3.8 scores 11 or 12 of 12, L2 has ALSO saturated** and no claim about
this model's knowledge-work ceiling may be made from either tier. A third
tier would then be required, and the honest report is that the box's
capability exceeds every instrument built for it so far - which is a finding,
but not the one this experiment set out to produce.

**A formatting observation worth carrying to any strict-format eval.**
Qwen3.8's responses here begin `Thinking: We need answer user's question...`
inside the CONTENT field rather than in a separate reasoning field. The
substring assertions in these suites are indifferent to it. A grader that
requires an exact output form would score it wrong on presentation, which is
precisely the `format_error` case the PS eval separates out - and there,
given an explicit two-line template, the same model complied on all 20
questions. The preamble is a default-format habit, not an inability to follow
a format.
