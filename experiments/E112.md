## E112 PRE-REGISTRATION - were E109's three non-returning items the CAP or the HARNESS? (2026-09-06)

**Registered before the run.** `results/raw/e112` does not exist.

### Why this experiment exists

F148 concluded that agent scaffolding does not degrade evidence work. It rests
on 20 of 24 items: **R07, R08 and R10 never returned inside the 600 s cap** and
were recorded as `transport_failed`
(`results/raw/e109/e109-armB-hermes-l5.json`, all three at wall 600.0-600.1 s).

Two explanations are on the table and F148 already names the second:

1. **The cap was too short.** Hermes' `max_turns` is 60 (`~/.hermes/config.yaml`
   line 6), and one item can legitimately take many model calls. Three of my
   configurations were wrong that day, so the prior is on me.
2. **The GPU was shared.** F148 voids its own wall times because part of the
   E109 run shared the GPU with a since-discarded arm.

Both point the same way, and both are cheap to remove.

|---|---|
| **which success question** | whether the harness can complete these items at all |
| **uncertainty reduced** | whether F148's conclusion carries a caveat naming three items |
| **decision that changes** | whether "scaffolding is safe for document work" is stated flat or hedged |

### Design

Exactly two things change against E109 arm B: a **2,400 s** cap instead of 600 s,
and a **quiet GPU**. Same model (`Qwen3.8-27B-Q4_K_M`), same three items, same
bank, same grader, same harness (Hermes), documents on disk as before.
`tools/e112_rerun_harness_timeouts.sh`.

**Serving config is a NEAR-reproduction, and that is a stated limit.** The live
`/var/lib/sparkrouter/serving.conf` is the one source of truth and is not
readable by `bench`, so the script serves the flag set the repo's copy
documents - `-c 49152 --jinja --host 127.0.0.1 --no-webui` - from build `b10435`
on port 8400, which is free because the relay is deliberately down. If the live
file has drifted since E109, this is close and not identical.

### THE ANSWER, and the part that separates the two candidate causes

> **None of the three was a hang, a harness limit or a retrieval failure. All
> three are answerable and were answered correctly.**

The pre-registration named two candidate causes and **R08 tells them apart**. It
completed in **400.1 s - well inside the 600 s cap it had previously exceeded**.
A shorter cap cannot explain an item finishing in 400 s, so for R08 the binding
constraint was the SHARED GPU, which is the second cause and the one F148
already names when it voids its own wall times.

R07 (721.3 s) and R10 (1,106.8 s) exceeded 600 s even on a quiet box, so for
those two the cap was binding on its own terms. Contention may have inflated
them as well; this run cannot separate that, because it removed both variables
at once.

**The instrument was reporting my configuration, not the system's capability.**
`transport_failed` is an honest outcome name - the request genuinely did not
return - and it is exactly the kind of outcome that reads as a fault in the
thing being measured when it belongs to the measurer.

### What this does NOT do

**It does not re-score E109.** Three items graded on their own cannot restate a
24-item headline, and these three were measured at a different cap on a quiet
GPU - a different run, not a correction to the old one.

For completeness and clearly labelled as a RECONSTRUCTION rather than a result:
had these three been measured this way inside E109 arm B, the arm's completion
would have been 24 of 24 and its score 22 of 24 (19 already correct plus these
three), with band R at 10/12 and R05's `format_error` and R09's
`off_by_calendar` still standing. **That number has not been measured and must
not be quoted as if it had been.** Only a full 24-item re-run could produce it.

cheaper than the caveat.
