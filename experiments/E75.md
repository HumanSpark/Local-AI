## E75 PRE-REGISTRATION - selective review: can the system beat its best model? (2026-08-20)

**Registered BEFORE any run.** Follows directly from E74's coverage result and
is registered now, while the coding arms hold the GPU, so it cannot be shaped by
seeing its own numbers.

### Why this experiment exists

| | |
|---|---|
| **which success question** | *What percentage of my serious knowledge work can I complete locally, within roughly 30-40 seconds...?* - E74 answered 83.3% for the best single model. This asks whether a SYSTEM does better |
| **uncertainty reduced** | whether the ensemble's 100% answer coverage is reachable without ground truth, or whether selecting the right answer needs the capability that produced it |
| **decision that changes** | whether the production route stays one model per tier, or becomes draft-then-review. That is a serving-architecture decision with a latency cost attached |

### The finding it comes from

E74, 24 items, three local models:

| | |
|---|---|
| all three correct | 14/24 |
| missed by at least one | 10/24 |
| **missed by all three** | **0/24** |
| **union - answer coverage** | **24/24** |

**Stated without overclaiming:** the local ensemble has 100% answer COVERAGE on
this bank. The remaining problem is selecting or validating the right answer
**without access to the ground truth**. A perfect router is an ORACLE, and
knowing which of three conflicting answers is correct may itself require much of
the intelligence that produced it. This experiment tests whether a cheap signal -
**disagreement** - recovers any of that headroom.

### The arms

Deliberately NOT a trained or hand-written task classifier. The routing signal
is disagreement between two independently produced answers.

| arm | configuration |
|---|---|
| **A** | Qwen3-30B-A3B alone - the baseline the system must beat (20/24 inside 30s) |
| **B** | Qwen3-30B-A3B and gpt-oss-120b answer independently; agree -> accept, materially disagree -> escalate |
| **C** | Qwen3-30B-A3B drafts, gpt-oss-120b verifies against the sources: accept / correct / abstain |
| **D** | Qwen3-30B-A3B, with Qwen3.8-27B reviewing ONLY on disagreement |

**Why these three models and not others.** Each is here for a measured property,
per the rule that no model joins without a hypothesis about the weakness it
fixes: Qwen3-30B-A3B is the only arm that meets the latency target (20/24 inside
30s); gpt-oss-120b has a **materially different error pattern** and zero critical
errors on the unanswerable items; Qwen3.8-27B has the same 21/24 accuracy with a
tail too long for routine use, so it is a last resort rather than a default.

### Metrics, per arm

`final_correct`, **`unsupported_assertion_rate`**, `escalated_pct`,
`total_ttca` (p50 and p95), **`false_corrections`**, and specifically
**`reviewer_broke_a_correct_answer`** - the count of cells where the reviewer
turned a correct draft into a wrong one.

**That last metric decides whether this ships.** A review layer that gains two
answers and breaks two has done nothing except add latency.

### THE DELIBERATELY-WRONG-DRAFT CONDITION IS NOT OPTIONAL

Every reviewing arm is run twice: once on real drafts, once on drafts seeded
with confident errors. **A reviewer that simply agrees with fluent prose scores
beautifully against correct drafts and is worthless.** Without this condition,
anchoring and reviewing are indistinguishable - which is the same trap H2's
condition 5 was written for.

### Predictions, each naming the FIELD it is scored against

| # | prediction | scored against |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | at least one reviewing arm beats arm A on `final_correct` | `final_correct`, predicted >= 22/24 for the best of B/C/D against A's 21/24 |
| 2 | no reviewing arm reaches the 24/24 oracle coverage | `final_correct`, predicted <= 23/24 |
| 3 | disagreement is a usable signal - escalation is neither near-zero nor near-total | `escalated_pct`, predicted between 15% and 60% |
| 4 | at least one reviewer breaks at least one correct answer | `reviewer_broke_a_correct_answer`, predicted >= 1 across arms |
| 5 | on seeded-wrong drafts, the reviewer catches fewer than it does on real drafts | `final_correct` on the seeded condition, below the real-draft condition |

**Prediction 2 is the honest one.** If a reviewing arm DID reach 24/24 that
would be a much larger result than expected and should be treated as a suspected
instrument fault first - per the standing rule this session earned twice.

### Registered decision rule

| observed | action |
|---|---|
| best reviewing arm >= 23/24 with `reviewer_broke_a_correct_answer` = 0 | selective review is the production architecture for the Think tier |
| gains offset by an equal number of breakages | **close the branch** - the layer adds latency and nothing else |
| `escalated_pct` > 60% | disagreement is not a signal here, it is noise; a different trigger is needed before this is retried |
| the seeded-wrong condition shows the reviewer agreeing with bad drafts | **anchoring, not reviewing.** Do not ship it whatever the headline says |

**Evidence to be written:** `results/raw/e75-<arm>-<condition>.json`.


## E75 RESULT - selective review does NOT beat the best single model. PREDICTION 1 FALSIFIED. (2026-08-20)

24 items, packs `s`+`m`. Drafts reused unchanged from E74 so the review layer is
the only variable. Reviewer gpt-oss-120b (`-c 65536`, arms B and C) and
Qwen3.8-27B (`reasoning_effort: medium`, `max_tokens` 2048, arm D). No routing
decision consulted the answer key.

| arm | correct | @30s | @40s | esc% | broke | fixed | net | critical | wall p50 | wall p95 | total wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** baseline Qwen3-30B-A3B | **21/24** | **20** | **20** | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 | **3.8s** | 28.5s | **233s** |
| **B** independent, agree-or-escalate | 21/24 | 15 | 17 | 29.2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 8.6s | 59.7s | 854s |
| **C** draft -> gpt-oss verifies | 20/24 | 13 | 19 | 29.2 | **1** | 0 | **-1** | 1 | 23.4s | 111.3s | 860s |
| **C seeded** same, corrupted drafts | 20/24 | 13 | 18 | **75.0** | 2 | **1** | -1 | 2 | 23.0s | 42.9s | 702s |
| **D** Qwen3.8 reviews on disagreement only | 21/24 | 15 | 17 | 29.2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0** | 8.6s | 210.4s | 1,051s |

### THE ANSWER

> **No. On this bank a bounded local multi-model system does not produce more
> correct completed answers than its best constituent model.** Every
> architecture tied the baseline at 21/24 or fell to 20/24. Not one reached 22.

Against the programme's primary target - *beat every constituent model on
correctness, keep >= 80% of tasks within 40 seconds, and add no critical errors*:

| | A | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| beats the best single model | **no** | no | no | no |
| >= 80% within 40s | **83%** | 71% | 79% | 71% |
| no increase in critical errors | - | ok | ok | **improved** |

**Only the baseline meets the latency criterion, and nothing meets the
correctness one.** Review costs **3.7x to 4.5x the total wall time** (233s ->
854-1,051s) and buys no additional correct answers.

### Scoring

| # | prediction | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a reviewing arm beats A, >= 22/24 | **FALSIFIED** - best is a tie at 21 |
| 2 | no reviewing arm reaches the 24/24 oracle | **HELD** |
| 3 | escalation between 15% and 60% | **HELD** - 29.2% |
| 4 | at least one reviewer breaks a correct answer | **HELD** - arm C broke `K06` |
| 5 | seeded drafts score below real drafts | **NOT HELD** - both 20/24 |

### The two findings worth more than the headline

**1. The reviewer genuinely reviews - it does not anchor.** Given corrupted
drafts its escalation rate went **29.2% -> 75.0%** and its `correct` actions went
**3 -> 10**. It detected the seeded errors and repaired them well enough to land
on the *same* 20/24 as the uncorrupted condition. Prediction 5 expected the
seeded condition to score worse; it did not.

> **The reviewer is competent at REPAIR and harmful at INITIATIVE.** Handed a
> broken draft it fixes it. Handed a correct draft, its interventions break more
> than they fix - arm C's single change to a correct answer was the only verdict
> that moved, and it moved the wrong way.

That is the cleanest possible statement of why this architecture fails here:
**the drafts are already good, so there is little to repair and plenty to
break.**

**2. Arm D removed the one critical error, at no correctness cost.** The
baseline's single critical error is `K05` - the unanswerable item, where
Qwen3-30B-A3B invented a hang rate. Qwen3.8-27B's review turned a *confidently
wrong* answer into a *hedged wrong* one. The verdict did not change; the
**fabrication** did.

> **Review bought SAFETY, not accuracy.** If the objective is "never assert
> something the sources do not support", arm D is the only architecture that
> improved on the baseline - at 4.5x the wall time.

### Registered decision rule fires: CLOSE THE BRANCH for correctness

The rule registered before the run: *"gains offset by an equal number of
breakages - close the branch; the layer adds latency and nothing else."* The
observed case is stronger than that - there were **no gains at all** on
correctness in any arm.

**Kept open, narrowly:** arm D as an *abstention* mechanism rather than an
accuracy mechanism, tested against the answerability suite rather than this
bank. That is a different hypothesis with a different metric and it needs its
own registration.

### Why the 24/24 oracle was never reachable, stated plainly

E74's coverage result said every question had at least one correct local answer.
This experiment tried the cheapest available selector - disagreement - and it
**recovered none of that headroom**. The agreement check escalated 7 of 24 cells
and changing the answer on those 7 altered **zero** verdicts in arms B and D.

That is the concrete form of the caution registered with the coverage figure:
**a perfect router is an oracle, and knowing which of two conflicting answers is
right is not cheaper than answering the question.** The coverage number remains
an upper bound and is now measured to be an unreachable one by this route.

### Instrument notes, both declared

- **The agreement check initially had a 16-token ceiling** and returned an empty
  string on every cell, because gpt-oss-120b reasons before answering. Caught by
  a 3-item smoke test before the full run; raised to 512.
- **Qwen3.8-27B returned EMPTY reviews for 115s and 57s** until
  `reasoning_effort` was declared. Its chat template resolves to `xhigh` when
  nothing is said, which F71 recorded burning 100,000 tokens over 2h46m and
  returning nothing. Set to `medium` per F71's own finding, declared in the
  metadata, and the arm re-run from scratch.
- **Protocol adherence is low and is reported rather than hidden**: gpt-oss-120b
  followed the ACCEPT/CORRECT/ABSTAIN protocol on 83.3% of cells,
  Qwen3.8-27B on **29.2%** - it mostly just rewrote the answer. A rewrite is
  still a decision and is scored as one.
