## E42: the speed question - how much of Qwen3.8's cost is reasoning, and what does removing it cost? (2026-08-15, pre-registered before any run)

**Why this exists.** The owner's question, verbatim: *"I really need to
understand the speed difference - and if we can get more speed from 3.8 -
surely it shouldn't be orders of magnitude slower"*. It is not orders of
magnitude slower per token. The E38 wall-clock gap decomposes as:

| factor | Qwen3.8-27B-Q4_K_M | Qwen3-30B-A3B-2507 | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| completion tokens, 12 questions | **6,832** | 270 | **25.3x more** |
| generation rate | 11.15 tok/s | 28.51 tok/s | 2.6x slower |
| wall | 617.1s | 13.6s | 45.4x |

The answers themselves are about 20 tokens each - roughly 240 tokens of actual
output across the whole run. **Approximately 96% of what Qwen3.8 generated was
reasoning, not answer.** Volume is the dominant term, not speed, and volume is
the term that might be controllable.

**A DEFAULT WAS DOING THE DECIDING, AND NOBODY CHOSE IT.** Reading the GGUF's
chat template shows Qwen3.8 resolves `reasoning_effort` to **`xhigh`** when the
caller says nothing:

```
{%- if enable_thinking is undefined or enable_thinking is true %}
{%- set resolved_reasoning_effort = reasoning_effort|default('xhigh') %}
```

**Every Qwen3.8 figure in this repo - E32, E33, E37, E38, and the E41
registration - was measured at the model's MAXIMUM reasoning setting**, by
default rather than by choice. That is a serving-config variable the results
do not record, which is Rule 8's exact failure: a benchmark measures a CONFIG,
not a box.

The levels are system-prompt instructions, not architecture:
- **xhigh** (default): "think carefully through the task, validate key
  assumptions, consider plausible alternatives, and prioritize correctness"
- **medium**: no reasoning instruction is added at all - the neutral case
- **low**: "keep your thinking brief and focused, moving directly to the
  conclusion without unnecessary elaboration"
- **off** (`enable_thinking: false`): no thinking block

**Config.** `tools/run_ps_eval.py --tier l2 --thinking {off,low,medium,xhigh}`.
Identical to E38 in every other respect: `-c 16384`, `-np 1`, temperature 0,
`max_tokens 4096`, same 12 questions, same pack. llama-server accepts
`reasoning_effort` and `chat_template_kwargs` PER REQUEST, so all four arms
run against one loaded model with no reload between them - which also removes
model-load variance from the timing comparison.

**The baseline is already measured**: E38 local at `default` = xhigh scored
**12/12 in 617.1s using 6,832 completion tokens**. The `xhigh` arm re-runs it
explicitly to confirm that `default` and `xhigh` are the same thing.

**Predictions (registered before any arm runs). The FIELD is named in each.
Bands are set HIGHER than instinct, deliberately: my registered bands have sat
below the measured result in three consecutive experiments (E37 four of five,
E38 two of two resolvable), which is a bias in the experimenter and not a run
of bad luck.**

1. **`correct` /12.** off **4-10**; low **8-12**; medium **9-12**; xhigh
   **12** (confirming the E38 baseline).
2. **`completion_tokens` at off is under 15% of xhigh's 6,832** (under 1,025).
3. **`wall_s` at off is under 60s**, against 617.1s - better than a 10x
   speedup.
4. **The actionable one: at least one setting scores >= 10/12 in under 25% of
   xhigh's wall time** (under 154s). If true there is a usable middle setting
   and the routing guide should name it. If false, the choice really is
   "accurate and slow" or "fast and wrong", and the guide should say that
   instead.
5. **`completion_tokens` ordering is off < low < medium < xhigh.** medium adds
   no instruction, so it should sit between the explicit "be brief" and the
   explicit "be thorough".

**Falsifier, in two directions.**
- **If `off` scores 11 or 12 of 12**, the reasoning is nearly free to remove
  on this task and xhigh is close to pure waste for knowledge work. That would
  make every wall-clock figure previously reported for this model an artefact
  of an unchosen default, and the routing guide would have to lead with it.
- **If `off` scores 3 or below**, the thinking is load-bearing, the cost is
  not recoverable by configuration, and the 45x gap is the real price of the
  +7 accuracy.

### E42 AMENDMENT 1 (declared 2026-08-15 10:34, BEFORE any E42 arm runs)

**Scope widened from one task type to three, on the owner's instruction:**
*"we're gonna have to then consider varying the thinking level for each task
... benchmark it again at all of the thinking levels for different tasks just
to get us an idea of how this works."*

The original registration swept four reasoning levels over the L2 knowledge
pack only. That answers "how much does thinking cost" but not the question
the routing guide actually needs, which is **whether the right level differs
by task**. A retrieval question and a hard coding problem have no reason to
want the same reasoning budget, and a single recommended setting would be
wrong for one of them.

**The matrix.** Four reasoning levels x three task types, all on
Qwen3.8-27B-Q4_K_M:

| task type | instrument | items | what it demands |
|---|---|---|---|
| knowledge | `--tier l2` | 12 | multi-hop retrieval, dates, precedence |
| writing | `run_writing_eval.py` | 7 | long-form generation under constraint |
| coding | `--task-set expert` | 8 | exact algorithms, contracts, complexity gates |

Levels: `off`, `low`, `medium`, `xhigh`. The `--thinking` flag is wired into
all three runners and recorded in every result JSON.

**The expert coding set is used rather than the core 15.** Core saturates - a
cheap cloud model scored 14/15 on it - so it cannot show a reasoning-level
difference. The expert set is also smaller, which matters because at xhigh a
15-task coding run took 4,283s and this is a 12-cell matrix.

**Predictions 1-5 stand unchanged for the knowledge row.** Three more are
registered for the matrix as a whole.

6. **The optimal level DIFFERS by task type.** Recorded field: the level with
   the highest score per task type, ties broken by lower `wall_s`. Registered:
   coding wants a strictly higher level than knowledge. If one level wins
   everywhere, the routing guide gets one setting and this amendment was
   unnecessary - which is a legitimate outcome and would simplify the advice.
7. **Writing degrades LEAST from turning thinking off.** Registered: writing
   at `off` retains at least 80% of its `xhigh` score, while knowledge and
   coding each lose more. The constraints in the writing eval - word budgets,
   banned words, keeping the unfavourable findings - are compliance rather
   than derivation, and compliance should not need a reasoning chain.
8. **Coding at `off` loses more than 40% of its `xhigh` pass count.** The
   expert tasks turn on exactness that has to be worked out - tie-breaks,
   atomicity, complexity gates - and that is the case where a chain should
   genuinely pay.

**Cost control.** 12 cells, and the coding row is the expensive one. If the
coding row at `xhigh` exceeds 3 hours for a single cell the remaining coding
cells are dropped to the two endpoints (`off` and `xhigh`) and that reduction
is recorded here rather than quietly performed - a silently truncated matrix
reads as full coverage when it is not.

### E42 AMENDMENT 2 (declared 2026-08-15 10:40, BEFORE any E42 arm runs)

**The owner's hypothesis, registered as his and before any arm runs.** Asked
what the quality difference is across thinking levels on a task of constant
difficulty, and what the quickest level is that still gives high-quality
results, he stated: *"Hypothesis is that it will be medium."*

Recorded here rather than paraphrased into my own predictions, because whose
prediction it was is part of the record. It is falsifiable in both directions
and the decision rule below settles it without further judgement.

**Two operating modes, which is the frame the results must answer in.** Also
from the owner: whether the box *"can genuinely work on the toughest of
knowledge work (with tasks in that maybe batch running where it's very slow)
and then on quicker tasks with almost real time chat"*. Those are different
questions with different acceptance bars, so E42 reports both:

- **BATCH** - the highest score any level reaches, latency disregarded. This
  answers "can it do the hard thing at all".
- **INTERACTIVE** - the best score achievable with a median per-item latency
  under 30s, and separately under 10s. This answers "can a person sit in
  front of it".

**The decision rule, declared before the data exists.** For each task type,
the RECOMMENDED level is the one with the lowest median per-item latency whose
score is **within 1 item** of the best score achieved by any level on that
task type. Ties go to the lower level.

The 1-item tolerance is a designed band declared up front, not a tolerance
invented after seeing a gap. Its justification: local runs at temperature 0
are deterministic (F59), so a 1-item difference is a real difference and not
noise - the tolerance exists to express that a single item is not worth a
multiple of the wall time, which is a routing judgement, not a measurement
claim. A drop of 2 or more items is treated as a genuine quality loss and
disqualifies the level.

**Latency is now measured per item, not per run.** All three runners record
`median_s`, `p95_s`, `min_s`, `max_s`, and counts under 10s and under 30s. A
total run wall says nothing about whether a person can sit in front of the
thing; the p95 is what decides that, because the slow tail is what a user
remembers.

**Prediction 9, and it is the owner's:** on the knowledge task the
recommended level under the rule above is **`medium`**.

**Prediction 10, mine, and it disagrees on one row:** `medium` wins the
knowledge row, but coding's recommended level is `xhigh` - the expert tasks
turn on exactness that has to be worked out rather than complied with. If
`medium` wins all three rows the owner's hypothesis is confirmed in its
strong form and the routing guide gets one setting instead of three.

**A budget defect found and fixed before the matrix ran, recorded because it
would have produced a fake result.** The writing eval's `max_tokens` default
was 4,096. At the reasoning level Qwen3.8 defaults to, the FIRST writing task
consumed the entire budget on reasoning in 339s and emitted no draft at all -
scored `truncated`. Every writing cell at `xhigh` and `medium` would have been
a truncation, and the matrix would have reported that thinking destroys
writing quality when what it destroyed was the token budget. The default is
now 16,384. The affected run was killed rather than completed.

### E42 RESULT - knowledge row (2026-08-15)

Qwen3.8-27B-Q4_K_M, L2 tier, 12 questions, `-c 16384`, `-np 1`, temperature 0,
`max_tokens 8192`. All four levels swept against ONE loaded model, so no
model-load variance is in the timing.

| level | correct | citations | completion tokens | wall | median/item | p95 | items <30s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| off | 8/12 | 9/12 | **398** | 88.5s | **5.88s** | 16.31s | **12** |
| low | **12/12** | **12/12** | 4,949 | **462.6s** | **41.53s** | **54.80s** | 3 |
| medium | 12/12 | 12/12 | 6,052 | 552.2s | 42.44s | 81.08s | 1 |
| xhigh | 12/12 | 11/12 | 6,832 | 617.3s | 47.51s | 82.65s | 2 |

**Prediction 1 HELD on all four bands.** off 8 (registered 4-10), low 12
(8-12), medium 12 (9-12), xhigh 12 (12). The xhigh cell also reproduces E38's
617.1s at 617.3s, confirming that `default` and `xhigh` are the same setting -
which was the harness check that prediction existed for.

**Prediction 2 HELD.** `off` used 398 completion tokens, **5.8%** of xhigh's
6,832 - registered as under 15%.

**Prediction 3 FALSIFIED.** `off` wall was 88.5s against a registered ceiling
of 60s. The miss is mostly the first question's cold prompt (16.31s against a
5.88s median); per-item it is comfortably inside what the prediction was
reaching for, but the field named was `wall_s` and `wall_s` was 88.5.

**Prediction 4 FALSIFIED, and this is the one that matters.** It registered
that at least one setting would reach >= 10/12 in under 25% of xhigh's wall
(under 154s). **None did.** The registration said what that would mean:
*"the choice really is 'accurate and slow' or 'fast and wrong', and the guide
should say that instead"*. So the routing guide must say that. There is no
middle setting on this task: 8/12 at a 5.88s median, or 12/12 at 41.53s, with
nothing in between.

**Prediction 5 HELD.** Token ordering is off < low < medium < xhigh
(398 < 4,949 < 6,052 < 6,832), exactly as registered.

**Prediction 9 - the OWNER'S hypothesis - FALSIFIED, narrowly and
informatively.** He predicted `medium` would be the quickest level giving high
quality. `medium` does give full quality; it is simply not the cheapest point
on the plateau. `low` matches it on every quality field (12/12 correct, 12/12
citations, identical category split) while using **18% fewer tokens** (4,949
against 6,052), **89.6s less wall time** (462.6s against 552.2s) and a **p95
26.3s lower** (54.80s against 81.08s). The recommended
level under the rule registered in AMENDMENT 2 is **`low`**.

**Prediction 10 - mine - FALSIFIED the same way.** I also picked `medium` for
this row.

**The quality plateau starts at `low`, and more reasoning is not monotonically
better.** xhigh spent 38% more tokens than low to reach the same score and
produced a WORSE citation (11/12 against 12/12). Maximum reasoning bought
nothing here and cost a citation.

**The two operating modes, answered for knowledge work.**

- **BATCH: `low`.** 12/12, 462.6s for twelve questions. `xhigh` is the
  template default, not a better setting, and should be turned off.
- **INTERACTIVE: `off`, with a named limitation.** 5.88s median, 12 of 12
  items under 30s and 11 under 10s. What it loses is exactly two things -
  `precedence` 2/2 -> 0/2 and `multihop` 3/3 -> 1/3. It KEEPS retrieval,
  effective-date reasoning, revocation chains, and the refusal to fabricate
  (`over_claim` 0 at every level). So interactive use is sound for lookup,
  extraction and summarising, and is unsafe for anything requiring figures to
  be combined across documents or a precedence rule to be applied.

**A caveat on the margin, stated rather than buried.** The median gap between
`low` and `medium` is 2% (41.53 vs 42.44s), which is inside timing noise even
though the outputs are deterministic at temperature 0 (F59). The
recommendation rests on the p95 (54.80 vs 81.08s), the token count and the
total wall, which are larger and more robust than the median.
