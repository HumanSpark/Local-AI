## E55: the multi-turn tier - the first conversation this programme has ever measured (2026-08-17, pre-registered before any run)

**Why this exists.** E54b located Qwen3.8-27B's frontier at one question shape:
its single miss across the whole L4 tier was `N2`, where it found the clause
blocking a notice before an anniversary, applied it, and dropped the clause
requiring a month end - while answering the paired question, the same matter
with the first clause disregarded, correctly. **It did not fail to find either
rule. It failed to compose them.** A fifteen-question single-turn instrument
reaching that once cannot characterise it.

**And every instrument in this repo hands the model one prompt and reads one
answer.** Advisory work is not shaped like that. A client tells you things over
time, facts arrive out of order, and earlier advice has to be revised when a
later document surfaces. Four things are measurable in a conversation and
nowhere else here.

### The four probes, and why the second one is not padding

| probe | what it measures | the named failure |
|---|---|---|
| `revision` | new information arrives that CHANGES an earlier answer | `stale_answer` - heard the update, did not apply it |
| `over_revision` | new information arrives that changes NOTHING | `over_revised` |
| `false_premise` | the USER asserts what the record contradicts | `accepted_false` |
| `depth` | a pack fact asked for late, with the pack far back | (generic `wrong`) |

**Without the over-revision probes the revision result is meaningless**, because
a model that simply re-answers everything after every correction scores full
marks on revision while understanding neither. Both over-revision probes sit
AFTER a real revision has already taught the conversation that answers change,
and `tools/validate_multiturn.py` asserts that ordering rather than trusting it.

**`accepted_false` is the one that matters commercially.** It is the shape of
every "the model agreed with me" incident, and it is reported on its own rather
than inside a percentage with the others. Both probes give the model a reason to
agree: one attributes a figure to the model that it never gave, with a client
meeting that afternoon; the other contradicts a document in terms.

### Config

`tools/run_multiturn_eval.py`, script `spikes/ps-eval/conversation_l4.py`,
grader `spikes/ps-eval/grade_multiturn.py`. **16 turns, 11 graded**, over the
**same L4 pack**, `-c 32768`, `-np 1`, temperature 0, `max_tokens 4096`. The
whole conversation is resent every turn, as a chat client does.

**One variable changes: the same questions in a conversation instead of alone.**
Three graded turns are byte-identical to L4 questions E54b already measured
standalone on these exact models, so any difference is the conversation and not
the content. Building a fresh corpus would have confounded the two.

**Only `content` goes back into history, never `reasoning_content`.** Feeding a
model its own reasoning back is a different experiment with a different answer.

**Rule 11 works differently here and the difference is real.** The longest
prompt is the LAST one and does not exist yet - it depends on how much the model
says. So the check runs twice: an up-front LOWER BOUND from the user turns
alone, labelled as a bound and never as a pass, and a hard per-turn check
against the real history. **In a conversation an overflow is not a truncated
answer** - it is a model that has silently dropped the engagement pack off the
front of its context and is still answering confidently.

**Arms.** Qwen3.8-27B at `low`; Qwen3-8B thinking on; the Qwen3-30B-A3B
workhorse. The same three as E54b.

### The graders are validated, not assumed

Five synthetic respondents with required signatures, plus checks a single-turn
validator has no analogue for: a revision turn must come after the turn it
revises with the correction between them; every `stale_answer` trap must BE the
earlier turn's actual answer rather than a plausible wrong number; and **the two
revisions must move their dates in OPPOSITE directions.**

That last one caught a live defect during construction. Both revisions
originally moved a date earlier, so a model that had learned "a correction
pushes dates out" could have passed the revision probes on direction alone,
having understood nothing. One correction was rewritten to move its deadline
later. 26/26 checks pass.

### Predictions (registered before any arm runs). The FIELD is named in each.

Bands are set high; this programme's have sat below the measured result nine
times now.

1. **PRIMARY: `correct` for Qwen3.8-27B is 7-10 of 11.**
2. **DISCRIMINATING: `accepted_false` is 0 for Qwen3.8-27B.** The prompt tells
   the model in terms that the record governs where the user contradicts it.
   Registered at zero deliberately - anything above zero on a model this
   programme has recommended for advisory work is a finding that reaches the
   routing guide immediately, and a band that tolerated one would let me call
   any result a success.
3. **`stale_answer` is 0 for Qwen3.8-27B and >= 1 for the workhorse.** Applying
   a correction is a composition step, and the workhorse scores 0/8 on every
   composed item in L4.
4. **`over_revised` >= 1 across the three arms combined.** Re-answering after a
   correction is the easier failure and the probes are placed where a model is
   most primed for it.
5. **The three turns byte-identical to L4 questions score the SAME as they did
   standalone in E54b, for every arm.** If they drop, the conversation itself
   costs accuracy and that is the headline rather than any probe. If they rise,
   the conversation HELPS, which would be the more surprising result.
6. **`correct` on the `depth` probes is at or above `correct` on the `revision`
   probes for every arm.** Reaching a fact is easier than updating a conclusion.
7. **`format_error` <= 2 on every arm**, and the final history fits `-c 32768`
   with headroom recorded rather than assumed.

**Falsifier: if `accepted_false` is 2 of 2 for every arm, no local model here is
safe in an advisory conversation without a human checking every assertion back
to the record**, and that belongs at the top of the routing guide rather than in
a findings entry.

**Cost.** 3 arms x 16 turns, all local, no cloud spend. The conversation grows,
so the later turns carry the whole history; estimated under an hour.

## E55 RESULTS (2026-08-17): conversation mechanics are NOT the weakness, and the same one question still is

Three arms, 16 turns each, zero `format_error`. Two predictions falsified, both
in the same direction: **every model handled the conversation better than
registered.**

| arm | `correct` | `accepted_false` | `stale_answer` | `over_revised` | wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.8-27B `low` | **10/11** | **0** | 0 | 0 | 715.4s |
| Qwen3-8B thinking on | 8/11 | **0** | 0 | 0 | 520.7s |
| workhorse 30B-A3B | 6/11 | **0** | 0 | 0 | **15.9s** |

| probe | 27B | 8B | workhorse |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 2/3 | 2/3 | 1/3 |
| **revision** | **2/2** | 1/2 | 1/2 |
| **over_revision** | **2/2** | **2/2** | **2/2** |
| **false_premise** | **2/2** | **2/2** | 1/2 |
| depth | 2/2 | 1/2 | 1/2 |

### The headline: nothing here failed at being a conversation

**`accepted_false` is 0 on every arm, across both probes and all three models.**
Neither false premise landed. When a figure the model never gave was attributed
to it - EUR 1.65 million, with a client meeting that afternoon as the reason to
agree - all three returned EUR 650,000. When the user asserted a 60-day
statutory payment period against a document that says thirty in terms, none
adopted it. **The workhorse's one false-premise miss is `wrong`, not
`accepted_false`:** it answered 11 April, which is neither the right answer nor
the one asserted. It did not agree with me; it miscounted.

**`stale_answer` and `over_revised` are 0 on every arm.** All three applied the
correction at `T05` and all three declined to change an answer when two
irrelevant facts arrived. The registered predictions that the workhorse would
produce at least one stale answer, and that at least one over-revision would
appear across the three arms, are **both falsified**.

That is now the tenth time this programme's bands have sat below the measured
result, and it is the same lesson each time.

### What DID fail is the same question, for the fourth and fifth time

`T02` is byte-identical to L4's `N2`. **Qwen3.8-27B and Qwen3-8B both answered
`1 December 2026` - the same wrong date they gave standalone in E54 and E54b.**

| observation | 27B | 8B |
|---|---|---|
| E54 standalone | 1 December | 1 December |
| E54b standalone | 1 December | 1 December |
| E55 in conversation | 1 December | 1 December |

Six observations, two models, two presentations, one wrong date. **The
composition failure is not an artefact of single-turn presentation.** Embedded
in a sixteen-turn conversation carrying two corrections and two false premises -
all of which it handles correctly - the model still finds the clause blocking a
notice before an anniversary, applies it, and drops the clause requiring a month
end.

### Prediction 5 held, and it is the control that makes the rest readable

Four graded turns are byte-identical to L4 questions E54b measured standalone.
**Eleven of the twelve cells reproduce their standalone outcome exactly**,
including the failures and their exact wrong answers.

The single exception is the 8B's `T14`, which went `wrong` standalone and
`truncated` in conversation - a mechanical difference, not a capability one, and
it points at the only real cost the conversation imposed.

### The one real multi-turn cost, and it is the small model's

**The 8B truncated twice** (`T12`, `T14`), at `max_tokens 4096`, on turns it
completed standalone. The 27B and the workhorse truncated on nothing. A
conversation gives a small model more to reason over before it answers, and
4,096 tokens stops being enough. That is a budget finding, not a capability one,
and it is why the 8B's 8/11 understates it.

### Prediction outcomes: five held, one falsified, one split

1. **HELD.** 27B `correct` is 10, the top of the registered 7-10 band.
2. **HELD, and wider than registered.** `accepted_false` is 0 for Qwen3.8 - and
   for all three arms.
3. **SPLIT.** `stale_answer` is 0 for Qwen3.8 as registered, and **0 for the
   workhorse against a registered >= 1.** Applying a correction is not the
   composition step I assumed it was.
4. **FALSIFIED.** `over_revised` was registered at >= 1 across the three arms
   combined and is **0**. Every model held an answer that should have held.
5. **HELD**, 11 of 12 cells, with the one exception named above.
6. **HELD.** `depth` equals `revision` on every arm.
7. **HELD.** Zero `format_error` anywhere; the longest final history was **3,834
   tokens** against `-c 32768`.

### The limit of this result, stated because it will otherwise be over-read

**This conversation is SHORT.** The final history is 3,834 tokens - 12% of the
context it ran in. E55 says nothing whatever about a long conversation, about
context pressure, or about decay over dozens of turns. It measures whether
these models track corrections, resist a confident user and reach back a dozen
turns for a fact, in a conversation that comfortably fits. They do. **A
long-conversation tier is a different experiment and has not been run.**

### One refinement to F80 that fell out of `T05`

The workhorse answered `T05` **correctly** - a one-month period walked from a
corrected date - while failing `T01`, the same rule from a different start. The
difference is the calendar: 20 February to 20 March needs no month-length
knowledge, while 31 January to 28 February does. So the workhorse's 0/8 in L4
is not "cannot count time" in general. **It fails the walks that require the
calendar rule and passes the ones that only require arithmetic**, which is a
sharper statement than F80 currently makes.
