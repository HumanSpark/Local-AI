## E58: can Qwen3.8 serve a TINY query quickly enough to be resident? (2026-08-19, pre-registered before any run)

**The question, sharpened by the owner:** not "which model is better" but *"can
3.8 serve tiny queries relatively quickly?"* If it can, one model serves
everything and the relay needs no routing. If it cannot, the workhorse stays
resident and document work is routed to the 27B by name.

**The traffic is now measured, not assumed.** `tools/relay_traffic_mix.py` over
the relay's own log - **952 requests, 2026-07-19 to 2026-08-14, 15 active days,
100% served by the workhorse**:

| prompt tokens | n | share of COUNT | median ms | share of TIME |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| under 1,000 | 869 | **91.3%** | 855 | 27.1% |
| 1,000-4,000 | 33 | 3.5% | 4,204 | 5.1% |
| 4,000-16,000 | 37 | 3.9% | 24,890 | 33.6% |
| 16,000-64,000 | 13 | 1.4% | 72,357 | 34.3% |

Median prompt **68 tokens**, p90 759, max 60,012. The 50 requests at or above
4,000 tokens are **5.3% of count and 67.9% of generation time**.

`tools/latency_matrix.py`, four arms (workhorse; 27B at `off`; 27B at `low`;
8B), three tiers drawn from that distribution rather than from taste, reps
weighted to the tier that decides it (chat 7, document 3, folder 2). Time to a
COMPLETE answer, not time for N tokens - capping generation would flatter a
model whose entire cost is that it thinks. Cold and warm reported separately so
the 45:1 prefill effect (F74) is not averaged into a single misleading number.
One serving config across all arms, `-b 512 -ub 128`, which E49 measured as the
27B's optimum and inert for `-b` - so it biases TOWARD the 27B and understates
the workhorse.

### Predictions (registered before any arm runs)

1. **PRIMARY: the 27B at `off` answers the chat tier in under 5s warm-median.**
   Under 5s is my threshold for "relatively quickly" in the owner's sense - a
   pause, not a wait.
2. **The workhorse's chat warm-median is within 2x of the relay's production
   855ms.** This is the calibration check: if the bench cannot reproduce the
   number the live service actually delivered, the bench is measuring something
   else and no comparison built on it is safe.
3. **The workhorse is 3-8x faster than the 27B at `off` on the chat tier.** The
   workhorse is MoE with roughly 3B active parameters against 27B dense, so
   the gap should be large and mostly per-token rather than prefill.
4. **`off` versus `low` on the chat tier is a bigger gap than workhorse versus
   27B-`off`.** F69 says the 27B has no middle setting; if that holds on tiny
   prompts too, the reasoning switch dominates the model choice.
5. **The 8B at `off`-equivalent beats the 27B at `off`** on the chat tier, being
   a third of the size.
6. **On the folder tier the 27B's cold time is dominated by prefill**, so its
   warm-median is at least 5x below its cold time.

**Falsifier: if the 27B at `off` lands under 2s warm-median on the chat tier**,
the two-model recommendation is wrong - it can serve the 91.3% acceptably and
should simply be made resident, since it wins every accuracy instrument and is
1.62 GiB smaller.

### The registered falsifier did not fire, so the deployed decision stands

*If the 27B at `off` lands under 2s warm-median on the chat tier, the two-model
recommendation is wrong.* It landed at 5.48s. **The workhorse stays resident and
document work is routed to the 27B by name** - which is what the routing guide
already says.

### Prediction 5 is the one worth keeping

The 8B is the second-fastest arm by token rate (41.62 tok/s) and the slowest
useful arm to an ANSWER on the chat tier, because it emits 394 tokens where the
27B emits 57. **Three of six predictions failed and the decision did not
change**, which is the honest shape of a latency experiment: the ranking was
mostly wrong and the threshold question it existed to answer was still answered.

**Evidence:** `results/raw/latency-20260819.json` plus its four serverlogs.
