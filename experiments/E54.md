## E54: the L4 tier - an instrument built to be FAILED (2026-08-17, pre-registered before any run)

**Why this exists.** Every purpose-built instrument in this repo is saturated
for Qwen3.8-27B: L1 20/20, L2 12/12, L3 12/12 at every scale to 236,122 tokens.
A ceiling ranks nothing. E52 added the reason this now matters more than it did
yesterday - **an instrument two models both saturate cannot reveal its own
confounds**, and a question-wording effect worth 2 of 12 sat inside L2/L3 from
2026-08-15 until a model small enough to be moved by it was finally run (F78).

**The design follows F60, which is the only measured theory of difficulty this
programme owns.** Across thirteen legal matters, a second BASIS to select did
not discriminate - m12 returned 4/5 from all three models and separated
nothing. A period that had to be WALKED did: m11-limitation-clocks 6/15,
m13-interest-two-clocks 7/15, against m08's 15/15. F60's closing instruction is
followed literally here: *"a future corpus should be built on periods that must
be counted, not on additional bases."* **Nine of the fifteen questions carry a
walked-period trap**, asserted mechanically by `tools/validate_ps_eval_l4.py`.

### What is different about L4, declared because it weakens the comparison

L1 to L2 to L3 held the domain fixed and raised the reasoning demanded. **L4
changes the DOMAIN**, to the three the owner picked on 2026-08-15: Irish/EU
statutory specifics, professional-services engagement terms, and adversarial
"what is wrong with this contract". A lower L4 score is therefore partly
evidence about a new domain rather than purely about capability, and it must
never be reported as though the ladder simply continued.

Two things limit the damage. The **outcome vocabulary is L2's**, so the failure
SHAPES stay comparable even where the totals do not. And one category is the
direct inverse of another, so no posture can score.

**Closed record, deliberately.** The statutory extracts AND the counting rules
are in the pack, including which reading governs where a rule has a genuine
professional ambiguity. This tier does not test whether a model remembers what
Article 12(3) says - that would confound recall with reasoning and only the
second bears on the routing decision. It tests whether, with the rule in front
of it, the model can walk the period.

**This is NOT a long-context test.** The pack is 10,029 chars, roughly 2.3K
tokens - smaller than L2's. Length is E35's question.

### Config

`tools/run_ps_eval.py --tier l4`, corpus `spikes/ps-eval/corpus_l4.py`,
questions and graders `spikes/ps-eval/questions_l4.py`. `-c 16384`, `-np 1`,
temperature 0, `max_tokens 4096`, one request per question, pack resent each
time. Fifteen questions in nine categories.

**Arms.** Qwen3.8-27B-Q4_K_M at `low` (E41's setting, and F69 measured `low`,
`medium` and `xhigh` as identical on knowledge work); Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct
workhorse as the control; Qwen3-8B-Q4_K_M with thinking ON, because E52
measured it as the second-best knowledge-work model in the building and a new
domain is where that ordering is most likely to break.

### Two named outcomes are added, and one of them IS the headline

- **`off_by_calendar`** - the right method, the wrong count. Treated a month as
  30 days, suspended an hours-period over a weekend, added two month-periods in
  sequence rather than to one trigger. This is F60's mechanism isolated, and it
  is the single number this tier exists to produce.
- **`wrong_trigger`** - counted correctly from the wrong event. One is
  arithmetic and the other is reading, and they need different fixes.

### The graders are validated, not assumed

Six synthetic respondents, each with a required signature, all passing at
registration: `perfect` 15/15; `calendar_blind` exactly 8 `off_by_calendar`;
`wrong_start` exactly 3 `wrong_trigger`; `fault_finder` (answers YES to every
yes/no) exactly 2 `wrong`; `quotes_the_page` exactly 3 `partial`; `unformatted`
15 `format_error`.

**And the check E48 taught is now mechanical.** An answer key that coincides
with a figure printed in the pack lets a model that copies a number grade as
`correct`, and no score can reveal it. **It caught two live instances during
construction**: the insurance shortfall was 1,000,000 - 500,000 = 500,000,
exactly the subcontract's own cover figure, and the records shortfall was
6 - 3 = 3, exactly the subcontract's own retention period. Both operands were
changed and the validator now asserts each difference differs from both of its
operands.

### Predictions (registered before any arm runs). The FIELD is named in each.

1. **PRIMARY: `correct` for Qwen3.8-27B is 8-13 of 15.** The band is set wide
   and deliberately below the ceiling: this tier exists to be failed, and the
   registered failure condition is at the bottom of this entry.
2. **DISCRIMINATING: `off_by_calendar` >= 2 for Qwen3.8-27B.** F60's whole
   claim is that walked periods are what separate models. If this is 0 while
   `correct` is high, F60 does not generalise beyond the closed-record matters
   it was derived from, and that is a more interesting result than a low score.
3. **The workhorse scores at least 4 below Qwen3.8 on `correct`.** It is 6
   below on L2 and L3. A new domain that collapses that gap would mean L2/L3
   were measuring something narrower than "knowledge work".
4. **`defect_absent` (X1 and X2): Qwen3.8 gets at least 1 of 2.** Both ask
   whether a real mismatch breaches a requirement that is NOT in scope, after
   three questions in a row that asked what is wrong with the contract. If
   every arm scores 0/2, an adversarial prompt produces a fault-finding posture
   rather than analysis, and that is a deployment warning that belongs in the
   routing guide regardless of the totals.
5. **Qwen3-8B scores below Qwen3.8-27B and above the workhorse**, reproducing
   E52's ordering in a domain it was not measured on.
6. **`over_claim` is 0 for Qwen3.8-27B.** It has declined correctly on every
   unanswerable item of every tier so far, and U1 is the hardest decline built
   yet - the pack discusses VAT and discusses fixed compensation, and never
   connects them.
7. **`format_error` <= 2 on every arm.** E37's falsifier, carried forward. A
   date question answered with no parseable date counts here, which is a new
   way to trip it.

**FALSIFIER, and it is the one worth watching: if Qwen3.8-27B scores 14 or 15,
L4 has not found the frontier either.** That would mean three tiers and a new
domain have all failed to bound this model on document reasoning, and the next
corpus would need a mechanism other than F60's - the honest next move then is
multi-turn or genuinely adversarial inputs, not a fourth pack of documents.

**Cost.** 45 questions across three local arms, no cloud spend. Estimated under
half an hour of GPU.

### E54: two properties of the config, declared 2026-08-17 while arm 1 was still running and before any result was read

Found by printing one complete prompt and reading what the model actually
receives, rather than trusting the pack and the template separately.

1. **The shared `PROMPT_TEMPLATE` tells the model that "where a document has
   been amended, the most recent amendment governs". L4 CONTAINS NO
   AMENDMENTS.** The instruction is inert here and is left in place
   deliberately: it is the same template L1, L2 and L3 were measured with, and
   changing it for one tier would make the failure shapes incomparable, which
   is the only thing L4 shares with the ladder. The risk it carries is small
   but real and is named rather than discovered later - an instruction to hunt
   for amendments could prime a model to look for something that is not there.
2. **`CHRON` lists its entries out of date order.** A document titled
   "chronology" that is not chronological is a mild realism defect. It is not a
   hidden difficulty - every question names its own subject and the list is six
   entries long - but a reader comparing L4 to a real engagement file should
   know it, and a model that assumes order will not be helped by it.

Neither is changed mid-experiment. Both are recorded here so that if the L4
scores come in low, these are already on the table as candidate explanations
rather than being reached for afterwards.

## E54 RESULTS (2026-08-17): L4 discriminates, and the separation is ENTIRELY the walked periods

Three local arms, 15 questions, zero `format_error`. **The registered falsifier
did not fire** - Qwen3.8-27B scored 13, not 14 or 15 - so for the first time
since 2026-08-15 there is a purpose-built instrument this model has not
exhausted.

| arm | `correct` | `citation_valid` | outcomes | wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.8-27B `low` | **13/15** | 15/15 | 1 off_by_calendar, 1 wrong | 863.3s |
| Qwen3-8B thinking on | **11/15** | 14/15 | 1 off_by_calendar, 1 truncated, 2 wrong | 413.7s |
| Qwen3-30B-A3B workhorse | **6/15** | 13/15 | 1 off_by_calendar, 1 wrong_trigger, 1 over_claim, 6 wrong | **14.9s** |

### F1 IS WITHDRAWN. It is a defective question and the defect is mine

`F1` asks for a fee total and carries **two conflicting specifications**:
*"applying clause 2.4 and the date on which notice actually takes effect"*
(derive the period) and *"the four calendar months September 2026 to December
2026 inclusive"* (here is the period). A model that derives gets a different
answer from one that reads, and both are defensible.

The 27B answered **EUR 79,200**, which is exactly September + October +
November - the period implied by clause 2.2 alone, ignoring 2.3. It derived
rather than read, and the question invited both. The 8B truncated at 4,096
tokens and the workhorse was wrong at 29 completion tokens.

**An ambiguous question scored as a capability failure is F20, and this
programme has caught it in other people's instruments twice.** So F1 comes out
of the score rather than being explained afterwards, and the totals are
restated on 14:

| arm | restated | **walked periods** | static analysis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.8-27B | **13/14** | **6/7** | 7/7 |
| Qwen3-8B | **11/14** | **4/7** | 7/7 |
| workhorse | **6/14** | **0/7** | 6/7 |

**The ordering and every gap are unchanged by the withdrawal**, which is the
test of whether a withdrawal is honest or convenient.

### The result, and it is F60 confirmed in a domain F60 was not derived from

**Split the fifteen questions into the seven that require walking a period and
the seven that do not, and the instrument resolves into two different tiers.**

- **Static analysis separates NOTHING.** Survival lookup, all three
  defect-finding items, both defect-absent items: **7/7, 7/7, 6/7**. The 30B
  MoE workhorse reads a contract, finds the clause that is missing, computes
  the shortfall between two figures in two documents, and correctly declines to
  call two out-of-scope mismatches a breach. It is not bad at contract
  analysis.
- **Walked periods separate completely: 6/7, 4/7, and 0/7.** The workhorse gets
  **none** of them. Not one date, not one interest calculation, not one notice
  period.

F60 was derived from thirteen closed-record legal matters and predicted that
*"a future corpus should be built on periods that must be counted, not on
additional bases."* This is a different domain, a different pack, different
question types and a model F60 never saw, and the mechanism reproduces exactly.
**What the small local models cannot do is walk a calendar.**

### Two failures worth naming individually

**Both capable models fail `N2` identically, and it is a composition failure.**
Asked for the date notice *actually* takes effect, the 27B and the 8B both
answered **1 December 2026**: they found clause 2.3 (notice may not take effect
before the first anniversary) and applied it, and both dropped clause 2.2 (it
must take effect on a month end). The answer is 31 December. Both got `N1` -
the same question with 2.3 explicitly disregarded - correct. So neither model
failed to find either rule; both failed to **compose two constraints that were
individually satisfied.** The workhorse did not reach the question at all,
answering 31 October.

**`B1` exposes a gap in this tier's own diagnostic, and it cuts against the
headline metric.** The 8B and the workhorse both answered **5 April** where the
answer is 6 April - an off-by-one in day counting. The registered
`off_by_calendar` trap list carried the two errors I predicted, suspending the
clock over the weekend (8 April) and rolling past the public holiday (7 April),
and **not the error that actually occurred.** So both were graded `wrong`.

### Prediction outcomes: six held, one falsified

1. **HELD.** `correct` for Qwen3.8-27B is 13, at the top of the registered
   8-13 band. The tier is hard and is not a ceiling.
2. **FALSIFIED - and the metric it names is undercounting.** `off_by_calendar`
   was registered at >= 2 for Qwen3.8 and is **1**. But B1 above shows the trap
   list missed the commonest real error, so the falsification is partly an
   instrument artefact rather than evidence about the model. **The honest
   reading is that `off_by_calendar` as built is a floor on calendar errors,
   not a count of them**, and the walked-vs-static split is the sound version
   of the same claim.
3. **HELD, and by more than registered.** The workhorse is **7** below
   Qwen3.8, against a registered floor of 4.
4. **HELD, at the ceiling.** `defect_absent` is **2/2 for every arm**,
   including the workhorse. After three consecutive questions asking what is
   wrong with the contract, no model adopted a fault-finding posture. The
   deployment warning that prediction was written to catch is not needed.
5. **HELD.** Qwen3-8B at 11 sits between the 27B's 13 and the workhorse's 6,
   reproducing E52's ordering in a domain it was not measured on.
6. **HELD.** `over_claim` is 0 for Qwen3.8 and for the 8B. The workhorse
   answered the unanswerable VAT item, making it the only arm to fabricate.
7. **HELD.** `format_error` is 0 on all three arms.

### What this changes

The routing guide's knowledge-work row rests on tiers where the workhorse
scored 5/12 and 4/12 without saying what it was failing at. **It is failing at
dates and money over time, and at nothing else measured here.** That is a
sharper and more useful instruction than a total, and it belongs in the guide:
*use the workhorse to read a contract; do not use it to work out a deadline.*

**The instrument itself needs one repair before it is re-used:** F1 must be
rewritten to name either the period or the derivation, not both, and the
`off_by_calendar` trap lists need the off-by-one forms that B1 produced.
Neither is done in this entry, because changing an instrument in the same pass
that reports its first result is how a tier stops being comparable to itself.

### E54b: the repair re-run (2026-08-17, declared before it ran)

Both repairs the results entry named are done, in a separate pass from the one
that reported the first result, so E54's numbers are already banked in git and
cannot be reshaped by the repair.

1. **`F1` rewritten.** The derivation clause is removed and the named period
   kept: *"What total fee is payable for the four calendar months September
   2026 to December 2026 inclusive?"* The derivation is what goes, not the
   period - keeping it would double-penalise a model that already lost `N2`,
   and this question exists to test the 1 October rate straddle, which is a
   walked period in its own right. **79,200 - the answer the ambiguous wording
   produced - is now a NAMED `off_by_calendar` trap**, so if it recurs it is
   visibly the same error rather than a fresh one.
2. **Off-by-one traps added** to `P1`, `P2`, `B1`, `N1` and `N2`, generated
   rather than typed. The original lists carried the errors that were predicted
   and not the one that happened.
3. **New mechanical check:** no trap may equal its own question's answer. A
   generated trap landing on the key would grade a CORRECT answer as a named
   failure - the inverse of the pack-collision check and just as invisible.
   22/22 checks pass.

**`F1`'s result is NOT comparable to the withdrawn one** - it is a different
question. The other fourteen are unchanged and are directly comparable.

**Predictions, registered before the re-run.**

1. **The fourteen unchanged questions reproduce their E54 outcomes exactly for
   every arm.** Temperature 0, same config, same sequence position. If any
   moves, F70's sequence effect reaches this tier and the totals need the same
   noise treatment F76 forced on the tool-use instrument.
2. **`off_by_calendar` RISES for at least one arm**, because the two `B1`
   answers of 5 April were previously graded `wrong` and are now named. This is
   a scoring change, not a capability change, and must be reported as one.
3. **At least one arm now scores `F1` correct.** The 27B's 79,200 came from
   deriving a period the question also named; with the derivation removed, the
   remaining task is noticing one rate change. If NO arm gets it, the rate
   straddle is genuinely hard and F1 becomes the tier's most discriminating
   item rather than its defect.
