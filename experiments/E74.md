## E74 PRE-REGISTRATION - KA-H1: real knowledge work on real documents (2026-08-19)

**Registered BEFORE any run.** This is the instrument for the first of the two
questions the programme has to answer with numbers.

### Why this experiment exists

| | |
|---|---|
| **which success question** | *What percentage of my serious knowledge work can I complete locally, within roughly 30-40 seconds, without wishing I had used a frontier model?* |
| **uncertainty reduced** | every existing knowledge instrument in this lab is either a CEILING for strong arms (L1 20/20, L2 12/12, L3-hard 12/12 - F91) or measures date arithmetic (AABR). Neither answers what share of ordinary document work completes correctly inside 30-40 seconds |
| **decision that changes** | whether local knowledge work becomes the default or stays experimental, and which arm serves the **Think** role in the Quick/Think/Build configuration |

### Why a new instrument rather than another tier of ps-eval

Two independent reasons, and each on its own would be enough. **ps-eval's tiers
are saturated** - L1 20/20, L2 12/12 and L3-hard 12/12 for every strong arm - and
the goal says explicitly that a test at a ceiling must stop being used to rank
strong models. **And its corpus is synthetic**, while the goal asks for real or
representative work including multi-document tasks.

### The corpus is this lab's own documents

`spikes/kw-eval/kw_corpus.py` assembles real sparkbench material - the
memory-edge deadlock report, the decode-hang incident, the harness rules, the
programme report, the routing guide, the R&D programme plan, and nine more at
the long size. Technical prose written to be acted on, by several hands, over
months, **with the inconsistencies that implies**. A corpus written in one
sitting to be answered has no conflicts in it to find, and "finding conflicts
between documents" is one of the eight work shapes the goal names.

**Every document postdates the arms' knowledge cutoff.** No arm can have
memorised an answer.

| pack | docs | chars |
|---|---|---|
| `s` | 4 | 55,665 |
| `m` | 6 | 140,526 |
| `l` | 15 | 263,339 |

**Sizes are named in CHARS, never tokens.** chars/4 understates technical text
here by roughly 40% - the "12K" E10 log was 16,140 tokens for Qwen - so a token
figure written now would be a guess dressed as a measurement. The runner
tokenizes each pack **through the server** and records that number.

**Packs are nested prefixes**, so a question answerable in `s` stays answerable
in `l`. Without that the ladder measures three different tests instead of
context handling.

### The items, and the gate that verifies them

14 items covering the goal's work shapes: `compare` 3, `implication` 3,
`conflict` 3, `ambiguity` 1, `recommend` 1, `summarise` 1, `longctx` 1,
`draft` 1.

**Every item carries the exact substrings its answer rests on, and the gate
refuses the bank if any is missing from that item's pack.** The answer key must
be something the corpus says, not something I remembered.

**The gate also checks ABSENCE for items claiming a question is unsettled** -
and it immediately caught one of mine. K12 asserted the corpus does not name the
canonical TTS voice; it does, in the first three lines of a document whose title
and bulk say the opposite. The item became a **conflict** item instead, and it
is now one of the better ones: the document says of itself that it *"reads
exactly like a settled decision"* and is not one.

### THIS RUN IS PACKS `s` AND `m` ONLY - 12 items

The `l` pack is deferred, deliberately. At 263,339 chars it needs roughly
`-c 131072`, and gpt-oss-120b at 59.03 GiB plus a KV cache that large is the
>60 GiB regime `docs/memory-edge-deadlock.md` governs. That belongs to KA-H2,
run attended with its own preflight, not bolted onto an unattended KA-H1 run.

### Arms

| arm | serving |
|---|---|
| `workhorse` Qwen3-30B-A3B-2507 | `-c 32768` (s) / `-c 65536` (m) |
| `qwen38` Qwen3.8-27B | same, plus `--spec-type draft-mtp --spec-draft-n-max 4` (F84's graduated operating point) |
| `gptoss` gpt-oss-120b | same |

**No tool schema is offered to any arm.** F94 measured tool presence as a large,
model-specific, non-monotonic variable - the workhorse lost three of twenty on a
set with no date question in it, as non-termination. An instrument that offers
tools is measuring the serving config as much as the model.

**The frontier comparator is run separately** via the Claude Code subagent
transport (as E65 and E69 did) and labelled as a DIFFERENT harness, never pooled
with an API arm.

### The headline metric is a PAIR

`success_at_30s` and `success_at_40s` first - correct AND inside the budget,
with wrong, truncated and non-terminating all scoring 0. Conditional TTCA
(p50 and p95 over successes only) second, always quoted with its success count.

Recorded per item: `wall_s`, `ttft_s`, `prompt_tokens`, `completion_tokens`,
`hit_token_cap`, `answer_chars`, outcome class, and `critical_error`.

**Truncation is a completion failure, not a wrong answer.** F20 recorded three
silently truncated answers as a capability result; `hit_token_cap` makes that
visible in the record rather than in a footnote.

### Predictions, each naming the FIELD it is scored against

| # | prediction | scored against |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | at least one arm reaches `success_at_40s` >= 8 of 12 | `success_at_40s`, per arm |
| 2 | the workhorse's `correct` total is the lowest of the three local arms | `correct`, per arm |
| 3 | no local arm scores 12/12 - the instrument does not saturate on its first outing | `correct`, max across arms, predicted <= 11 |
| 4 | gpt-oss-120b's `conditional_ttca_p50` on pack `m` is under 30s | `conditional_ttca_p50`, arm `gptoss`, pack `m` |
| 5 | at least one arm commits a critical error - a confident answer to the item the corpus does not settle | `critical_errors`, summed across arms, predicted >= 1 |

**Prediction 3 is the one that decides whether this instrument survives.** A
12/12 makes it another L3-hard and it must be made harder before it is used to
rank anything.

### Registered decision rule

| observed | action |
|---|---|
| any arm 12/12 | the instrument is a ceiling. Do NOT report a ranking; harden it first |
| spread across arms `correct` = 0 | it does not discriminate; rebuild rather than extend |
| best arm `success_at_40s` >= 8/12 | local knowledge work is viable at this budget for this shape of task; extend the bank toward 30 items and add the frontier comparator before any percentage is quoted |
| best arm `success_at_40s` <= 4/12 | the 30-40s budget is the binding constraint, not capability - report that, and re-run without a deadline to separate the two |

**No percentage answer to the success question is claimed from 12 items.** The
output is a discrimination verdict, a failure taxonomy and a latency
distribution. A rate needs the extended bank and the frontier arm beside it.

**Evidence to be written:** `results/raw/kw-<arm>-<pack>.json`.

### What this changes before a single local arm runs

**The instrument has NO headroom above the local arms.** Its entire
discriminating value now rests on the local arms scoring below 12, and if they
do not, this is another L3-hard (F91) - a tie at ceiling, which is a statement
about the ruler and not about the models. That is registered here, before the

Prediction 3 was worded *"no LOCAL arm scores 12/12"* and stands unchanged. What
this adds is the reference: the ceiling is real and a frontier model reaches it.

### TWO SCORER DEFECTS FOUND AND FIXED, BOTH BEFORE ANY LOCAL ARM RAN

Both were false NEGATIVES - the scorer marking a correct answer wrong - and both
are declared here rather than folded in quietly. Neither changes what counts as
a correct answer; each corrects a mechanical check that could not recognise one.

1. **Inflection.** K09's check required the literal string `hang`; the answer
   said the model **"hung"**. `must_contain_all` entries may now be a LIST of
   spellings, satisfied by any one. English inflection is not a capability
   difference.
2. **Negation.** K07's expected answer is literally *"do not use the
   workhorse"*, and the trap phrase `use the workhorse` matched inside it. Trap
   phrases are now only counted where they are ASSERTED, not where they are
   being ruled out.

**Why fixing these now is legitimate and not retrofitting.** No local arm had
run, so no local result could have influenced either fix; both apply uniformly
to every arm including this one; and each corrects a demonstrable false
negative rather than loosening the key. The frontier arm's score moved 11 -> 12
as a result, and that movement is stated rather than presented as the original
figure. Both defects now have tests naming the answer that exposed them.

**Evidence:** `results/raw/kw-frontier-subagent-{s,m}.json` (each carrying its
`harness_caveats` block), `results/raw/kw-scores-frontier.json`.

### THREE ITEMS WERE REPAIRED, AND THE REASON MATTERS MORE THAN THE ITEMS

`K19`, `K23` and `K25` each admitted **more than one defensible answer from the
corpus**, and the frontier arm gave the reading the key was not written against:

- `K19` asked for "two properties" of the downloads rule. The rule's TITLE says
  explicit and manifest-recorded; its BODY requires size and SHA256. Both true.
- `K23` asked for a model that "tops one kind of evaluation and fails another
  badly". That fits the workhorse (document matrix, then zero on real repos) AND
  gpt-oss-120b (12/12 on the hard tier, then a decode hang).
- `K25` asked for "a headline metric reported as a PAIR". The corpus contains
  TWO such pairs - Rule 10's aggregate/capacity throughput, and the programme's
  success-at-deadline/conditional TTCA.

**They were REPAIRED, not widened.** Widening a key each time a strong arm
surprises me tunes the instrument to that arm, and this was the third such
adjustment in one evening. Removing the ambiguity keeps the item and keeps it
fair, and **the frontier arm was re-run on the repaired wording** so no arm is
scored on a question another arm never saw.

**The pattern is the finding, and it generalises beyond this instrument:**
authoring items against a corpus you know well produces questions that read as
unambiguous to the author and are not. Three of twenty-six, and every one was
invisible until an arm answered it a different way. A pilot arm run BEFORE the
arms under test is therefore not a luxury; it is how the instrument gets
debugged without contaminating the measurement.

**Declared, because it bears on how the frontier figure should be read.** Two
scorer fixes (inflection, negation) and three item repairs were all prompted by
this arm's own answers. Its score under the ORIGINAL checks and wording was
**11/12 on the first twelve**; under the repaired instrument it is 23/24 of
twenty-four. The local arms are scored on the repaired instrument, which is the
only fair comparison available - but the frontier arm alone had the opportunity
to shape it, and that is a real asymmetry rather than a footnote.

**A stale-answer bug was caught in the collection step and is worth recording:**
the banking script skipped an id it already held rather than replacing it, so
`K19` was first scored against its PRE-repair answer and read as wrong. Found by
checking why a repaired item still failed - not by trusting the total.

**gpt-oss-120b is the WORST local arm on real knowledge work: 11 of 24.** The

**E70's question is therefore answered, and not in the direction it was posed.**
E70 asked whether the 30-40s budget neutralises gpt-oss's latency advantage. It
does more than that: at this budget gpt-oss is beaten on ACCURACY by both other
local arms, so the latency argument never gets made.

## E74 TAXONOMY CORRECTION - gpt-oss-120b fabricated NOTHING. I did not check. (2026-08-20)

**Trigger:** authoring E76's absence gate required verifying which topics the
corpus does not settle. That check was run against the three passages the E74
taxonomy had recorded as "confident fabrications with invented citations".

**All three are real corpus content:**

| claimed fabrication | verified |
|---|---|
| `--jinja` advice attributed to the harness rules (`K06`) | **PRESENT** - `docs/HARNESS-RULES.md` lines 165-190 |
| "template-confounded ... excluding chains it is 96%" (`K10`) | **PRESENT** - routing guide line 82, verbatim |
| Qwen3-8B "listed as 4.68 GiB" (`K24`) | **PRESENT** - routing guide lines 42 and 111 |

**gpt-oss-120b invented nothing.** It retrieved real passages and applied them
to the wrong questions. That is a retrieval and reasoning failure, and it is a
materially less alarming one than confabulation.

### The corrected taxonomy of its six misses

| item | mechanism | whose fault |
|---|---|---|
| `K06` | **the corpus contains a SECOND explicitly-corrected recommendation** - the `--jinja` retraction - which the answer identified correctly and cited to the right file. The key accepted only the Mistral correction | **instrument** - a fourth ambiguous item |
| `K10` | wrong conclusion from a genuinely conflicting corpus (38% on the full set vs 96% excluding chains) plus a misattributed source file | model |
| `K09` | retrieved a real restriction, but the deadlock one rather than the unattended-use one | model |
| `K08` | hit the 1,024-token ceiling, empty answer | configuration |
| `K24` | hit the same ceiling mid-answer | configuration |
| `K16` | correct in substance, not settleable by the mechanical check | instrument |

**Two real misses, two truncations, two instrument defects. Zero fabrications.**

### How I got it wrong, because the mechanism matters

I read three answers that *looked* invented - confident, specific, with a file
citation - and classified them as fabrication **without checking whether the
quoted text was in the pack.** The check takes one `grep`. The absence gate
built into the item bank exists precisely to prevent this class of error, and I
committed it in prose where no gate runs.

**This is the third time in two days the same shape has appeared:** a surprising
model-specific result that turned out to be a property of the measurement rather
than of the model. First the `/tmp` worktree, then the ASCII scorer, now this.
The standing rule already written for it applies to prose conclusions and not
only to code:

> **Any surprising model-specific failure cluster triggers an instrument audit
> before it becomes an architectural conclusion.**

**What changes.** The claim "three of its six real misses are confident
fabrications with invented citations" is **RETRACTED**. It appeared in the
interim report and in the email sent to the owner on 2026-08-20, both of which
are corrected. gpt-oss-120b's zero critical errors and its zero fabrications now
say the same thing rather than pulling against each other.

**What does not change.** Its corrected score of 18/24 stands, and it is still
third of three local arms on the 30-second objective. `K10` and `K09` remain
genuine misses.
