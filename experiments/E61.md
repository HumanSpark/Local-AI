## E61: can a different MoE close the hard-tier gap, or is the split DENSE vs SPARSE? (2026-08-19, pre-registered before any run)

**The question.** The workhorse scores **5/12** on the L3 hard tier where
Qwen3.8-27B scores **12/12**. Two models already on hand test whether a
different MoE closes that, without a download:

| model | arch | what it tests |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-30B-A3B (workhorse) | MoE, ~3B active | existing baseline, **5/12** |
| **GLM-4.7-Flash** | MLA, 30B-A3B class | can another small-active MoE improve hard reasoning? |
| **gpt-oss-120b** | MoE, 117B total / ~5.1B active | does much larger TOTAL capacity help despite tiny active set? |
| Qwen3.8-27B | dense | quality ceiling, **12/12** |

**Both candidates are already downloaded and manifest-recorded.** GLM-4.7-Flash
is live on benchhost; gpt-oss-120b (59.03 GiB, 3 shards, SHA256 pinned, fetched
2026-07-03) is on lanhost's backup store. No new downloads gate this.

**Speed is ignored on the first pass** beyond checking it is not catastrophic.
This tier is about whether the capability exists at all.

### What is already known, and why it lowers but does not settle the prior

gpt-oss-120b has been measured on speed and writing, never on knowledge work:
**53.44 tg128, 48.35 tg@8K, drafting 3.00, MMLU 72.7%** - and that MMLU is
BELOW the workhorse's 80.2%. GLM-4.7-Flash has architecture findings (F8: it
runs the deepseek2/MLA graph; F11, F18: MLA anti-scales under concurrency) and
a drafting score of 3.10, but has **never been run on any knowledge-work tier**.

MMLU is not the failure being measured. L3-hard is much closer to it, so a low
MMLU lowers the prior without deciding anything.

### Registered decision bands, applied to BOTH candidates

- **10-12/12** - serious reviewer candidate. Proceed to an E57-style held-out
  run and to latency.
- **8-9/12** - interesting middle tier, worth pursuing ONLY if latency is
  compelling.
- **7/12 or below** - not enough gain over the workhorse to justify another
  resident model. Close the candidate.

### Predictions

1. **PRIMARY: gpt-oss-120b scores 7/12 or below**, i.e. lands in the closing
   band. Its MMLU is below the workhorse's and the L3 failure is about holding
   implicit constraints together, which total parameter count may not buy.
2. **GLM-4.7-Flash scores 5-8/12** - better than the workhorse but well short of
   the 27B.
3. **Neither reaches 10/12.**
4. **The failures cluster on the SAME questions the workhorse misses** -
   precedence and multi-hop.

**The falsifier that would change the architecture: either candidate at 10/12 or
above.** That would mean a fast sparse model can do the work the dense 27B is
resident for, and the routing decision reopens.

**The counter-argument, registered because it is the more likely finding.** If
BOTH candidates fail in roughly the same places as the workhorse, the useful
conclusion is that **the dense-versus-sparse split matters more than choosing a
better MoE**. That closes the whole MoE-hunting branch and promotes the cascade
experiment - workhorse drafts, 27B audits and corrects, measured on total
elapsed time to a correct answer - to the front of the queue. A negative result
here is therefore worth as much as a positive one.

**Cost.** Two arms on an existing 12-question tier, local, no cloud spend.
gpt-oss-120b at 59.03 GiB is NEAR-EDGE for the amdgpu suballocator deadlock
(`docs/memory-edge-deadlock.md`): it must be loaded with the box quiet, with no
concurrent download, backup or hashing, and with someone able to power-cycle.
The backup and any downloads complete FIRST, and nothing overlaps that load.

### E61 GLM CONFIRMATION: the decision rule, registered BEFORE the arm runs (2026-08-19 06:28)

Written while the `max_tokens 16384` arm is still queued and unrun, because a
budget that gets reinterpreted after seeing the score is a moving target.

GLM-4.7-Flash scored **10/12** at `max_tokens 4096`. Both misses - `P1`
(precedence) and `Z1` (underspecified) - hit `finish_reason: length` at exactly
4,096 generated tokens. They are truncations, **not demonstrated wrong answers**,
which means 10/12 **may underestimate** what GLM can achieve. It does not mean
the true score is higher: a completed answer can still be wrong.

**The rule:**

1. **If both truncated cases complete CORRECTLY at 16,384 tokens**, the 4,096
   result was budget-limited. GLM earns latency testing against the real traffic
   mix **immediately**, ahead of GPT-OSS-120B.
2. **If either completes INCORRECTLY**, that is a capability result. Retain the
   resulting 10/12 or 11/12 and apply the existing decision bands **without
   reinterpretation** - no further budget raises, no "it was nearly right".
3. **Either way the 4,096 figure remains the headline** for comparison against
   the workhorse's 5/12 and the 27B's 12/12, because that is the budget all three
   were measured at.

**And if GLM reaches 12/12, it is NOT thereby equivalent to the dense 27B.** The
next instrument is E57's held-out depth design, not another single-turn tier.
E57 is precisely the experiment that showed a strong single-turn result
disappearing when the context structure changed - Qwen3-8B held 5 of 5 anchors
with its own answer in view and lost 4 of 10 without it. A sparse model matching
12/12 on one hard tier would be an excellent result and would still say nothing
about whether it holds up across a long conversation.

## F86 - model evaluation and SYSTEM evaluation are different measurements, and ranking models produces wrong engineering answers

Recorded 2026-08-19 as the session's most transferable output. Five results from
this programme, each of which a model-ranking table gets wrong:

| observation | what ranking models would conclude | what is actually true |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-8B: 41.6 tok/s, second-fastest arm | fast model | **slowest but one to a user** - 9.46s, because it emits 394 tokens where the 27B emits 57 |
| MTP on Qwen3.8-27B | "MTP is worth 2.08x" | **2.08x on structured legal work, 1.32x on chat** - same model, same flag, same hardware |
| gpt-oss-120b: 117B total against the workhorse's 30B | more capacity, better answers | **MMLU 72.7% vs 80.2%** and no evidence either way on the hard-tier failures that actually matter |
| GLM-4.7-Flash: 10/12 | a 10/12 model | **cannot yet distinguish capability failure from budget exhaustion** |
| workhorse: 5/12 on L3-hard | too weak for document work | **may be an excellent first stage** in a system whose answer is checked by the 27B |

**The hierarchy this implies, and the order matters:**

```
model metrics  ->  workload metrics  ->  task outcome  ->  system cost
```

Each level can invert the one before it. A model metric (tok/s) inverted at the
workload level (the 8B). A workload metric (2.08x decode) failed to transfer
across workloads (MTP on chat). A task outcome may yet invert a capability score
(the workhorse at 5/12 as a drafting stage).

**`tok/s` becomes actively misleading the moment cascades enter.** A two-call
system can generate fewer tokens per second in aggregate and still reach a
trustworthy answer sooner, if the expensive model only has to inspect and repair
a concise draft rather than write one. Any cascade instrument must therefore
measure **total elapsed time to a correct answer, counting every call**, and
never a per-model rate.

**These are DESIGN PRINCIPLES, not conclusions.** Their use is to settle what
the next experiment should measure whenever there is ambiguity. The question is
not *which model wins* but **which arrangement reaches a trustworthy answer
fastest and most economically.**

- The useful unit of optimisation is the **workload**, not the model.
- The useful unit of architecture is the **task outcome**, not the model call.

### E61 gpt-oss-120b: the smoke test's interpretation, registered BEFORE it runs (2026-08-19)

The arm is **UNRUN**. Its 0/12 is twelve `transport_failed` outcomes - a
transport failure before the model produced any evaluable answer is
**infrastructure evidence, not model-quality evidence** - and must never be
quoted as a capability score. Detail: `docs/2026-08-19-gptoss120b-decode-hang.md`.

`tools/smoke_first_token.py`: one ten-token prompt, `max_tokens 32`, hard
timeout, **streaming** so time-to-first-token is stamped separately from total
request time. TTFT is the measurement here, not the response - if loading
succeeds, prompt evaluation succeeds, and the first sampled token never appears,
the boundary is narrowed considerably.

**The four branches, and what each does and does NOT support:**

1. **No generated token before the timeout** -> supports a first-decode /
   backend stall. **It does not by itself prove Vulkan/RADV is the cause.** A GPU
   fence wait is a plausible mechanism; the externally observed symptom is a
   stalled decode and nothing more. Do not write a mechanism into the record that
   was not measured.
2. **Tokens arrive but generation never terminates** -> supports the
   reasoning/runaway branch, the F71 shape (Qwen3.8 at `xhigh` burning 100,000
   tokens over 2h 46m and emitting nothing).
3. **32 tokens complete normally** -> the failure is INTERMITTENT or
   workload/context dependent. **Only then** is a slightly larger reproduction
   justified.
4. **The process exits or crashes** -> preserve stderr, the serverlog, exit
   status and the amdgpu kernel messages BEFORE another attempt. bench
   cannot read the kernel ring buffer, so `dmesg | grep -i amdgpu` is an owner
   action.

**Priority is explicitly subordinate to GLM.** If GLM's confirmation completes
both truncated cases correctly, the strategic value of debugging gpt-oss drops
sharply. If GLM fails one or both, getting gpt-oss to emit a first token becomes
considerably more valuable. The two must not be allowed to masquerade as each
other:

> **GLM's confirmation decides architecture priority. The gpt-oss smoke test
> diagnoses infrastructure. Neither result is evidence of the other kind.**

Which is the general form of what this session keeps re-learning: **before asking
how well the model performed, establish that you measured the model at all.**

## E61 GLM RESULT (2026-08-19): 10/12 with two NON-TERMINATING reasoning failures

**GLM-4.7-Flash scores 10/12 on the hard tier, with two non-terminating
reasoning failures that persist from 4,096 to 16,384 output tokens.**

That wording is deliberate. It keeps capability and robustness separate, and it
makes clear that the larger budget **falsified** the "it just needed more room"
interpretation rather than confirming it.

| budget | score | wall | P1 | Z1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `max_tokens 4096` | 10/12 | 423.9s | truncated, 4,096 gen | truncated, 4,096 gen |
| `max_tokens 16384` | **10/12** | 1001.2s | **truncated, 16,384 gen** | **truncated, 16,384 gen** |

Every other cell identical. Wall time 2.4x, entirely from the two runaways.

**The shape matters more than the score.** The ten successful answers use a
**median of 1,365** generated tokens. P1 and Z1 reach **16,384 without producing
an answer** - **exactly 12.0x** the median. That is not a modest long-tail
effect; it is a distinct failure mode.

**Reasoning volume on the two:** 68,016 and 65,662 characters, and no answer.

**Correction 2026-08-19 (median restated 1,373 -> 1,365).** The ten completed
answers are 898, 1006, 1077, 1162, 1357, 1373, 1640, 1661, 1946, 2328. With an
even sample the median is the mean of the 5th and 6th values, **1,365**; 1,373 is
the upper-middle element - `sorted[n//2]` on an even-length list. It changes the
headline ratio in the finding's favour: `16384/1365` is **12.00x exactly**, where
`16384/1373` is 11.93x, so "at least 12x" was true only on the corrected figure.
Recomputed from `results/raw/e61-20260819-glm47flash-l3n0{,-budget16k}.json`;
both arms carry the identical ten completions, which is itself the
bit-determinism of F88.

### Against the registered rule: reality produced a THIRD branch

The rule registered before the run had two branches - both complete correctly
(budget-limited), or either completes incorrectly (capability result). Neither
fits: **neither completed at all.** Rule 2's operative clause is applied
unchanged - **10/12 stands, and no further budget raises** - and the rule is NOT
retrofitted to claim it anticipated this.

**A precision the score does not carry:** capability on P1 and Z1 is
**UNMEASURED, not demonstrated-absent.** The model never committed, so whether
it could have reached the right answer is unknown. "10/12 with 2
non-terminating" is a stronger and more defensible claim than a bare 10/12, and
is the form that should be quoted.

### The qualitative pattern, labelled as a pattern from two cases

`P1` is **precedence**; `Z1` is **underspecified**. Both require **committing
under conflict or ambiguity**. Failing to terminate on exactly those two, while
finishing ten others at a 1,365-token median, is not a knowledge failure - it is
**a failure to terminate deliberation when there is no clean resolution.**

This is a pattern from two cases, not a general theorem, and must be quoted that
way.

**For a reviewer role it is pointed.** "These two rules conflict" is a NORMAL
input for a reviewer, not an edge case. The practical phrasing:

> GLM showed strong capability on most hard items, but on the two questions that
> required committing under conflict or ambiguity it failed to terminate even
> with a 4x larger token budget. That makes it risky as an **unattended**
> reviewer unless a watchdog and fallback are part of the design.

That says why the failure matters operationally without claiming it "cannot
reason about conflict", which the evidence does not support.

### The counterpoint, which must not be lost

**10/12 is still materially stronger than the workhorse's 5/12 on this tier.**
E61 rules out GLM as a **dependable unattended reviewer without a watchdog**. It
does NOT rule out GLM as a useful specialist for bounded workloads where a hard
generation cap and a fallback route are acceptable. Do not discard it.

### Three things to carry forward as METRICS

1. **Non-termination rate is a first-class robustness metric**, tracked
   alongside correctness. A wrong answer is detectable downstream; a reviewer
   that sometimes enters a 16K-token loop becomes the bottleneck for the whole
   task.
2. **A stopping rule for future diagnostics:** if an item exceeds roughly **4-6x
   the model's median successful generation length** without reaching an answer,
   treat it as a runaway candidate and terminate rather than assuming more budget
   helps. **NOT applied retroactively to the published GLM score** - it is a
   time-saver for later work, not a re-scoring.
3. **Commitment latency**, a metric this programme does not yet have. Total
   tokens and wall time cannot distinguish a model taking a long time because
   the task is genuinely long from one consuming thousands of tokens without
   ever crossing from deliberation into an answer. P1 and Z1 are the latter.
   Even a crude proxy - *reasoning tokens before the first answer-bearing
   sentence* - would be valuable for comparing reviewer candidates.

## E61 gpt-oss-120b: the bisect - the original failure is INTERMITTENT and remains UNEXPLAINED

The smoke test completed normally: **TTFT 0.98s**, 31 chunks, 1.5s. That killed
both registered hypotheses - no first-decode stall, no runaway - and selected
branch 3: workload/context dependent, so a larger reproduction was justified.

`tools/bisect_hang.py` walked five rungs **inside one model load**, moving one
variable at a time from the condition known to work toward the condition known
to fail. Loading once removes load-to-load variation as a confound and spends
the wall clock on requests rather than on reloading a model that already works.

| rung | condition | result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | tiny prompt, `max_tokens 32` | ok, ttft 0.88s |
| 2 | tiny prompt, `max_tokens 4096` | ok, ttft 0.16s |
| 3 | 4,000-char prompt, `max_tokens 32` | ok, ttft 2.44s |
| 4 | real 13,393-char pack, `max_tokens 32` | ok, ttft 8.57s |
| **5** | **real pack, `max_tokens 4096` - the failing condition** | **ok, ttft 0.15s, 305 chunks, 6.4s** |

**NO rung stalled, including the full failing condition.**

Applying the interpretation rules exactly as registered:

- **The original failure remains intermittent and unexplained.**
- **The walk succeeding does NOT retroactively make the first failure "not
  real".** Twelve `transport_failed` outcomes happened and the serverlog stopped
  mid-decode.
- A rung failing would have identified **the condition that exposes** the
  failure, not the root cause; none did, so not even that is available.
- It remains **infrastructure evidence, not model-quality evidence**.

Rung 5 satisfies the "single hard-tier item before committing to all 12" gate -
real pack, real question shape, the full answer budget - so the 12-question
re-run follows.

## The evidence-class distinction, which is now the session's organising idea

**Failures of COMPLETION, TRANSPORT, CORRECTNESS and LATENCY are different
evidence classes and must not collapse into one score.** This session produced
one of each:

| class | instance | what it is NOT |
|---|---|---|
| transport | gpt-oss 12x `transport_failed` | not a 0/12 capability score |
| completion | GLM P1/Z1 non-terminating | not a wrong answer; capability there is unmeasured |
| correctness | GLM 10/12, workhorse 5/12 | not a statement about speed or reliability |
| latency | 27B 5.48s vs workhorse 0.91s chat | not a statement about quality |

**A model can have good capability and still be a poor system component because
of its failure mode.** That is exactly what a leaderboard cannot show, and it is
the sharpest form of F86 this session produced.

## E61 gpt-oss-120b RESULT (2026-08-19): 12/12 - the dense/sparse split is NOT the limiting factor

The 12-question re-run, after the smoke test and the five-rung bisect established
the earlier failure was intermittent infrastructure rather than model behaviour.

| arm | score | wall | median gen | non-terminating |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| workhorse 30B-A3B | 5/12 | **14.1s** | 23 | 0 |
| GLM-4.7-Flash | 10/12 | 423.9s | 1,640 | **2** |
| **gpt-oss-120b** | **12/12** | **178.7s** | 392 | 0 |
| Qwen3.8-27B (dense) | 12/12 | 485.6s | 401 | 0 |

**gpt-oss-120b matches the dense 27B's score at 2.7x the speed**, with
near-identical median generation (392 vs 401 tokens), citations 12/12, and zero
non-termination. Per-question wall: min 4.38s, median 13.28s, max 27.22s.

### Predictions: the primary one was decisively falsified

1. **PRIMARY - gpt-oss-120b scores 7/12 or below. FALSIFIED at 12/12.** The
   reasoning behind it - MMLU 72.7% against the workhorse's 80.2%, and total
   capacity not necessarily buying the specific failure being measured - was
   sound as a prior and simply wrong. MMLU did not predict this tier.
2. **GLM 5-8/12. FALSIFIED** at 10/12.
3. **Neither reaches 10/12. FALSIFIED twice over.**
4. **Failures cluster on the same questions the workhorse misses. VOID** - one
   candidate had no failures at all.

**The registered counter-argument is REJECTED.** It said that if both candidates
failed in the same places, the dense-versus-sparse split would matter more than
choosing a better MoE, and the MoE-hunting branch should close. Instead a sparse
model with roughly 5.1B active parameters matched the dense model exactly.
**Sparsity is not the limiting factor**, and the branch closes in the opposite
direction from the one anticipated.

### What this does NOT establish

- **ONE run**, and the immediately preceding run of the **identical
  configuration** failed entirely with 12x `transport_failed`
  (`docs/2026-08-19-gptoss120b-decode-hang.md`, still unexplained). **Not
  load-bearing until repeated.**
- **Capability only.** Robustness (E57 held-out depth), efficiency (real traffic
  latency) and system value are all open - the F86 four questions, three of them
  unanswered.
- `distractors=0` only. F74's scaling behaviour is unmeasured for this model.

### Why the diagnostic sequence mattered

Had the first run's 0/12 been recorded as a capability score, this model would
have been discarded as hopeless. It is instead the joint-best arm on the tier.
The distinction between transport failure and model quality (F87) was worth
exactly one model in this instance.
