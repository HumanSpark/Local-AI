## E86 PRE-REGISTRATION - is E85's K05 flip POSITION-INDEPENDENT? And the literal repeat is abandoned as vacuous (2026-08-29)

**Registered before any arm runs.** The owner asked for "the E85 repeat". This
is not that run, and the reason is recorded here rather than discovered later.

### Why the repeat as specified would have measured NOTHING

E85 closed with: *"A repeat. Two arms, same configuration, to establish whether
K05 and K06 flip deterministically or are draws."*

**F59 forbids it, and F59 is ACTIVE and explicitly scoped to this case:**
*"llama-server generation at temperature 0 is BYTE-deterministic (10/10) ... at
temperature 0 with nothing varying, extra reps reproduce the same bytes and
measure nothing. Reps become informative only when something in the config
moves."* Status line: *"Applies to any llama-server arm at temperature 0."*

`tools/run_kw_eval.py` hardcodes `"temperature": 0`. F88 says the same thing from
the other side - a bit-deterministic arm *"reproduces its errors exactly as
faithfully as its successes"*, so a second identical run *"retires exactly one
hypothesis and no others"*, and the hypothesis it retires ("E85 got lucky") is
not the one E85 needed retired.

So an identical re-run was guaranteed to reproduce K05 and K06 and would have
been reported as "the flip is deterministic" - which F59 already tells us a
priori, and which does **not** distinguish a real q8_0 effect from an item this
bank cannot resolve. That is a **vacuous success**: a check whose preconditions
make its assertion meaningless. It is registered here as abandoned, not silently
replaced.

### What the two flipped items actually are - read before designing

**K05 is a real and meaningful difference.** It is a deliberate abstention item
(`"is_unanswerable": True`, category `ambiguity`): the pack gives no hang rate.
P0 (f16) answered *"1 in 1 ... the probability is 1 (100%)"*. P1 (q8_0) answered
*"the material does not provide a measured per-load probability ... cannot be
determined"*. That is the over-claim failure mode against the refusal-to-fabricate
property, not a coin flip between two near-equal answers.

**K06 is a DEFECTIVE ITEM and is withdrawn from the verdict count.** Its key is
`must_contain_any: ["Mistral","conditional"]` + `must_contain_all: ["3","13"]`.
P1 gave the Mistral/E45 correction and passed. P0 instead identified the
`--jinja` retraction - which is genuinely present in the pack at
`docs/HARNESS-RULES.md:165-169` ("`--jinja` turns out to default to ENABLED on
this build, so the flag was a no-op ... retracted 2026-07-17"), cited with its
control file. **Both answers are correct.** The question says *"advice that
explicitly corrects an earlier recommendation"* as though there is one; the six
m-pack documents carry 19 instances of correction language. K06 presupposes a
uniqueness the corpus does not have.

This is **F95's shape** - *"a deterministic grader can be reproducible and still
be systematically wrong, and reproducibility makes it MORE dangerous"*. Under
determinism K06 would have reproduced forever and been read as a stable finding.

**Consequence, registered now so it cannot be chosen after seeing results:** K06
is not counted as a capability difference in this experiment or in any future
statement about q8_0, and E85's headline becomes *at most* 1 of 24, pending this
run.

### The design - vary the ONE thing F59 permits to be informative

F59 says reps inform only when something in the config moves. E82 established
that in a shared llama-server slot, **item order and preceding traffic change
answers deterministically** - and bounded that effect at 2 items on a 24-item
bank, which is exactly the size of the E85 effect. So the live question is not
*"is it deterministic"* (it is) but *"is it bigger than the bank's own position
sensitivity"*.

K05 sits 5th of 11 in the s pack. `--items` selects ids and preserves corpus
order, so preceding traffic can be varied by removing items ahead of it.

| condition | invocation | K05's position | preceding traffic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1** | `--pack s` (all 11) | 5th | K01-K04 |
| **C2** | `--items K05` | 1st | none |
| **C3** | `--items K01,K02,K05` | 3rd | K01, K02 |

Both arms run all three. **`-c 98304` is held fixed across every arm and
condition** (E84's lesson), so KV type and position are the only variables, and
they are varied one at a time.

**Arms.** Relay binary `/opt/sparkrouter/llama.cpp/build/bin/llama-server`,
build 9865, served model `Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507-Q4_K_M.gguf`, second
server on port 8139. The relay is never touched.

| arm | flags |
|---|---|
| **P0** | `-c 98304 --jinja` |
| **P1** | `-c 98304 -fa on -ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0 --jinja` |

**Corpus pinned at `98dad04` in a worktree, and the pin is LOAD-BEARING.** The
s pack at HEAD is **56,223 chars**; at the pin it is **55,665**, matching E85's
recorded `pack_chars` exactly. Two of the four s-pack documents have changed
since. Running at HEAD would have moved the corpus and the position together -
F59's own scar, where a rewritten input was attributed to the only variable
anyone had noticed.

### AMENDMENT 1 (declared before the affected re-run, 2026-08-29)

**The registration named a pinned corpus; the METHOD I used did not implement
it.** First run is VOID and prediction 1 correctly caught it.

`tools/run_kw_eval.py:41` does
`sys.path.insert(0, Path(__file__).resolve().parent.parent / "spikes" / "kw-eval")`.
Position 0 precedes `PYTHONPATH`, so HEAD's runner always resolves HEAD's corpus
no matter what the environment says. The run built the **HEAD** s pack at
**56,223 chars** instead of the pinned **55,665**, and only 2 of 11 answers
matched E85.

**The pre-run check that "verified" the pin was itself invalid, and this is the
transferable part.** It ran `PYTHONPATH=... python3 -` with a bare
`import kw_corpus` - a script with no `sys.path.insert` of its own. That path
resolved the pin correctly and proved nothing, because **the experiment never
takes it**. `development-discipline.md` says to suspect the ruler before the
data; here the ruler was measured on a different instrument than the one under
test. A corpus pin must be verified through *the same entry point the run uses*.

**Fix:** HEAD's `run_kw_eval.py` is copied INTO the worktree and executed from
there, so `Path(__file__).parent.parent` resolves inside the pin. Verified
through that entry point: **55,665**, matching E85's recorded `pack_chars`.
This is evidently what E85 itself did - its meta carries `item_packs`, a HEAD-only
field, alongside the pinned char count.

**`spikes/kw-eval/kw_items.py` has 0 commits since `98dad04`**, so questions,
keys and `is_unanswerable` flags are byte-identical between pin and HEAD, and
scoring the runs with HEAD's `score_kw_eval` is like-for-like.

**The void run is RETAINED** as `results/raw/e86-void-headcorpus-{p0,p1}-{c1,c2,c3}.json`
rather than deleted. Its two arms are internally like-for-like against each other
at the HEAD corpus, so it may later serve as an incidental second-corpus
replication. **No claim is made from it here.**

**Predictions and the decision rule are UNCHANGED.**

### What E85's objection now amounts to

E85 stopped the q8_0 re-seed on *"2 of 24 verdicts differ"*. That resolves to:

1. **K06 - withdrawn.** A defective item. Both arms answered correctly; the key
   accepts only one of two genuine corrections present in the pack.
2. **K05 - real, position-independent, and knife-edge.** It reproduces perfectly
   at one corpus state and is absent at another 1% away.

**So the correctness objection to q8_0 does not generalise.** It was never a
claim about q8_0 changing answers in production; it is a claim about one
abstention item at one corpus state, where f16 happens to sit on the wrong side
of a boundary that q8_0 happens to sit on the right side of. Both flips E85 saw
favoured q8_0, and the one that survives scrutiny still does - q8_0 declined
where f16 invented a statistic.

**The STOP is not lifted here.** Two things still stand between this and a
re-seed, and neither is a correctness question:

- **Throughput on build 9865 is still unmeasured.** The +8.35% / +14.82% figures
  are build-770 numbers. Rule 9 voids every timing in E85 and E86 alike.
- **Re-seeding the live relay is the owner's decision**, and the active line must
  carry `-c 98304`, not the old default's `49152`.

### The transferable lesson, and it is about the ruler

The pre-registered perturbation - item position - was the *weaker* test. It held
3/3 and would have been reported as "the difference is real". The perturbation
that actually bounded the finding was one I introduced **by accident**, kept only
because the amendment retained a void run instead of deleting it.

**A corpus is a variable in every run that uses it** (F59 recorded exactly this
after a matter file was edited between runs). E86 adds the sharper version: on a
knife-edge item, a 1% corpus change is a **larger** perturbation than moving the
item from first to fifth in the conversation. When bounding a small difference,
vary the INPUT before varying the ORDER.
