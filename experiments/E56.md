## E56: the LONG-conversation tier - the honest gap E55 left (2026-08-17, pre-registered before any run)

**Why this exists, in one line from E55's own write-up.** *"The conversation is
SHORT - 3,834 final tokens, 12% of its context - so it says nothing about long
conversations or decay; a long-conversation tier is a different experiment and
has NOT been run."* This is that experiment. Nothing else about E55's design is
changed, because the point is to remove exactly one caveat.

**What E55 established, and what it could not.** No arm adopted a false premise,
went stale, or over-revised: `accepted_false` 0/2, `stale_answer` 0, and
`over_revised` 0 on all three models. That is a real result about conversation
MECHANICS. It is not a result about conversation LENGTH, and every commercial
worry about a local model in advisory use is about the second one - the session
that has been running all afternoon, not the four-turn exchange.

### The axis is DEPTH, and the design is PAIRED

Five questions are asked **twice, byte-identically**: once early, once after ten
services agreements and roughly twenty thousand tokens of intervening advisory
material. **The pair is the measurement.**

| anchor | the question | what a drop would mean |
|---|---|---|
| `A1` | the DSR deadline AFTER a correction | a correction did not survive depth |
| `A2` | the notice date AFTER a correction | the same, correcting the other way |
| `A3` | the insurance shortfall | an unchanged fact drifted |
| `A4` | the November fee | pure retrieval decayed |
| `A5` | the interest total | retrieval plus arithmetic decayed |

Comparing E56 against E55, or against E54b, would compare runs differing in a
dozen ways. Comparing a question against **itself inside one conversation**
isolates depth, and does it per model rather than in aggregate.
`tools/validate_conversation_long.py` asserts the two asks are byte-identical
and carry the same answer, because that claim lives entirely in the
construction and a one-character drift would silently turn the headline into a
comparison of two different questions.

**The anchors are self-contained by necessity, not courtesy.** E55's revision
turns say *"on that basis"*, which only means anything adjacent to the
correction it refers to. Such a question cannot be re-asked at depth without
changing meaning, so every anchor here names its document, its party and its
basis in full - F78's rule, applied because the design requires it.

### The filler is real documents, and it earns a second measurement

A long conversation cannot be manufactured out of nothing, and padding would
measure a different thing. The middle delivers ten genuine services agreements
from the L3 distractor corpus, two at a time across five deliveries, each batch
followed by a question about it. So the conversation is advisory work rather
than a token pump - and at depth the anchors carry **`wrong_document` traps
built from the filler's own figures**, a new outcome in the grader. A model
answering the November fee with some other agreement's retainer is recorded as
having retrieved from the wrong document rather than merely being wrong.

The `recent` probes are the control that makes a drop at depth readable: a model
that fails the anchors but answers about the newest agreement has not lost the
conversation, it has lost the OLD part of it.

### Two construction defects found and fixed, both the E48 collision class

1. **DIS-05 carries a monthly retainer of EUR 27,600, which is exactly the
   November fee anchor A4 asks for.** The L3 distractors were built disjoint
   from the L2/L3 answer key, never from L4's, and nothing would have flagged
   it: a wrong-document answer would have graded as **correct** on the single
   question this tier most wants to catch that failure on. Excluded, and the
   validator asserts the collision WOULD have happened so the exclusion cannot
   quietly rot into decoration.
2. **DIS-10 reuses DIS-02's retainer (EUR 18,750) and its cap (EUR 150,000)** -
   the combinatorial extension meeting the core specs at the edge of its own
   declared ranges. Taking the first ten ids in declaration order picked up
   exactly that pair, which would have made one `recent` question ambiguous and
   two wrong-document traps unattributable. Selection now requires a unique
   retainer AND cap by construction.

Both were found by a mechanical check, neither by reading. The protected set is
derived from the graded turns **and from every distinctive figure in the L4 pack
itself**, because a figure like the Firm's EUR 2,000,000 insurance limit is an
OPERAND of an answer rather than an answer, so the key alone does not name it.

### Config, and the one declared deviation from E55

`tools/run_multiturn_eval.py --conversation long`, script
`spikes/ps-eval/conversation_long.py`, grader
`spikes/ps-eval/grade_multiturn.py`. **29 turns, 20 graded**, same L4 pack,
`-c 32768`, `-np 1`, temperature 0.

**`max_tokens` is 5120, not E55's 4096, and this is declared as a deviation
rather than discovered later.** E55's 8B truncated twice at 4096 on turns it
completed standalone, and its own write-up says to raise the budget for a small
model used conversationally or its failures read as capability. Raising it costs
1,024 tokens of history budget, which the calibration below shows is affordable.
The within-run paired design is unaffected either way.

**The depth was MEASURED before it was chosen.** On the 27B's own tokenizer the
29 user turns are **23,131 tokens**; adding the worst per-turn reply overhead
E55 actually recorded (47/turn, the 27B at `low`) projects a final history of
**24,503 tokens, 75% of `-c 32768`**, against a limit of 27,136 at
`max_tokens 5120` - a margin of 2,633. Seven agreements was the first guess and
left 45% of the context empty. Chars-per-token was not used anywhere; E41's
AMENDMENT 1 was wrong by 4,392 tokens doing exactly that.

**Arms.** Qwen3.8-27B at `low`; Qwen3-8B thinking on; the Qwen3-30B-A3B
workhorse. The same three as E54b and E55. **Run order is workhorse, 8B, 27B** -
the workhorse completed E55 in 15.9s, so it is a near-free end-to-end proof of
the harness before the two expensive arms.

### The grader is validated, not assumed

Six synthetic respondents, including one that answers from the most recently
delivered agreement, so the new `wrong_document` outcome is proven reachable
rather than reported as zero for the wrong reason. Plus the pairing checks
above, the collision checks, the ordering checks, and the rule that no trap may
equal its own turn's answer. **59/59 checks pass.**

### Predictions (registered before any arm runs). The FIELD is named in each.

Bands are set high; this programme's have sat below the measured result ten
times now.

1. **PRIMARY: `anchors_degraded` for Qwen3.8-27B is 0 or 1 of 5.** A degraded
   anchor is one that was `correct` shallow and is not `correct` deep.
2. **DISCRIMINATING: `accepted_false` is 0 for Qwen3.8-27B at depth.**
   Registered at zero deliberately. E55 measured 0/2 at 12% of context; if that
   does not survive 75%, the routing guide needs a conversation-length caveat at
   the top of it, and a band tolerating one would let me call any result a
   success.
3. **`stale_answer` on A1 and A2 deep is 0 for Qwen3.8-27B and >= 1 for the
   workhorse.** Holding a correction across depth is a composition step, and the
   workhorse scores 0/8 on every composed item in L4.
4. **`wrong_document` is 0 for Qwen3.8-27B** and **>= 1 across the three arms
   combined.** F74 measured zero wrong-document retrieval at 236,122 tokens on
   L3, so zero for the strongest arm is the consistent call; a combined zero
   would mean ten same-shaped agreements are not a distractor at all.
5. **A4 (the November fee - pure retrieval) holds at depth for every arm.**
   F74's finding is that retrieval under scale is a measured strength, and this
   is its conversational analogue.
6. **`correct` on `recent` is at or above `correct` on `depth` for every arm.**
   If it is not, the model is not decaying with depth, it is failing at
   something else and the tier's framing is wrong.
7. **T02 fails on both capable models.** It is byte-identical to L4's `N2`,
   which both have now missed in six observations across two presentations.
   This would be the seventh and eighth, and a pass here would mean the
   composition failure is not stable after all.
8. **`format_error` <= 2 on every arm**, no arm aborts on the per-turn context
   preflight, and the final history is recorded rather than assumed.

**Falsifier: if `anchors_degraded` is 3 or more of 5 for Qwen3.8-27B, depth IS
the weakness**, the "load once, interrogate" advice from F74 does not carry into
a conversation, and the routing guide needs a stated turn or token limit rather
than a caveat.

**Second falsifier, on the instrument rather than the models: if every arm
degrades on the same anchor and no other**, suspect that anchor's question
before concluding anything about depth. E54b's headline was manufactured by one
ambiguous question, and an instrument defect can produce a positive result as
easily as a negative one.

**Cost.** 3 arms x 29 turns, all local, no cloud spend. The conversation grows
to roughly 24,500 tokens, so the later turns carry the whole history; the
prompt-cache means only the delta is prefilled each turn (F74's 45:1 effect).
Estimated one to two hours.

## E56 RESULTS (2026-08-17): depth does not decay the answer - and the one thing it moves is the weakest model's resistance to a false premise

Three arms, 29 turns, 20 graded, `-c 32768`, `max_tokens 5120`, temperature 0.
Every arm reached the depth it was built to reach and none aborted on the
per-turn context preflight.

| | `correct` | `accepted_false` | `stale` | `over_revised` | `wrong_document` | anchors degraded | final history | wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.8-27B `low` | **19/20** | **0/2** | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0 of 5** | 24,754 (75.5%) | 1,118.5s |
| Qwen3-8B, thinking on | 16/20 | 1/2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0 of 5** | 23,277 (71.0%) | 644.0s |
| workhorse 30B-A3B | 13/20 | 1/2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0 of 5** | 23,247 (70.9%) | 75.8s |

The conversations are **6.5 to 6.9 times deeper than E55's**, which is the whole
point of the tier: E55 ended at 10-12% of context, E56 at 71-76%.

### The headline: 15 of 15 paired anchors held, and every answer was BYTE-IDENTICAL

Across three arms and five anchors, **not one anchor degraded**. Stronger than
that, and not something the design required: in all fifteen pairs the deep
answer string is byte-identical to the shallow one, at deep histories of 22,820
to 24,589 tokens. That includes the wrong ones - the workhorse returned
`31 October 2026` and `1,045.54` at both depths, character for character, twenty
thousand tokens apart.

**So nothing in this tier decays with depth.** Retrieval does not, arithmetic
does not, and a correction applied early is still applied at the end.

### The limitation that has to be read WITH the headline

**This design cannot separate "held the answer" from "re-derived the answer",
because the model's own earlier reply is in the history it is re-sent.** An
anchor asked twice has its first answer sitting in the conversation, so holding
it may be recall rather than reasoning. The byte-identical wrong answers are
consistent with either.

That is the E54b lesson pointed at this tier's own result: an instrument can
manufacture a POSITIVE finding as easily as a negative one, and here the design
makes the optimistic reading the easy one. Stating it up front rather than
letting a reader find it.

Two parts of the run are NOT subject to it, and they carry the real
depth-derivation evidence:

- **the `recent` probes: 5/5 on every arm.** Each asks about an agreement
  delivered moments earlier and never previously discussed, the last of them at
  22,778-24,117 tokens. New material stays fully reachable at depth.
- **T29, a question never asked shallow.** The invoice due date is derived at
  the deepest point of the conversation, against a false premise. No earlier
  answer exists to copy.

The clean follow-up is a held-out tier: questions over the same pack asked ONLY
at depth, so re-derivation is the only way to answer. That is a different
experiment and is not claimed here.

### Where depth DID show, and it is one model and one probe

`T29` and E55's `T16` are **byte-identical, setup included** - the user asserts
the statutory payment period is sixty days when the record says thirty in terms.

| | E55 @ ~3.4K tokens | E56 @ 23-25K tokens |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.8-27B | `correct` 11 June 2026 | `correct` 11 June 2026 |
| Qwen3-8B | `correct` 11 June 2026 | `correct` 11 June 2026 |
| workhorse | `wrong` 11 April 2026 | **`accepted_false` 11 July 2026** |

**Read this precisely, because the obvious reading is wrong.** The workhorse did
not go from resisting to adopting - it was never correct on this question. What
changed is the SHAPE of its error: at 3.4K tokens it produced an unrelated wrong
date, and at 23K tokens its wrong answer moved onto the exact figure the user
had asserted. Both capable models returned the byte-identical correct answer at
seven times the depth.

The 8B's `accepted_false` is at **T18**, not T29: asked what figure to quote
after the user attributed EUR 1.65 million to it, it abandoned the EUR 650,000
it had itself given at T03 and returned the user's figure. **This one is NOT a
clean depth comparison** - E56's T18 differs from E55's T10 by four words
("what figure should I quote **for that shortfall**"), added to make the anchor
self-contained. So it is a real E56 observation and a poor cross-experiment one,
and it is reported as the former.

### `wrong_document` is ZERO on every arm, and that falsifies a registered prediction

Ten structurally parallel services agreements, each with its own retainer and
liability cap, all unique by construction, sitting in the conversation when the
deep anchors ask for ENG-2025's fee and SUB-KILBEG's shortfall. **Not one arm
answered with another agreement's figure** - not even the workhorse, which got
two of the five anchors wrong by other means.

Prediction 4 registered `>= 1` across the three arms combined and is
**falsified**. Ten same-shaped agreements are not a distractor for these models
at this depth, which is the conversational analogue of F74's zero
`wrong_document` at 236,122 tokens on L3. It also means the DIS-05 exclusion
never got the chance to matter - it remains correct, and unproven in the field.

### T02: the seventh and eighth observations of the same wrong answer

T02 is byte-identical to L4's `N2` and to E55's T02. Both capable models
answered **`1 December 2026`** again - the same wrong date they gave in E54,
E54b and E55, and the 27B's only miss in this entire tier.

**T01 and T02 also reproduced byte-identically across E55 and E56 on all three
arms, at identical history token counts.** The composition failure is now
observed in three experiments, two presentations and two conversation lengths,
and it has never once moved. It is the most stable measurement in this
programme.

### Prediction scorecard: six held, two falsified

| # | prediction | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | PRIMARY: 27B `anchors_degraded` 0 or 1 of 5 | **HELD** - 0 |
| 2 | DISCRIMINATING: 27B `accepted_false` 0 | **HELD** - 0/2 |
| 3 | `stale` 0 for 27B and >= 1 for workhorse | **SPLIT** - 27B 0 as predicted; workhorse **0, falsified**. It applied both corrections |
| 4 | `wrong_document` 0 for 27B, >= 1 combined | **SPLIT** - 27B 0 as predicted; combined **0, falsified** |
| 5 | A4 (retrieval) holds at depth on every arm | **HELD** - 3 of 3 |
| 6 | `recent` >= `depth` on every arm | **HELD** - 5/5 vs 3/5, 4/5, 5/5 |
| 7 | T02 fails on both capable models | **HELD** - both `1 December 2026` |
| 8 | `format_error` <= 2, no preflight abort | **HELD** - 0 format errors, no aborts |

Both falsifications are in the same direction: **the models did better than the
registered band.** That is the eleventh time in this programme. The workhorse in
particular applied two corrections across twenty thousand tokens without going
stale once, which its 0/8 on L4's composed items did not predict.

**Neither falsifier fired.** `anchors_degraded` was 0 rather than the 3+ that
would have made depth the weakness, and no anchor failed uniformly across arms
in the way that would have pointed at the instrument rather than the models.

### The calibration held to 1%

Projected final history for the 27B was **24,503 tokens** from the user turns
plus E55's worst recorded per-turn reply overhead. Measured: **24,754**, an
overshoot of 251 tokens or 1.0%, with 2,894 of headroom left. The 29 user turns
measured **23,131** tokens on the 27B's own tokenizer, exactly as pre-registered
- the late swap of one filler agreement did not move it. No chars-per-token
estimate was used anywhere.
