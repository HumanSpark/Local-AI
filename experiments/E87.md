## E87 PRE-REGISTRATION - q8_0 THROUGHPUT on the relay's own build, in a real bench window, with flash attention separated out (2026-08-29)

**Registered before any arm runs.** This closes the last technical gap in front
of the q8_0 re-seed. E86 settled correctness; the outstanding claim is the
**+8.35% / +14.82% decode gain**, which is a **build-770** measurement at
`-c 32768` / `-c 65536`. Rule 9 voided every timing in E85 and E86 because the
relay was serving. **This run takes the window**, so timings are valid.

### The confound E77 could not separate, and this run can

Quantising V **forces flash attention on**. So E77's "q8_0 vs f16" compares
`q8_0 + fa` against `f16 + no fa` - two changes at once. If most of the decode
gain is flash attention, `-fa on` is available **without** touching KV, at no
correctness risk at all. A third arm settles it.

| arm | flags (`-c 98304` fixed on all three) | isolates |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | `-c 98304 --jinja` | production **exactly as it runs now** |
| **B** | `-c 98304 -fa on --jinja` | **flash attention alone**, f16 KV |
| **C** | `-c 98304 -fa on -ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0 --jinja` | the shipped change |

B minus A = flash attention. C minus B = KV quantisation. C minus A = what the
re-seed actually buys.

**Binary** `/opt/sparkrouter/llama.cpp/build/bin/llama-server` (build 9865),
**model** the served `Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507-Q4_K_M.gguf`, **corpus pinned
at `98dad04`** and verified through the run entry point (s=55,665, m=140,526) -
amendment 1 of E86 applies, the runner is executed from inside the worktree
because `PYTHONPATH` cannot pin it.

**Packs s and m, 3 repetitions each.** F15 puts single-run noise at +/-1.5%, and
a single run cannot distinguish an 8% gain from a warm cache. **Median of 3 is
the reported figure**; the spread is reported with it.

### The window is REAL and the relay goes down

Run via `sudo bash tools/relay_bench_window.sh kvquant`, which sets sparkrouter's
advisory maintenance flag, stops `llama-gateway.service`, runs the bench
unprivileged as `bench`, and **restores the relay on ANY exit** through an
EXIT/INT/TERM trap. The gateway reports 503 maintenance with a reason rather
than looking like an unexplained outage.

## E87 RESULT - the shipped gain is REAL and it is ALL KV quantisation; flash attention buys nothing; and within-process reps are not independent (2026-08-29)

**Runs:** `results/raw/e87-{A,B,C}-{s,m}-r{1,2,3}.json`, 18 runs, all rc=0.
Relay binary build 9865, served model, `-c 98304` fixed, corpus pinned at
`98dad04`. **Taken in a real maintenance window** (`relay_bench_window.sh
kvquant`) - gateway stopped, advisory flag set, GPU quiet. **Rule 9 does not
apply: these timings are VALID.** Relay restored by the script's trap; window
closed.

### Decode, tok/s (per item, `completion_tokens / (wall_s - ttft_s)`, 3 reps pooled)

| arm | config | pack s | pack m |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | f16, production as it runs now | 55.04 | 35.16 |
| **B** | f16 + `-fa on` | 54.97 | 35.17 |
| **C** | q8_0 + `-fa on` (shipped) | **59.38** | **40.83** |

### Which change buys it - the question E77 could not ask

| pack | B vs A (flash attention) | C vs B (KV quantisation) | C vs A (shipped) |
|---|---|---|---|
| s | **-0.14%** | **+8.02%** | **+7.87%** |
| m | **+0.04%** | **+16.08%** | **+16.13%** |

**Flash attention alone buys NOTHING.** Prediction 4 is FALSIFIED and the answer
is unambiguous: A and B are within 0.14% on both packs. **The entire gain is the
smaller KV cache**, which is a memory-bandwidth effect, not an attention-kernel
one. The cheaper option I registered a decision branch for **does not exist** -
`-fa on` with f16 KV gets you nothing, so the speed cannot be had without
quantising KV.

**The delta is stable across reps to under 1pp** (s: +7.50/+8.14/+7.36;
m: +16.28/+16.05/+15.96), and rep-1-only - the cold runs that reproduce E85
exactly - gives +7.50% / +16.28%. The answer-text divergence in reps 2-3
(below) did **not** contaminate the timing.

### Prediction 3's falsification is the nuanced one, and it cuts against E85

I predicted E77's figures would not reproduce, because build AND context both
differ. **They reproduced within noise on both packs.**

So the *same* build-770 extrapolation was **unsafe for correctness** (E85:
0 of 24 became 2 of 24) and **safe for throughput** (E87: reproduced inside
+/-1.5pp). **Extrapolation safety is a property of the AXIS, not of the
config.** E85's lesson was never "build 770 numbers are worthless" - it was
"you cannot know which axis travels without measuring". Both halves of that are
now evidenced.

### Prediction 1's falsification is a NEW finding - see F96

Rep 1 reproduces E85 byte-for-byte on both arms. Reps 2 and 3 do not, in the
same process, at the same config, at temperature 0.

**Byte-determinism holds across PROCESSES (F88) and for a COLD first run. It does
NOT hold for repeated runs inside one server process.** The intervening traffic -
the other pack, the previous rep - leaves cache state that changes later answers.
This is E82's order effect operating *across runs* rather than within one.

**Consequence for method:** reps inside one process are **not** independent
replicates for correctness. They remain valid for throughput, which is what this
experiment needed them for and why its conclusion survives. Recorded as **F96**.

The control's PURPOSE - proving the timed configs are the ones E85/E86
characterised - **is met by rep 1**, at 11/11 on both arms. The prediction is
still scored FALSIFIED as written rather than reinterpreted after the fact.
