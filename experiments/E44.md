## E44: what happens when the budget stops being the constraint? (2026-08-15, pre-registered before any run)

**Why this exists.** Owner instruction, after seeing that `run_matters.py`
would have killed the E43 long matters at 600s: *"I want to see what happens
when these tasks have huge token and time budgets available - what can they do
and how long do they really take?"* Proposed 90 min / 100k tokens and 180 min /
300k tokens on the toughest matters.

**Three measured facts reshaped the design before it was registered.**

1. **300,000 tokens is not reachable.** `n_ctx_train` for
   Qwen3.8-27B-Q4_K_M is **262,144**, and the prompt shares that budget.
   Arm B is registered at **200,000**, which fits with room.
2. **The CLOCK binds before the token ceiling in both arms.** Sustained
   generation measured on this model today is **12.02 tok/s** (E42 writing
   `medium`: 51,367 completion tokens over 4,273.1s of item wall). 90 min =
   5,400s admits ~64,900 tokens; 180 min = 10,800s admits ~129,800. Neither
   arm can reach its token ceiling, and generation slows further as the KV
   grows. **This is registered as a known property, not discovered later:**
   the arms differ in TIME, and the token numbers are headroom rather than
   the binding constraint.
3. **Memory is not a risk, and this was checked against the deadlock rule.**
   Qwen3.8 is a DeltaNet hybrid: llama-server reports KV for only **16 of 64
   layers** (2,048 MiB at 32,768 cells = 64 KiB/token), the other 48 holding a
   constant 149.62 MiB recurrent state. So a full 262,144 context is
   16,384 MiB of KV; with the 15,088 MiB model that is **~31 GiB total**,
   against 112 GiB free and the ~60 GiB threshold in
   docs/memory-edge-deadlock.md. Arm B at `-c 262144` stays under it.

**What the evidence predicts, stated before the run.** Across **402 graded
items on 2026-08-15, `finish_reason: length` occurred exactly 5 times** - all
five in E33 coding, all at exactly 8,192 of 8,192. Every other item returned
`stop`. The largest completion ever observed on this box is **13,156 tokens**
(E42 writing `medium`), and on knowledge work the maximum is **967** (E42
`xhigh`). **The budget has never bound on knowledge work.** So the honest
prior is that arms A and B will produce near-identical answers and the
experiment will show that budget was never the constraint on matters - which
is itself the answer to the owner's question.

**Arms.** All local, Qwen3.8-27B-Q4_K_M, b10435, `-np 1`, temperature 0,
`thinking=xhigh`. xhigh is chosen deliberately over E42's `low`
recommendation: it is the setting that SPENDS the most tokens, so it is the
setting most likely to use headroom. A budget probe run at the cheapest
setting would answer nothing.

| arm | items | max_tokens | request timeout | `-c` | KV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | m11, m12, m13 | 100,000 | 16,967s (derived) | 131,072 | 8,192 MiB |
| B | m11, m12, m13 | 200,000 | 33,633s (derived) | 262,144 | 16,384 MiB |
| C | the 5 capped coding tasks | 100,000 | 16,967s (derived) | 131,072 | 8,192 MiB |

**The 90/180-minute budgets are HEADROOM, not kill switches, and this is a
deliberate departure from the instruction as literally worded.** The owner
asked for a 90-minute and a 180-minute time budget. Implementing those as
request timeouts was wired up and then removed before any run, because **an
HTTP request timeout returns NOTHING** - not the answer, not the token count,
not the wall time. Killing a generation at 90 minutes would destroy exactly
the measurement the experiment exists to take, on precisely the items most
likely to be long. That is the E33-void failure mode (a fixed timeout under a
raised budget), and re-creating it here on purpose would be worse than
inheriting it by accident.

So the CAP is `max_tokens`, which returns `finish_reason: length` together
with the tokens and the answer, and the request timeout sits above it at the
derived floor. **Duration is therefore MEASURED, not bounded** - "how long do
they really take" is answered by reading `seconds` off each record rather than
by a clock that stops it. If a matter runs past 90 minutes that is a result,
and the monitor surfaces it while it is happening.

Arm C is `parse_semver`, `normalize_path`, `roman_roundtrip`, `word_break`,
`eval_expr` - the only items in this repo that have ever hit a cap. The owner
chose to add them: the matters answer his question, and arm C tests the one
place a budget demonstrably bit.

**Baseline for A and B** is E43 at `max_tokens 24,576` (queue 7, already
registered). **Baseline for C** is E33's capped run and the E33 RE-RUN 2 at
24,576 (queued).

**Predictions. The FIELD is named in each. Bands are set high deliberately -
my registered bands have sat BELOW the measured result in E37, E38, the E42
knowledge row and the E42 writing row, four in a row in the same direction.**

1. **`finish_reason` is `stop` for all 3 matters in BOTH arms A and B.** No
   matter reaches even 100,000 tokens. If any returns `length`, the budget was
   a real constraint on legal work and E43's 24,576 is suspect.
2. **Arm A and arm B produce the SAME score** on all 3 matters, +/- 1 point of
   45 (3 x 15). If they differ by more, generation is not deterministic at
   these lengths and F59 does not extend to 100k-token generations - which
   would be a finding about the box, not the model.
3. **`completion_tokens` for the hardest matter (m11 or m13) is 2,000-25,000**
   in arm A. The registered band is wide because nothing on this corpus has
   been measured at any budget above 2,000.
4. **Total score across m11+m12+m13 in arm A is 18-38 of 45.** F60 measured
   m11 at 6/15 and m13 at 7/15 for OTHER models at `max_tokens 2000`; this is
   a different model with 50x the budget.
5. **Arm C: at least 3 of the 5 capped tasks return `stop` instead of
   `length`,** and at least 2 that previously scored 0 now pass. If all 5
   still hit `length` at 100,000 the model is looping, not thinking, and that
   is a much more interesting result than a score.
6. **Arm C's per-task wall exceeds 600s for at least one task.** These are the
   tasks that spent 8,192 tokens without finishing.

**Falsifier: if arm B differs from arm A on any matter, the whole
"budget was never the constraint" reading collapses** and E43's 24,576 has to
be re-examined before its results are used for the routing verdict.

**Cost, and the runaway guard.** With the caps on `max_tokens` rather than the
clock, the theoretical worst case is large: arm A 3 x 4.7h, arm B 3 x 9.3h,
arm C 5 x 4.7h. **That worst case requires the model to generate 100,000-200,000
tokens per item, which prediction 1 says it will not do** - the largest
completion ever observed on this box is 13,156 tokens, about 18 minutes. The
expected runtime is therefore ~20 min/item, and the theoretical ceiling exists
only because the cap was moved off the clock to preserve data.

The guard is observation, not a timeout: the queue log is monitored per item,
and any item still running well past 90 minutes will be visible while it
happens and can be killed by hand. A run killed that way loses one item's data
and is recorded as such; a run killed by a request timeout loses it silently,
which is the distinction the whole design turns on. Runs after queue 7.

**CORRECTION TO E41 AMENDMENT 1: my overflow claim was WRONG, and the run
proves it.**

AMENDMENT 1 changed `max_tokens` 8192 -> 4096 and justified it by arguing that
n=9 at 8,192 would need 33,801 tokens against a 32,768 context - an overflow
of 1,033 on the one arm that matters. **The measured prompt is 21,217 tokens,

21,217 + 8,192 = **29,409 of 32,768 - it FITS, with 3,359 spare.** There was
no overflow to catch.

The error is a ruler error of exactly the kind flagged one message earlier and
then repeated: I measured **3.67 chars/token on the L2 pack** and applied it
to a pack that is mostly distractor text, which tokenizes at **4.43
chars/token**. Distractors are boilerplate services-agreement prose and
compress better than the dense base documents. The estimate ran **20.6% high**,
and it ran high in the direction that manufactures an alarm.

| n | chars | tokens | chars/token |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 13,221 | 3,625 | 3.647 |
| 4 | 49,076 | 11,441 | 4.289 |
| 9 | 93,920 | 21,217 | 4.427 |

**What the amendment got right, and why it still stands:** the queue passed

Recorded because a justification that sounded rigorous and was arithmetically

one already written into `development-discipline.md`: a ratio measured on one
corpus is not a ruler for a different corpus, and "measured" is not the same
