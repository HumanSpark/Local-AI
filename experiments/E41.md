## E41: the scale sweep - does the answer survive being buried? (2026-08-15, pre-registered before any run)

**Why this exists.** L1 (20/20) and L2 (12/12 on the unquantised arm) are both
about three pages. Real professional work arrives as a folder: the master
agreement, its amendments, and a dozen other contracts the same client has
signed with other suppliers, every one of which has its own liability cap,
notice period and retention rule. Nothing measured so far tells the owner
whether this box can work at that size, which is the shape of the question
actually asked.

**ONE VARIABLE CHANGES: how much text the answer is buried in.** The twelve
questions, the reasoning they demand and the correct answers are IDENTICAL to
L2 and are derived from `QUESTIONS_L2` in code rather than retyped, so they
cannot drift. `tools/validate_ps_eval_l3.py` asserts that equality
mechanically - a tier that made the questions harder AND the pack longer could
not attribute a drop to either, and attribution is the whole point.

**The distractors are deliberately confusable.** Nine additional services
agreements between the same Client and DIFFERENT suppliers, structurally
parallel to the real one, each with its own retainer, cap, notice period,
retention rule and response time. Making them topic-free - a lease, a pension
summary - would test nothing, because no model confuses a pension scheme with
a services agreement. The question being asked is the one a firm actually gets
wrong: given ten agreements that all have a liability cap, can you report the
cap from the RIGHT one?

**`wrong_document` is a new outcome and the signature failure of this tier.**
Distractor figures are drawn from ranges disjoint from the real pack, asserted
at import: retainers 18,750-37,400 against the real 42,000-51,000; caps
150,000-415,000 and 2.1-3.4M against 500,000 and 1.25M; retention 3/9/18/36/48
against 6/12/24. An answer of 275,000 is therefore not merely wrong - it is
the Harbourgate agreement's cap, and it says the model retrieved fluently from
the wrong contract. Folding that into a generic `wrong` would hide the one
thing this tier measures.

**Every question names its agreement**, asserted by the validator. In a pack
of ten services agreements an unscoped question has no single right answer.
This is E38 AMENDMENT 1's lesson applied before the fact rather than after.

**The real documents are NOT placed first.** The pack interleaves distractors
around them, putting the master agreement roughly a quarter of the way in and
scattering the amendments after it. A pack whose answer always sits at the top
would measure reading order, and "models read the beginning of the context" is
a known result needing no new experiment.

**Config.** `tools/run_ps_eval.py --tier l3 --distractors N`, llama-server
b10435, `-c 32768`, `-np 1`, temperature 0, `max_tokens 4096`, request timeout
derived. Sweep points, measured:

| n | chars | approx tokens |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 13,221 | 3,305 (identical to the L2 pack) |
| 4 | 49,076 | 12,269 |
| 9 | 93,920 | 23,480 |

**Arms.** Qwen3.8-27B-Q4_K_M at n=0, 4, 9. Qwen3-30B-A3B-2507 at n=0 and 9 -
the control needs only the endpoints to show whether any degradation is a
property of the box or of the subject.

**Predictions (registered before any arm runs). The FIELD is named in each.**

1. **`correct` at n=0 is 12/12 for Qwen3.8 and reproduces the E38 local
   result exactly.** This is a harness check, not a model test: n=0 IS the L2
   pack, byte-identical, and a different score means the sweep harness is
   broken rather than that anything was learned.
2. **`correct` at n=9 is at least 2 lower than at n=0** for Qwen3.8. Bands:
   n=4 **10-12**, n=9 **7-11**.
3. **`wrong_document` is at least 1 at n=9** for Qwen3.8. This is the sharp
   prediction: degradation under scale should show up as confident retrieval
   from the wrong contract, not as declining to answer.
4. **The `multihop` category degrades FIRST.** M1, M2 and M3 each need two or
   three facts from different documents, so each retrieval is another chance
   to land in the wrong agreement. Registered: `multihop` at n=9 is **0-2 of
   3**, and is the lowest-scoring category.
5. **The workhorse control degrades MORE than Qwen3.8 between n=0 and n=9**,
   measured as the drop in `correct`. It already failed L1 computation and
   conflict; a longer pack should widen the gap rather than narrow it.

**Not predicted:** which individual questions fail.

**Falsifier for the whole experiment: if `correct` at n=9 equals `correct` at
n=0, scale to ~23.5K tokens is not a boundary either.** That would be the
third instrument this model has exhausted in one day, and the honest report is
that the boundary has not been found rather than that there isn't one - the
next probe would have to go further out, and I would say plainly that three
successive designs failed to locate it.

### E41 AMENDMENT 1 (declared 2026-08-15 12:37, BEFORE any E41 arm runs)

Two changes: a correction to the queue, and a setting the registration failed
to name.

**1. `max_tokens` is 4,096 - the registered value - and the queue that would
have run it at 8,192 has been replaced.** The queue script passed
`--max-tokens 8192`, which does not fit at n=9:

| n | pack chars | prompt tokens | +4,096 | +8,192 | ctx |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 13,221 | 3,605 | 7,701 | 11,797 | 32,768 |
| 4 | 49,076 | ~13,382 | 17,478 | 21,574 | 32,768 |
| 9 | 93,920 | ~25,609 | 29,705 | **33,801** | 32,768 |

n=9 at 8,192 overflows by 1,033 tokens. **The arm that would have broken is
the arm the experiment exists to measure**, and the breakage would have
presented as degraded `correct` and raised `wrong_document` at n=9 - which is
prediction 2 and prediction 3. A context overflow would have been read as the
finding.

Prompt tokens are derived from the MEASURED ratio, not the chars/4 heuristic:
the L2 pack is 13,221 chars and llama-server reported 3,605 prompt tokens for
it across four E42 cells, so **3.67 chars/token** on this corpus. The
registration's own "approx tokens" column used chars/4 and understated every
row by about 9%; it is left as written and corrected here rather than edited
in place. At 4,096 the largest n=9 request needs 29,705 of 32,768, and the
largest completion observed anywhere on this corpus is 967 tokens (E42
xhigh), so 4,096 is a 4x margin rather than a tight fit.

**2. Qwen3.8's arms run at `thinking=low`; the workhorse keeps the harness
default.** The registration named `-c`, `-np`, temperature and `max_tokens`
but not the reasoning level, which F65 says is not a complete config. Left
unnamed it would have inherited the template's `xhigh`. `low` is chosen
because E42 measured `low` and `xhigh` as identical on this corpus (12/12,
same category split) with `low` 18% cheaper in tokens, so `xhigh` would spend
the budget without changing the score.

This changes prediction 1's anchor, and the change is stated rather than
absorbed: n=0 should reproduce **`e42-know-low` (12/12)**, not the E38
default-effort run. Both are 12/12, so the band is unaffected; the comparable
file is not.

The workhorse is not a reasoning model and has no equivalent setting, so the
two arms are NOT matched on effort. That is acceptable here because the
sweep's claim is about each model against ITSELF across n - prediction 5
compares the two DROPS, not the two scores - but it is stated so no later
reader treats a cross-model score at fixed n as effort-matched.

**Method note.** Queue 6 was running when this was found. A bash script is
read incrementally, so editing one in flight can execute garbage; the parent
shell was killed instead, and the in-flight python child - 52 minutes into the
E42 writing `medium` cell - was left running and waited on by the replacement
queue. Nothing was re-measured and nothing was lost.

### E38 RESULT - the falsifier FIRED. L2 is saturated.

All arms: llama-server b10435, `-c 16384`, `-np 1`, temperature 0,
`max_tokens 4096`, full-offload gate passing. Cloud arm on the instrument as
amended by AMENDMENT 1.

| field | Qwen3.8 Q4_K_M (local) | Qwen3.8 unquantised (cloud) | workhorse (control) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `correct` /12 | **12** | **12** | 5 |
| `citation_valid` /12 | 11 | 11 | 6 |
| date_effective /3 | 3 | 3 | 2 |
| revocation /2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| precedence /2 | 2 | 2 | **0** |
| multihop /3 | 3 | 3 | **0** |
| exhaustive /1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

| `over_claim_rate` | 0.00 | 0.00 | **1.00** |

**THE WHOLE-TIER FALSIFIER FIRED.** The registration said: *"if Qwen3.8 scores
11 or 12 of 12, L2 has ALSO saturated"*, and the ruling filed before this run
resolved it against the local Q4_K_M arm. It scored 12. **L2 is saturated and
no ceiling claim may be made from it.** That is the second instrument built
and exhausted in one day.

**Prediction resolution.**

1. **SPLIT.** Qwen3.8 registered **6-10**, measured **12** - falsified above
   the band. The workhorse band **2-6** measured **5** - held. The cloud band
   **7-11** measured **12** - falsified above the band.
2. **FALSIFIED.** `premature >= 1` was registered for Qwen3.8 and measured
   **0**. The registration named what that would mean: *"the model is reading
   effective dates and not pattern-matching, which is the single most
   impressive thing it could do on this tier"*. It did that. The control
   produced the `premature` instead, answering D2 with 1,250,000 - applying
   Amendment 2 three months before its own date.
3. **HELD.** `over_applied == 1` on P2 was registered for at least one local
   arm. The workhorse answered 1 business day, carrying the precedence rule
   past the scope the side letter draws. Qwen3.8 answered 2.
4. **HELD.** Z1 graded `correct` for Qwen3.8. The workhorse answered "No" -
   a confident fabrication on a question the pack does not address, giving it
   an `over_claim_rate` of **1.00** against Qwen3.8's 0.00.
5. **HELD.** The gap was registered at **>= 3** and measured **7**.

**Quantisation still costs nothing.** Local Q4_K_M and the unquantised cloud
arm both scored 12/12 with identical category splits and the same single
citation slip (D3 cited as "AMD-3, clause 1" rather than paragraph 2). That
now holds at both L1 and L2.

**The control's failures are the tier working, not the tier being unfair.**
Every one of the workhorse's seven misses landed on a NAMED diagnostic
outcome rather than a generic wrong: `premature` on the effective-date trap,
`precedence_missed` where the rule should have been applied, `over_applied`
where it should not, `over_claim` on the underspecified item. A pass/fail eval
would have reported 5/12 and said nothing about why.

**The workhorse is not slow, it is not thinking.** 13.6s for twelve questions
against Qwen3.8's 617.1s - 45x. The run is genuine: every question sent the
full ~3,450-token pack, the first took 4.00s and the rest 0.42-0.56s on a
cached prefix, and answers were 11-29 completion tokens with **zero reasoning
tokens**. Qwen3.8 spends hundreds of reasoning tokens per answer and gets
multihop 3/3; the workhorse commits immediately and gets 0/3. That is the
trade, measured: **45x the wall time for +7 of 12, and the +7 is concentrated
in exactly the reasoning the incumbent cannot do at all.**

**Three predictions held and two were falsified, and both falsifications were
in the same direction - I predicted this model would be worse than it is.**
That is now the third experiment in a row where my registered bands sat below
the measured result (E37 four of five, E38 two of two resolvable). The bands
are not being set from evidence; they are being set from a prior about
27B-class local models that this model keeps refuting. Recorded because a
systematic bias in one direction is a fact about the experimenter, and the
next registration has to correct for it explicitly rather than promise to.
