## E85 PRE-REGISTRATION - does q8_0 change the RELAY's answers, on the RELAY's build, at the RELAY's context? (2026-08-28, evening)

**Registered before any arm runs.** This verifies a change that has already been
made to `serving.conf.default` (`3f10a3b`, sparkrouter) on the owner's decision.
The active runtime config is unchanged and still needs an operator.

### Why this exists

E77 measured q8_0 on **sparkbench build 770 (`9e40df63b`)** at `-c 32768` and
`-c 65536`. The relay runs **build 9865 (`067de9371`)** at `-c 98304`. Three
things differ between the evidence and the deployment, and this repository
already knows b9865 behaves differently in at least one KV respect - CLAUDE.md
records `kv_unified = 'true'` there and `'false'` on the bench build.

Shipping on an extrapolation and calling it measured is the failure this lab
names *context substitution*: a claim proved in a reconstructed context and
asserted of the real one. **This closes that gap for the half that matters.**

### Rule 9 is breached DELIBERATELY, and only correctness is read

The relay is **up and serving**. Every timing in this run is therefore **void**,
and none is reported. That is not a compromise: tonight's E77 addendum
prediction 1 tested exactly this premise and **held** - all eight arms reproduced
their correctness exactly against a run taken under contention. A contended GPU
changes the clock, not the answer.

**Throughput on build 9865 is NOT measured here** and remains an extrapolation
until a bench window is taken. Said plainly rather than left implied.

### Arms - `-c 98304` held FIXED, per E84's lesson

E84 established that changing `-c` alone rewrites answers, so `-c` is constant
across every arm here and the KV type is the only variable.

| arm | flags | what it represents |
|---|---|---|
| **P0** | `-c 98304 --jinja` (no `-ctk`/`-ctv`, no `-fa`) | production **as it is running right now** |
| **P1** | `-c 98304 -fa on -ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0 --jinja` | production **as `3f10a3b` would ship it** |

Both packs, s (11 items) and m (13 items) - the same 24-item bank E77 used.
Binary is the relay's own `/opt/sparkrouter/llama.cpp/build/bin/llama-server`,
model is the served `Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507-Q4_K_M.gguf`, corpus pinned at
`98dad04`. A second server on a spare port; the relay is never touched.

### Predictions, each naming the FIELD it is scored against

| # | prediction | scored against | falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | q8_0 changes no verdict on the production build | per-item `verdict`, P0 vs P1, both packs | any item whose verdict differs |
| 2 | the flag is actually in force - not silently ignored | the server's `llama_kv_cache: size` startup line for P1 | anything other than `K (q8_0)` / `V (q8_0)` |
| 3 | it rewrites text without changing verdicts, as on build 770 | answers byte-identical P0 vs P1 | **0 rewritten** - which would mean the flag did nothing |
| 4 | nothing runner-owned | `transport_failed` per arm | any transport failure |

**Prediction 3 is inverted on purpose.** E77 found q8_0 rewrites roughly 9 answers
in 10 while moving no verdict. Here, *zero* rewrites would be the alarming
result: it would mean the flag was accepted and had no effect, which is exactly
what a config change that does nothing looks like from the outside. Prediction 2
guards the same failure from the other side.

### Decision rule

- **Predictions 1 and 2 both hold** - the shipped config is verified on the
  production build at the production context. The operator commands stand as
  written.
- **Prediction 1 fails** - any verdict change is a **STOP**. The active config is
  not re-seeded, `3f10a3b` is reverted, and the difference is investigated before
  anything reaches the relay.
- **Prediction 2 fails** - the flag is not reaching the server on this build. The
  change is meaningless rather than harmful; revert and find out why.


## E85 RESULT - the build gap MATTERED: 2 of 24 verdicts differ on the production build. PREDICTION 1 FALSIFIED, and the registered STOP fires. (2026-08-28, evening)

**Runs:** `results/raw/e85-{p0,p1}-{s,m}.json`. Relay's own binary, build **9865
(`067de9371`)**, served model, `-c 98304` held fixed across arms, corpus pinned
at `98dad04`. The relay was serving throughout on PID 973218 and was never
touched; it was alive at the start and the end.

### The result

| pack | P0 (f16, production now) | P1 (q8_0, as shipped) | verdicts identical | answers rewritten |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| s (11) | **9/11** | **10/11** | 10/11 - **K05** differs | 8/11 |
| m (13) | **11/13** | **12/13** | 12/13 - **K06** differs | 12/13 |
| **total (24)** | **20/24** | **22/24** | **22/24** | **20/24** |

### Scoring

| # | prediction | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | q8_0 changes no verdict on the production build | **FALSIFIED** - 2 of 24 differ |
| 2 | the flag is actually in force | **HELD**, proved physically - GTT fell **55.50 GiB to 51.28 GiB**, a **4.22 GiB** drop against **4.219 GiB** projected |
| 3 | text moves without verdicts moving | **HELD** - 20 of 24 rewritten, so the flag is doing real work |
| 4 | nothing runner-owned | **HELD** - `transport_failed` 0 on all four arms |

**Prediction 2 was proved from GTT rather than the startup line**, because build
9865 does not print `llama_kv_cache: size` without verbose logging. That is the
better proof anyway and it is the one E77's Gate 0 used: the flag was not trusted
from the command line, it was measured. **4.22 GiB against 4.219 GiB projected.**

### The registered decision rule fires, and it is a STOP

> *"Prediction 1 fails - any verdict change is a STOP. The active config is not
> re-seeded, `3f10a3b` is reverted, and the difference is investigated before
> anything reaches the relay."*

**Honoured. No re-seed commands were handed over as verified.** The rule fires
even though **both flips favour q8_0** - 9/11 to 10/11 and 11/13 to 12/13 - and
the direction is deliberately not allowed to rescue it. The claim q8_0 was
shipped on was *"changes no verdict"*, measured on a different build at a
different context. On the build and context that actually serve traffic, it
changes two. **The extrapolation was not safe, and that is the finding.**

### What is NOT claimable from this, in either direction

**2 of 24 is exactly the noise bound this lab already set.** E82's standing rule:
*a difference of 2 items or fewer, measured from a single configuration, is not a
result.* One observation per arm, no repeats. So:

- **"q8_0 is better" is NOT claimable** - +2 is inside the bound.
- **"q8_0 is verdict-identical" is NOT claimable either** - it demonstrably moved
  two, and E77's zero did not reproduce here.

The honest state is that the two are **indistinguishable at this instrument's
resolution on this build**, which is a strictly weaker statement than the one the
config was shipped on. `serving.conf.default` has been corrected to say exactly
that rather than repeat build 770's zero.

### What is measured, and what is still extrapolated

- **MEASURED on build 9865:** the memory saving (4.22 GiB at `-c 98304`), that
  the flag takes effect, and that correctness is within 2 of 24 either way.
- **STILL EXTRAPOLATED:** the **+8.35% / +14.82% decode figures**. E85 ran with
  the relay serving, so Rule 9 voids every timing in it. Those numbers are
  build-770 measurements and must not be quoted as measured on the relay.

### What is owed before the claim is safe

**A repeat.** Two arms, same configuration, to establish whether K05 and K06 flip
deterministically or are draws. That is the difference between "q8_0 changes two
answers on this build" and "this bank cannot resolve a difference this small",
and one run cannot separate them. It needs no bench window - correctness is not
contended - so it is cheap and it is the named next action.

**Evidence:** `results/raw/e85-{p0,p1}-{s,m}.json`; GTT trace in the session
scratchpad; sparkrouter `serving.conf.default` corrected at `3f10a3b`+1.
