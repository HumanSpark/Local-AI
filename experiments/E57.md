## E57: the HELD-OUT deep tier - closing E56's own-answer confound (2026-08-19, pre-registered before any run)

**Why this exists, in one line from E56's own write-up.** *"This design cannot
separate 'held the answer' from 're-derived the answer', because the model's own
earlier reply is in the history it is re-sent."* E56's headline is that every
deep answer came back **byte-identical** to its shallow one, twenty thousand
tokens apart. That is either a strong retention result or an expensively
measured copy, and E56 cannot tell you which. E54b's lesson is that an
instrument defect manufactures a positive result as readily as a negative one,
and this is precisely the shape that would do it.

### The design: hold the question out, and pay for it with a crossover

**Every graded question is asked EXACTLY ONCE per run.** A question answered at
depth here has never been answered in this conversation, so a correct answer had
to come from the pack rather than from scrollback.

Holding a question out destroys E56's within-run control - ask it once and a
wrong answer at depth could be depth, or could be a hard question. So the tier
is a **crossover**, two runs per model over two disjoint sets of five:

| run | turns 1-5 | turns 6-13 | turns 14-18 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `heldout-ab` | **set A** shallow | ten agreements + 3 `recent` probes | **set B** deep |
| `heldout-ba` | **set B** shallow | *byte-identical middle* | **set A** deep |

Every question is therefore observed shallow **and** deep, and never twice in
one conversation. Each question is its own control across the pair, so
set-level difficulty cancels **by construction** rather than by my judgement
that two sets look about as hard - the judgement E54's ambiguous question
punished.

### The ten questions are selected by RULE, and the rule was declared first

They are every question in the L4 bank the conversation lineage (E55, E56) has
never asked, less `U1`. That excludes `P1`, `N2`, `I1` and `D3`, and leaves ten:
**P2, P3, B1, N1, F1, S1, D1, D2, X1, X2.** The split alternates in declaration
order - `SET_A` = P2, B1, F1, D1, X1; `SET_B` = P3, N1, S1, D2, X2 - which is
arbitrary, and which happens to give each set two dates, one numeric, one choice
and one yes/no. Nobody chose that balance; the validator asserts it so a later
bank edit cannot destroy it silently.

`U1` is the single exclusion and it is a grader fact rather than a taste: its
correct answer is a **decline**, and `grade_multiturn` treats declining as the
safe direction of `wrong` - right for every other item, exactly backwards for
that one.

The selection is re-derived in `tools/validate_conversation_heldout.py` from
`QUESTIONS_L4` itself, so a question added to the bank later changes the
selection rather than sitting silently outside it.

### One fixed preamble, and what it costs

All ten carry `"Referring to the engagement pack provided at the start of this
conversation: "` and are otherwise the bank question **verbatim** (asserted).
Ten further services agreements arrive mid-conversation, and several bank
questions were written for a context where the pack was the only thing on the
table - *"the controller"*, *"the Firm"*, no document named. F78 says name the
document or a small model burns its budget deciding which you meant. Left alone,
that ambiguity would bite **only at depth**, which is exactly where the
measurement is.

**The cost, stated rather than discovered:** pointing the model back at the pack
makes this retrieval-WHEN-DIRECTED. That is the professionally realistic case -
an advisor says *"in the engagement letter"* - but it is not spontaneous
retrieval, and E57 cannot speak to that.

### What E57 deliberately does NOT measure

**Corrections.** E56's revision probes need a fact stated mid-conversation and
re-asked after, which is a question that *cannot* be held out - its meaning
depends on the turn before it. F81 and F82 already report that revisions survive
both a short conversation and a deep one. This tier asks the narrower question
neither could: at depth, with nothing of its own to copy, can the model still
read the pack?

**Depth separately from distraction.** At turn 14 the model has both a longer
history *and* ten more documents. E56 conflates these too. Separating them needs
a third arm padded with non-document material, which is a different experiment.

### The middle is inherited, byte-identical, on purpose

Same L4 pack, same ten agreements in the same order, same DIS-05 exclusion,
imported from `conversation_long.py` rather than rebuilt. Re-deriving it would be
a second implementation of a decision that was hard to get right once, and would
break the single thing that makes E57 readable next to E56: **the depth is the
same depth.** Projected final history is **22,300-24,700 tokens, 68-75% of
`-c 32768`**, against E56's measured 71-76% - computed from E56's own measured
tokens-per-char, and treated as a projection until the runs report the real
figure.

**Rule 12 is re-applied rather than assumed.** The filler was built disjoint from
the L2/L3 key and re-checked against E56's key - never against these ten
questions, which E56 never asked. The validator re-derives the protected set from
this tier's own graded turns plus every distinctive figure in the L4 pack, and
finds **no collision**.

### Config

`tools/run_multiturn_eval.py --conversation heldout-ab` / `heldout-ba`, script
`spikes/ps-eval/conversation_heldout.py`, grader
`spikes/ps-eval/grade_multiturn.py` (extended with `choice` and `partial`, both
copied byte-for-byte from the L4 branch so a multiple-choice item cannot score
differently inside a conversation than outside one; E55's 26/26 and E56's 59/59
both still pass after the change). **18 turns, 13 graded** (5 shallow, 5 deep,
3 `recent`), `-c 32768`, `-np 1`, temperature 0, `max_tokens 5120` - all
unchanged from E56.

**Arms.** Qwen3.8-27B at `low`; Qwen3-8B thinking on; the Qwen3-30B-A3B
workhorse. **Six runs**, order workhorse `ab`/`ba`, then 8B, then 27B - the
workhorse is a near-free end-to-end proof of the harness before the expensive
arms. `tools/validate_conversation_heldout.py`: **114/114 checks pass**,
including four synthetic respondents that prove `wrong_document` and `partial`
are reachable rather than reported as zero for the wrong reason.

### The ceiling is declared UP FRONT, because it limits what a pass can mean

On E54b, standalone, these ten score **27B 10/10, 8B 9/10, workhorse 5/10**. The
mechanical rule handed the strongest model a set it already aces, because the
hard items (`N2`, `I1`) are the ones the lineage used and the rule excludes.
Changing the selection now, having seen those numbers, is the cherry-picking the
rule exists to prevent - so it stands, and the consequence is stated instead:
**for the 27B this tier can detect a DROP, not a frontier.** That is the right
instrument for the question actually being asked, since a shallow ceiling is the
cleanest possible baseline for a drop test, but it is not a difficulty probe and
must not be reported as one.

### Predictions (registered before any arm runs). The FIELD is named in each.

Bands are set high; this programme's have sat below the measured result eleven
times now.

1. **PRIMARY: for Qwen3.8-27B, `deep_correct` is at most 1 below
   `shallow_correct`** out of 10. If holding the question out costs the strong
   model nothing, E56's byte-identical result was retention and not a copy.
2. **CONTROL: `shallow_correct` for Qwen3.8-27B is 9 or 10 of 10**, matching its
   E54b standalone 10/10 to within one. If the shallow arm does not reproduce
   standalone, the scope preamble or the conversation framing changed the
   questions and the crossover's baseline is not a baseline.
3. **`wrong_document` is 0 on every arm and in both orders.** F74 measured zero
   wrong-document retrieval at 236,122 tokens and F82 measured zero across all
   three arms. Registered at zero deliberately: the most interesting available
   outcome is that removing the model's own prior answer is what makes
   wrong-document retrieval appear, and a band tolerating one would hide it.
4. **`recent` is 3/3 on every arm in both orders.** E56 measured 5/5 on all
   three. If a model misses a question about an agreement delivered moments
   earlier, it is not decaying with depth and the tier's framing is wrong.
5. **The workhorse's `deep_correct` is within 2 of its `shallow_correct`**, in
   either direction. Its L4 cells are the noisy ones - 5/10 standalone with
   three distinct failure shapes - so a wider band is honest rather than
   generous.
6. **B1 is not `correct` for the 8B or the workhorse in EITHER position.** Both
   scored `off_by_calendar` on it standalone: 72 hours from Friday afternoon
   lands on Easter Monday, and rule C.3 says the clock does not stop. A pass here
   would mean the conversation helped, which nothing predicts.
7. **`format_error` <= 2 per run**, no arm aborts on the per-turn context
   preflight, and every run records its real final history rather than the
   projection above.
8. **Byte-identical answers shallow-versus-deep will be MUCH rarer than E56's
   15/15**, because these are two separate runs rather than two turns of one.
   Registered so that a low number is read as the design working, not as
   instability.

**Falsifier: if Qwen3.8-27B's `deep_correct` is 3 or more below its
`shallow_correct`**, then E56's byte-identical headline WAS the own-answer
confound. F82 would need withdrawing to the much weaker claim that a model can
re-read its own answer at depth, and the routing guide's "load once, interrogate"
advice would need a stated turn limit for conversational use.

**Second falsifier, on the instrument rather than the models: if every arm
degrades on the same one or two questions and no others**, suspect those
questions before concluding anything about depth - the E54b lesson, applied to
this tier's own headline.

**Third falsifier, on the crossover itself: if a model's set-A and set-B shallow
scores differ by 2 or more** in a way E54b's standalone split does not predict,
the two sets are not interchangeable and the crossover is measuring set
difficulty rather than depth.

**Cost.** 6 runs x 18 turns, all local, no cloud spend. Estimated one to two
hours. No model here is near the >~60GiB memory edge, so the deadlock rules do
not bind.

## E57 RESULTS (2026-08-19): the 27B does not decay at depth, the 8B does, and E56 could not tell those apart

Six runs, three models, two orders each. Every arm completed; `format_error` was
**0 everywhere** and `wrong_document` **0 everywhere**. Depth landed at
**69.3-72.5% of `-c 32768`**, against E56's 71-76% - the same depth, which is
what makes the two readable side by side.

| | standalone (E54b) | **shallow** | **deep** | degraded | `recent` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.8-27B `low` | 10/10 | **10/10** | **10/10** | **none** | 6/6 |
| Qwen3-8B | 9/10 | 9/10 | **6/10** | **4** | 6/6 |
| workhorse 30B-A3B | 5/10 | 3/10 | **5/10** | none | 6/6 |

**F83. Held out from its own answer, the 27B does not decay and the 8B does.**
E56 reported that no arm degraded at depth. For the 27B that survives contact
with the held-out design untouched. For the 8B it does not: it holds 5 of 5
paired anchors in E56 and loses **4 of 10** held-out questions here. The
difference between those two results is precisely the confound E57 was built to
remove, and it changed the result rather than qualifying it.

### What the 27B did, and the part that was not predicted

Ten of ten shallow, ten of ten deep, and its shallow arm reproduced its E54b
standalone score **exactly**, which was the registered control. Prediction 8
said byte-identical answers would be much rarer than E56's 15/15 because these
are two separate runs rather than two turns of one. **All ten of its answers are
byte-identical between the `ab` and `ba` runs** - same string, asked at turn 3
in one run and turn 16 in the other, twenty thousand tokens apart, across two
separate server processes. That is a determinism result E56 could not have
produced and E57 did not set out to measure.

### What the 8B did, and it is one failure mode rather than four

Four degradations, and the mechanism is visible in the token counts rather than
inferred: **the model reasons MORE at depth and lands worse.**

| | shallow | deep |
|---|---|---|
| `F1` | **correct** in 558 generated tokens | **truncated** at the 5,120 ceiling - 9.2x the reasoning, no answer |
| `P2` | **correct** in 1,376 tokens (`30 April 2026`) | **wrong** in 2,303 tokens (`31 March 2026`) |
| `X1` | **correct** in 539 tokens (`NO`) | **wrong** in 562 tokens (`YES`) |

`F1`'s deep cell is a BUDGET exhaustion, not a wrong answer - `finish_reason`
`length`, 13,891 characters of reasoning against 1,500 shallow. E55 already
warned that a small model used conversationally needs a bigger answer budget or
its failures read as capability, and this is that warning landing again on a
tier that raised the budget to 5,120 and still hit it. Counted honestly the 8B
is **3 genuine degradations and 1 truncation**, not 4 capability failures.

### The workhorse improved, and the tier cannot say much about it

3/10 shallow to 5/10 deep, with `P3` and `F1` both moving from wrong to correct.
Do not read that as depth helping. Its shallow arm is **below its own standalone
5/10**, so the shallow cell is near this instrument's floor and a floor cannot
degrade. What the tier does establish for the workhorse is negative and useful:
it never retrieved from the wrong document, and it answered every `recent` probe.

**One observation worth recording without over-reading it.** The workhorse scores
`X1` and `X2` **correct standalone and wrong in BOTH conversational positions** -
the two `defect_absent` traps, where the right answer is that nothing is
breached. The 8B gets both right shallow and both wrong deep. F76 says single
cells are noisy, and this is four cells across two models, so it is a lead
rather than a finding: the conversational framing may prime a model that has
just been asked what is wrong with a contract to keep finding things wrong.

### A grader defect, found by an arm rather than by the validator

`grade_multiturn` labelled a wrong `YES` as `over_revised` on **any** yes/no
item. That is correct for E56, whose only yes/no turn WAS the over-revision
probe, and wrong for E57, which contains no revisions at all - so `X1` and `X2`
were reported as over-revisions in a tier with nothing to over-revise.
`grade_answer_l4` calls the same answer `wrong`, so a yes/no item was scoring a
different label inside a conversation than outside one. **That is exactly the
divergence the `choice` branch was copied byte-for-byte to prevent, and `yes_no`
was missed.**

The repair gates `over_revised` on the turn actually being an over-revision
probe. It was NOT applied mid-queue - that would have graded later arms with
different code than earlier ones. Instead `tools/regrade_multiturn.py`
re-derived the labels from the stored answers, which is E54b's pattern and costs
no GPU:

- **Proven no-op first:** the tool reproduced the runner's own grading on arm 1
  with 0 cells moved, before any change. So every cell it moved afterwards is
  attributable to the repair alone.
- **Proven no-op on the prior tiers:** all six E55 and E56 runs regrade with
  **0 cells moved**, reproducing their published scores exactly.
- **On E57: 6 cells moved, all `over_revised` -> `wrong`, all `X1`/`X2`, and no
  `correct` count changed on any arm.**

The validator now carries a `says_yes_anyway` respondent that answers YES to a
want-NO item and asserts it grades `wrong` with `over_revised` at 0. **116/116.**
Nothing caught this before an arm produced it, because no synthetic respondent
had ever answered YES to a want-NO item - the signature table tested the shapes
I had thought of.

### Predictions: six held, two falsified, and both falsifications are the models beating the band

1. **PRIMARY - 27B `deep_correct` at most 1 below shallow. HELD**, at 0 below.
2. **CONTROL - 27B shallow 9 or 10. HELD** at 10/10, matching standalone exactly.
3. **`wrong_document` 0 on every arm and both orders. HELD.** Removing the
   model's own prior answer did NOT make wrong-document retrieval appear.
4. **`recent` 3/3 every arm, both orders. HELD**, 6/6 on all three models.
5. **Workhorse deep within 2 of shallow. HELD**, at exactly +2 - the edge of the
   band, and in the improving direction.
6. **B1 not correct for the 8B or workhorse in either position. FALSIFIED for the
   8B**, which answered `6 April 2026 at 14:30` in BOTH positions - walking 72
   hours through the Easter Monday holiday that rule C.3 says does not stop the
   clock, where standalone it scored `off_by_calendar`. Held for the workhorse.
7. **`format_error` <= 2 per run. HELD** at 0 on all six; one `truncated`, which
   is a different outcome and is reported as one.
8. **Byte-identical shallow-vs-deep much rarer than E56's 15/15. FALSIFIED for
   the 27B** at 10/10 across two separate runs. Held for the 8B and workhorse,
   both 5/10.

**Twelve and thirteen.** That is the twelfth and thirteenth time this
programme's registered bands have sat below the measured result, and no
registered falsifier has ever fired.

### What this does to F82, and what it does not

**The primary falsifier did not fire, so F82's headline stands for the 27B.**
Its byte-identical deep answers in E56 were retention, not a copy - it
reproduces the same answers when its own answer is removed, and reproduces them
across separate processes.

**F82's "no arm degraded" is AMENDED for the 8B.** E56 could not distinguish
holding from re-reading, and for the small model the distinction turns out to
matter: 5 of 5 anchors held when its own answer was available, 6 of 10 when it
was not. Anyone quoting E56's result for a small model must now quote E57's
instead.

**What E57 still cannot separate:** depth from distraction. At turn 14 the model
has a longer history AND ten more documents, exactly as in E56. A third arm
padded with non-document material would separate them and has not been run.
Nor does this tier speak to spontaneous retrieval - every question carries a
preamble pointing at the pack, which is the realistic advisory case and is not
the same as the model finding the pack unprompted.
