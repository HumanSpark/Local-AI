## E40: technical writing - can this box draft the work, and can it be trusted with the bad news? (2026-08-15, pre-registered before any run)

**Why this exists.** The owner's question is direct: can the box be used for
writing, and for technical white papers. Every writing result in this repo
(F34, F35, F37) is drafting scored by a frontier model acting as judge, so the
ruler is an unmeasured cloud model and the answer rests on its opinion. E40
removes the judge.

**What is deliberately NOT measured.** Whether the prose is any good. Elegance,
register, rhythm and persuasiveness are real and are not mechanically
decidable, and the style canon says so itself - section 10 of
`style-rules.md` lists sixteen judgement rules no gate can see and states that
a clean sweep is not a clean bill. The result JSON carries that list in a
`not_measured` field so no reader can take a score for a verdict on quality.

**What IS measured** is every property that makes a draft unpublishable
however well it reads.

1. **`fabrication_rate`** - a numeral not in the source brief and not
   derivable from it. Every legitimate figure, including the derivations a
   competent writer performs (the 8.47x throughput ratio, the 1.37 gap, the
   EUR 720 VAT component), is enumerated in `spikes/writing-eval/source.py`
   WITH its derivation, so the allowlist is auditable rather than trusted.
2. **`negative_retention`** - the brief carries three findings unfavourable
   to the thing being written about, and most tasks require them to survive
   into the output. This is the failure fluency actively hides: the most
   readable summary of an awkward result is usually the one that omits it,
   and no fluency measure can see it. The `persuasive_pressure` task exists
   only to put this under load - it demands a genuinely persuasive case that
   still states every unfavourable finding.
3. **House style**, graded against the ACTUAL canon rather than a paraphrase.
   `house_style.py` implements the gated rules of SR-1 to SR-56 with each hit
   tagged by SR number, preserving the canon's surface scoping: SR-50 (no
   product names) binds only the durable-copy task, because applying it to a
   technical report that names its own test hardware would be wrong.
4. **Length compliance** against a stated word budget.

**The prompt and the grader share one source.** Every rule the grader enforces
is rendered into the prompt by `house_style.style_guide()` from the same
constants. A hand-written prompt would drift and the eval would be scoring
telepathy rather than instruction-following.

**Seven tasks:** `exec_summary`, `honest_limits`, `plain_english` (rounding
forbidden), `client_email`, `abstract` (hard 90-word ceiling),
`persuasive_pressure`, `book_passage` (durable copy, SR-50 binds).

**Graders validated before any model runs.** Six synthetic respondents each
produce their signature, and a hand-written compliant draft exists for all
seven tasks, scoring clean and fitting every word budget. That last check is
the important one: if a careful human cannot satisfy the constraints inside
the budget, the task is unfair and a model score against it would measure an
impossible instruction.

**Gates calibrated against the author's own 161,801-word corpus**
(`reference/author-books/`), which found two real gate defects - a
leading-boundary match fired the US-spelling gate on "laborious" and
"laboratory", and the weekday gate flagged the Quit Question that SR-40
sanctions by name. Both fixed before any model was scored.

**Config.** `tools/run_writing_eval.py`, llama-server b10435, `-c 16384`,
`-np 1`, temperature 0, `max_tokens 4096`. One request per task.

**Arms.** Qwen3.8-27B-Q4_K_M (subject); Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 (the
workhorse incumbent, which the routing guide currently sends drafting work
to); `qwen/qwen3.8-27b` cloud if budget allows.

**Predictions (registered before any arm runs). The FIELD is named in each.**

1. **`fabrication_rate`.** Qwen3.8 **0.14-0.57** (1 to 4 of 7 tasks carrying
   an invented figure); workhorse **0.29-0.71**. Both models scored 0.00
   over-claim on the PS eval's unanswerable items, but declining to answer is
   a different act from writing 200 words of persuasive prose, and the
   pressure to produce a round number is created by the task.
2. **`negative_retention` is LOWEST on `persuasive_pressure` for every arm.**
   Recorded field: `outcome == "dropped_negative"` by task id. If an arm
   drops a finding anywhere, this is where.
3. **BOTH local arms fail `book_passage` on SR-50.** The forbidden product
   names are sitting in the model's own context, supplied as facts in the
   brief, and the instruction asks it to substitute categories for names it
   was just handed. This is the sharpest single prediction here and the one
   most likely to be wrong in an interesting way.
4. **`correct` (fully clean) out of 7.** Qwen3.8 **1-4**; workhorse **0-3**.
5. **`abstract` breaches its 90-word ceiling for at least one arm.** A hard
   ceiling stated twice in the prompt is a pure instruction-following test
   with no reasoning content.

**Not predicted:** which style rule each arm trips. The metric bands are the
falsifiable part.

**Falsifier for the whole experiment: if EVERY arm scores 0 of 7, the task is
over-constrained and ranks nothing.** Twenty-odd simultaneous style rules plus
a word budget plus a fabrication ban is a lot to hold, and a floor is as
useless as a ceiling. The response would be to grade the dimensions
separately rather than requiring a clean sweep for `correct`, and to say
plainly that the first design was too harsh - not to quote the zeros.

### E38 AMENDMENT 1 (declared 2026-08-15 09:58, BEFORE any local arm runs)

**A question defect, found by a model answering it better than my key did.**

The cloud arm scored 11/12. The single miss is V1, and V1 is wrong, not the
model.

V1 asks: *"For how many months may the Supplier retain Client personal data
following termination, in respect of a termination occurring in February
2026?"* My key is **24** - Amendment 3 revokes Amendment 2's paragraph 3 and
reinstates the original 24-month period, which is the revocation chain the
question exists to test. The model answered **6**.

Six is the Policy's period, and SIDE-1 - the precedence side letter I added
to this corpus for the P1 and P2 questions - makes the Policy prevail over the
Agreement on data matters. So the model applied the precedence rule and gave
the period that actually governs. On the question as worded, that is
defensible and arguably better than my key.

**The general lesson, which is worth more than the fixed question: adding a
document to a corpus can silently change the correct answer to a question
already in it.** SIDE-1 was written to make P1 and P2 possible. Nothing in the
process of adding it asked what it did to the eleven questions already
present, and the graders could not catch it because the synthetic respondents
answer what I intended rather than what a careful reader would. A validator
built from my own reference answers cannot find an error in my own reference
answers.

**The amendment.** V1 is scoped explicitly to the Agreement:

> "Under the Agreement as amended, and disregarding the Client's internal
> policy, for how many months may the Supplier retain Client personal data
> following a termination occurring in February 2026?"

and **6** is added as a named trap on V1 with the outcome `over_applied` - a
model that still answers 6 after being told to disregard the Policy has
carried the precedence rule past an explicit instruction, which is a real and
nameable failure rather than generic wrongness.

**What this does to the arms already run.** The cloud arm ran on the ORIGINAL
wording. Its V1 row is NOT comparable to any arm run after this amendment and
must not be pooled with them. The cloud arm will be re-run on the amended
instrument; it costs about $0.05. Until it is, the comparable figure for that
arm is **11 of the 11 unaffected questions**, not 11/12.

**No prediction changes.** The registered bands in E38 stand exactly as
written and are resolved against the amended instrument.

### E38: the whole-tier falsifier, and which arm resolves it (declared 2026-08-15 09:58, BEFORE any local arm runs)

The registration says: *"if Qwen3.8 scores 11 or 12 of 12, L2 has ALSO
saturated"*. The cloud arm has now scored 11. **The registration does not say
WHICH Qwen3.8 arm resolves that falsifier, and that ambiguity is a
specification defect of exactly the kind F65 names** - the fourth this week.
It is declared now, before the local arm runs, rather than resolved after
seeing a score that suits one reading.

**Ruling: the falsifier is resolved against the LOCAL Qwen3.8-27B-Q4_K_M
arm.** The reason is that the entire programme exists to decide what runs on
this box. A cloud arm is a reference point for what quantisation costs, not a
candidate for any routing slot, so a ceiling claim about "this model on this
hardware" cannot be settled by a model served somewhere else.

**The cloud arm's 11 is recorded as strong corroborating evidence and not as
the resolution.** If the local arm also reaches 11 or 12, L2 has saturated,
and the honest report is that this box's knowledge-work capability exceeds
every instrument built for it so far - which is a finding, and is not the one
this experiment set out to produce.

### E33 RE-RUN AT 24576: VOID. The harness destroyed it, and the harness was mine.

**The re-run produced no usable capability result and its numbers must not be
quoted.** It reports 10/15 passed. Four tasks produced NO DATA AT ALL.

| | 8192 run | 24576 re-run |
|---|---|---|
| pass | 9/15 | 10/15 |
| truncated | 5 | 0 |
| transport_failed | 0 | **4** |
| error | 1 | 1 |
| wall | 4,283s | 5,324s |

**Every one of the four transport failures reads `timed out` at a wall time of
900.1 seconds.** That is the runner's `--request-timeout` default of 900s,
which I did not raise when I raised `max_tokens` from 8192 to 24576 under
Amendment 2. At the ~12.6 tok/s this model sustains at `-np 1`, a full
24,576-token generation needs about 1,950 seconds. So the amendment
guaranteed that any task using more than roughly 11,300 tokens would die at
the transport layer - and that is exactly the set of tasks the re-run existed
to measure.

**The falsifier is NOT resolved.** Amendment 2 registered that at least 3 of
the 5 tasks truncated at 8192 must complete and emit code, and that 3 or more
truncating again would mean non-termination rather than a budget limit.
Measured: 4 of the 5 timed out and 1 (`roman_roundtrip`) PASSED. A timeout is
neither outcome. Nothing about non-termination is established or refuted, and
the one pass is weak evidence that the budget mattered for at least one task.

**This is a fifth pre-registration-adjacent failure and it is a new kind.**
F65's three were specification errors - the registration named the wrong
thing. The fourth (E33 Amendment 1) was a filing error. This one is a
CONFIGURATION COUPLING error: the amendment was specified correctly, filed
correctly and in advance, and still could not produce data because raising one
parameter silently invalidated another that nobody had thought of as related.
Declaring an amendment does not make it executable.

**Fixed at the source rather than in this run's command line.**
`resolve_request_timeout()` now derives the timeout from `max_tokens` at a
conservative 6 tok/s floor plus a 300s margin, in all three runners
(`run_coding_eval`, `run_ps_eval`, `run_writing_eval`). Omitting the flag
yields 4,396s at 24576 and 983s at 4096. An explicit shorter value is still
honoured, because capping a run deliberately is legitimate, but it now prints
a warning naming this incident. The silent case is what is closed.

**Also fixed: the result JSON did not record `ctx`, `request_timeout` or
`exec_timeout`.** Diagnosing this meant inferring the timeout from a `wall_s`
of 900.1 rather than reading it. All three are now recorded by every runner. A
config that a result cannot be re-derived from is a config that gets argued
about later.

**The re-run must itself be re-run.** Queued as E33 RE-RUN 2 with the derived
timeout. No prediction changes; Amendment 2's registered bands and its
two-directional falsifier stand and are resolved against that run.
