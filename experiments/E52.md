## E52: can a model that fits a laptop do real document work? (2026-08-17, pre-registered before any run)

**Why this exists.** Every knowledge-work, legal and writing measurement in this
repo has been taken on models of 24B and up. The question asked most often in
public - and the one a consulting client asks first - is whether a model small
enough to run on ordinary hardware can do the work, and nothing here answers it.
The 8B and 4B artefacts fetched in July were throughput-only (Step 8 Block 2b)
and are no longer on disk.

**ONE VARIABLE CHANGES: model size.** The instruments, the packs, the keys, the
graders, the prompt, the temperature and the answer budget are all held at the
values E37, E38 and E41 already ran, so the 8B's numbers drop straight into the
existing tables.

### The arms, counted honestly

**One new arm runs.** The comparators are already measured and are quoted, not
re-run:

| arm | status | L1 `correct` | L2 `correct` | L3 n=9 `correct` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-8B-Q4_K_M (4.68 GiB) | **NEW - this experiment** | ? | ? | ? |
| Qwen3.8-27B-Q4_K_M (15.66 GiB) | measured E37/E38/E41 | 20/20 | 12/12 | 12/12 |
| Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 (17.28 GiB) | measured E37/E38/E41 | 14/20 | 5/12 | 4/12 |

**This is NOT a clean size isolation, and calling it one would be false.** The
8B is Qwen3, the 27B is Qwen3.5-generation (`arch=qwen35`), and the workhorse is
a 30B MoE with 3B active. Size, generation and architecture all move together.
The question E52 answers is a **deployment** question - *is a laptop-sized model
good enough for this work* - not a scaling-law question, and it must not be
quoted as the latter.

### The one thing that is already decided by the artefact, before any run

`n_ctx_train` is **40,960** for the 8B against **262,144** for the 27B - a
factor of **6.4**. F74's headline, 12/12 at **236,122 tokens (90.1% of trained
context)**, is not a result the 8B can fail; it is a run the 8B **cannot
attempt**. The largest E48 rung it could hold is below n=29 (~61,700 tokens).
This is read from the GGUF metadata, not predicted, and it is stated here so
that the ladder's ceiling is not later reported as a discovery.

### Config

`tools/run_ps_eval.py`, model `/opt/models/staging/Qwen3-8B-Q4_K_M.gguf`
(official `Qwen/Qwen3-8B-GGUF`, rev `7c41481f`, size-exact and sha256-verified
against the pinned upstream oid, `manifests/MANIFEST.md`). Temperature 0,
`--np 1`, `max_tokens 4096`, one request per question, pack resent each time.

**Reasoning control differs from the 27B's and the difference is mechanical.**
The 8B's embedded chat template honours `enable_thinking` and contains **no**
`reasoning_effort` (both checked by reading the template out of the GGUF, not
assumed). So `--thinking low|medium|xhigh` would be a **silent no-op** on this
model - the flag would be recorded in the result file while changing nothing.
Only two settings are meaningful and only those are used: `default` (thinking
**ON**, the template's default) and `off`.

| # | tier | `-c` | thinking | comparator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | L1 | 16384 | default (ON) | E37 27B `default`, workhorse `default` |
| 2 | L2 | 16384 | default (ON) | E38 27B `default`, workhorse `default` |
| 3 | L3 n=0 | 32768 | default (ON) | E41 27B `low`, workhorse `default` |
| 4 | L3 n=4 | 32768 | default (ON) | E41 27B `low` |
| 5 | L3 n=9 | 32768 | default (ON) | E41 27B `low`, workhorse `default` |
| 6 | L3 n=14 | 40960 | default (ON) | **none** - new rung, at the 8B's ceiling |
| 7 | L1 | 16384 | **off** | arm 1 |

Arms 3-5 use E41's exact `-c 32768` and byte-identical packs, so they are
like-for-like. Arm 6 has no comparator and needs `-c 40960` (the pack is ~31.3K
estimated tokens and 4,096 of answer budget puts it at ~35.4K, over 32768); it
is declared as a different config for that reason. The 27B arms it is compared
against ran at `thinking: low`, the 8B has no such dial, and that asymmetry is
recorded rather than corrected - there is no setting that would make them equal.

### Predictions (registered before any arm runs). The FIELD is named in each.

Bands are set **high** deliberately: HANDOFF records that prediction bands sat
below the measured result eight times before E49, and E37 falsified four of five
in the same direction.

1. **PRIMARY: `correct` on L1 is 13-18 of 20** - at or above the 30B MoE
   workhorse's 14/20, on a model less than a third its size. If the 8B beats 14,
   the routing guide's "workhorse" slot is open to challenge on capability
   grounds and the reason to keep it becomes speed alone.
2. **`correct` on L2 is 5-9 of 12.** The workhorse scored 5, the 27B 12. L2 was
   built to punish the "latest amendment wins" heuristic, and a small model with
   thinking on is the case where that heuristic is most likely to be all there
   is.
3. **DISCRIMINATING: `correct` at L3 n=9 is at least 3 BELOW `correct` at L3
   n=0.** The 27B was flat across the same three rungs (12/12/12, F74's
   precursor) and the workhorse fell 5 to 4. Distractor load is where a small
   model should break, and this is the claim most likely to be wrong.
4. **`over_claim_rate` on L1 (fabricated answers over the 4 unanswerable items)
   is 0.00-0.50.** Both local arms scored 0.00 on L1; declining to answer is the
   one thing the whole field has been good at, and a small model breaking it
   would be the most commercially serious result available here.
5. **`citation_valid` is at or below `correct` on every arm.** Registered as an
   inequality rather than a band because E37's version of this prediction was
   falsified by the 27B scoring 20/20 on both.
6. **Turning thinking `off` costs at least 3 on L1 `correct`** (arm 7 vs arm 1).
   E42 measured what reasoning buys on the 27B; this is the same question on a
   model with a third of the parameters, where there is less to fall back on.
7. **`wall_s` on L1 arm 1 is under 300s**, against the 27B's 597.9s. **This is a
   speed figure for THIS GPU and does not transfer to a laptop** - the artefact
   fits a laptop's memory, the throughput was not measured on one, and the
   report must say so.

**Not predicted:** which individual questions fail. F76 is the reason - a
single per-question cell is a sample of one here, and naming them after the fact
would be theatre.

**Falsifier for the whole experiment:** if any arm records `format_error` on
more than 4 of its questions, that arm's content scores are not interpretable
and must not be quoted. This is E37's falsifier carried forward unchanged, and
it bites harder on a small model, which is likelier to drop the two-line
`ANSWER:`/`CITATION:` form.

**Throughput is NOT comparable to the July Step 8 Block 2b figures.** That 8B
was a different artefact from a different publisher (`unsloth/Qwen3-8B-GGUF`,
rev `a6adef13`, a different sha256) and is no longer on disk. F29 - a quantiser
and a publisher are part of the config.

**Cost.** 100 questions across seven arms (20 + 12 + 12 + 12 + 12 + 12 + 20),
all local, no cloud spend. Estimated
under an hour of GPU on the 27B's per-question rates, and the 8B is smaller.

### E52 AMENDMENT 1, declared 2026-08-17 before the arm it adds

**A gap opened between two arms that the design treated as the same
instrument, and it has to be closed rather than noted.** L2 scored **9/12 with
3 `truncated`**; L3 at n=0 scored **11/12 with none**. The registered config
table calls the L3 n=0 pack "byte-identical to L2", and `pack_chars` is 13,221
in both, so the first reading was that `-c` (16384 vs 32768) had moved it -
which would contradict E44 arms A and B, where a doubling of `-c` left the 27B
byte-identical.

**That reading is wrong, and checking it is what found the real difference.**
`prompt_tokens` is not equal across the two arms - it differs on 11 of 12
questions, by 2 to 35 tokens. The packs are identical; **the QUESTIONS are
not.** `QUESTIONS_L3` restates each `QUESTIONS_L2` item with the agreement and
the party named in full ("Under the Master Services Agreement dated 1 March
2024 between Northwind Logistics Limited and Calderwood Advisory LLP, as
amended, what monthly retainer..."), because a terse question becomes ambiguous
once the pack contains 119 similar agreements. Eleven of the twelve differ;
only `N1` is word-for-word the same. What `tools/validate_ps_eval_l3.py`
asserts is equality of the REASONING and the KEYS, not of the question text.

So the two arms differ in **two** things at once - `-c` and question wording -
and neither the 2-point gap nor the three truncations can be attributed. On the
27B this was invisible: E38 and E41 n=0 both scored 12/12, so a confound that
has been in the instrument since 2026-08-15 only became visible on a model
small enough to be moved by it.

**Arm 8, added: L2 tier at `-c 32768`**, every other setting held at arm 2's
values. One variable changes against arm 2.

**Prediction, registered before it runs.** `truncated` stays at **3** and
`correct` stays at **9/12**, because the cause is question wording and not
`-c`. If instead the truncations disappear at the larger `-c`, then `-c` moves
temperature-0 output on this model where E44 showed it does not on the 27B, and
that is a serving finding that outranks anything else in E52.

**Either way the L2 arm's 9/12 is not quotable as a capability score.** Three of
twelve answers were cut off at the 4,096-token budget rather than answered
wrongly - F20's trap, a mechanical failure recorded as a capability failure. The
capability reading for this question set is L3 n=0's 11/12.

## E52 RESULTS (2026-08-17): the small model is not the weak link the design assumed

Eight arms, all local, no cloud spend. Zero `format_error` on every arm, so
E37's falsifier did not fire and the content scores are interpretable.

| arm | tier | `-c` | thinking | `correct` | `citation_valid` | outcomes | wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | L1 | 16384 | on | **16/20** | 19/20 | 3 partial, 1 over_claim | 258.0s |
| 7 | L1 | 16384 | **off** | **12/20** | 17/20 | 5 wrong, 2 partial, 1 over_claim | **20.5s** |
| 2 | L2 | 16384 | on | 9/12 | 9/12 | **3 truncated** | 533.4s |
| 8 | L2 | **32768** | on | 9/12 | 9/12 | **3 truncated** | 532.2s |
| 3 | L3 n=0 | 32768 | on | **11/12** | 12/12 | 1 wrong | 269.5s |
| 4 | L3 n=4 | 32768 | on | 10/12 | 11/12 | 1 wrong, 1 over_applied | 386.1s |
| 5 | L3 n=9 | 32768 | on | 10/12 | 11/12 | 1 premature, 1 over_applied | 500.5s |
| 6 | L3 n=14 | 40960 | on | **11/12** | 9/12 | 1 over_applied | 489.6s |

Against the comparators, which were measured on 2026-08-15 and are quoted, not
re-run:

| | 8B **4.68 GiB** | workhorse 30B-A3B 17.28 GiB | Qwen3.8-27B 15.66 GiB |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | **16/20** in 258.0s | 14/20 in 16.0s | 20/20 in 597.9s |
| L2 | 9/12 (3 truncated) | 5/12 | 12/12 |
| L3 n=0 | **11/12** | 5/12 | 12/12 |
| L3 n=9 | **10/12** | 4/12 | 12/12 |

**The headline: a 4.68 GiB model beats a 17.28 GiB MoE by 5 to 6 points on the
two harder tiers, and gives up 1 to 2 against a 27B that is 3.34x its weight.**
It is 16.1x slower than the workhorse on L1 and 2.32x faster than the 27B.

### Prediction outcomes: five held, two falsified

1. **HELD.** `correct` on L1 is 16, inside the registered 13-18, and above the
   workhorse's 14.
2. **HELD on the letter and NOT QUOTABLE.** L2 `correct` is 9, the top of the
   registered 5-9 band - but 3 of the 12 are `truncated`, not wrong. See
   Amendment 1. The capability reading for this question set is arm 3's 11/12.
3. **FALSIFIED, and it is the most useful result here.** `correct` at L3 n=9 was
   registered at least 3 below n=0. It is **1** below (10 vs 11), and n=14
   recovers to 11/12 at a measured 30,039 tokens - 73.3% of the 8B's trained
   context. **Distractor load is not where this model breaks.**
4. **HELD.** `over_claim_rate` on L1 is 0.25 (U1 of 4), inside 0.00-0.50. Both
   larger local arms scored 0.00, so the 8B is the first model measured here to
   fabricate an answer to an unanswerable item.
5. **FALSIFIED on 5 of 8 arms.** `citation_valid` was registered at or below
   `correct`; it is ABOVE it on arms 1, 7, 3, 4 and 5 (19 vs 16, 17 vs 12, 12 vs
   11, 11 vs 10, 11 vs 10). **This model names the right clause and then gets
   the answer wrong more often than the reverse** - which is the opposite of the
   failure mode a citation requirement is usually added to catch, and it means a
   plausible citation is no evidence the answer is right.
6. **HELD, and more sharply than registered.** Thinking `off` costs 4 on L1
   (16 -> 12), and it is not spread out: **all four `computation` items flip
   correct -> wrong**, and X2 `conflict` goes from "NO" (the right judgement) to
   "YES" (the conflict missed entirely, which is exactly what the workhorse
   did). Wall goes 258.0s -> 20.5s, a factor of **12.59**; median latency 8.95s
   -> 0.79s. **With thinking off the 8B scores BELOW the workhorse; with it on,
   above.** For this model reasoning is not a tuning preference, it is the
   capability.
7. **HELD.** L1 wall is 258.0s against the 27B's 597.9s. **This is a figure for
   this GPU. The artefact fits a laptop's memory; the throughput was not
   measured on one and must not be reported as if it were.**

### Amendment 1 resolved: `-c` is inert, the QUESTION WORDING is the difference

Arm 8 ran L2 at `-c 32768` against arm 2's `-c 16384`, one variable changed.
Predicted: 9/12 with 3 `truncated`. Measured: **9/12 with 3 `truncated`, the
same three questions (D2, P1, P2), and all twelve answers byte-identical -
including `completion_tokens` to the token.**

So a doubling of `-c` leaves temperature-0 output bit-for-bit unchanged on the
8B, reproducing E44 arms A and B on a second model and a different
architecture. The 2-point gap between L2 and L3 n=0 is entirely the question
wording: `QUESTIONS_L3` names the agreement and the party in full, and on the
same 13,221-char pack that phrasing takes D2, P1 and P2 from 4,096 tokens of
runaway reasoning to 1,510, 782 and 736 tokens of correct answer.

**That is a deployment instruction, not a curiosity.** Naming the document and
the party in the question is worth 2 of 12 on this model, and it costs nothing.
The 27B scored 12/12 on both phrasings, so the effect is invisible at 27B and
the confound sat in the instrument from 2026-08-15 until a model small enough
to be moved by it was run.

### What the flat ladder hides

The total is flat (11, 10, 10, 11) and the composition is not:

| | n=0 | n=4 | n=9 | n=14 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `P2` scoped precedence | correct | **over_applied** | **over_applied** | **over_applied** |
| `V2`, `M2`, `D1` | 1 miss | 1 miss | 1 miss | 0 |

`P2` is correct with no distractors and applies the side letter out of scope in
all three arms that have them. **Every cell here is n=1**, so per F76 the three
singleton misses are not evidence of anything; P2's 3-of-3 is directional and no
stronger than that. A powered re-run would be needed to call it, and it is not
worth one on its own - E53 is the registered vehicle for that class of question.

### Two measured facts about the artefact, for anyone comparing token counts

- On the byte-identical 93,920-char n=9 pack the 8B tokenizes to **20,572**
  tokens and the 27B to **21,267** - **3.27% fewer**. The tokenizers are
  different generations (`qwen3` vs `qwen35`), so a token count measured on one
  model is not transferable to the other even on identical input.
- The n=14 preflight left **6,833 tokens** of headroom at `-c 40960`, so two or
  three further rungs would fit before the trained context stops it. They were
  not run: the curve is already flat over a 10x range of pack size, and another
  point on a flat curve is not worth the GPU. **The hard stop is the artefact's
  40,960-token trained context**, which is 6.4x below the 27B's - F74's
  236,122-token result stays a run this model cannot attempt.
