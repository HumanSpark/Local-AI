### Prediction 1 held, and more strongly than registered

**Across all three arms and all fourteen unchanged questions, not one ANSWER
STRING changed.** Not one. The only outcome differences are `B1` for the 8B and
the workhorse, where a byte-identical answer - `'5 April 2026 at 14:30'` in both
runs - moved from `wrong` to `off_by_calendar` because the trap list now names
that error.

So F70's sequence effect does **not** reach this tier: at temperature 0, same
config, same sequence position, the output is fully reproducible and the totals
need none of the noise treatment F76 forced on the tool-use instrument. That is
worth more than the score, because it means every per-question cell in E54 and
E54b is a real observation rather than one sample of a noisy quantity.

### Predictions 2 and 3 held, and 3 is the one that matters

`off_by_calendar` rose for two of three arms, purely from re-grading identical
answers - a scoring change, demonstrably not a capability change.

**Two arms now score `F1` correct, both at EUR 106,800.** The 27B previously
answered 79,200 and the 8B truncated. Removing the ambiguous derivation clause
turned a defensible-but-wrong answer into the right one, which settles what F1
was: **a defective question, not a hard one.** The withdrawal was correct.

### AND THAT FALSIFIES E54's HEADLINE. Recorded loudly because it was published

E54's registered falsifier read: *"if Qwen3.8-27B scores 14 or 15, L4 has not
found the frontier either."* On the repaired instrument it scores **14**.

**The falsifier fires.** L4 does not bound Qwen3.8-27B.

**The 13/15 E54 reported was partly an artefact of my own broken question.** Had
F1 been written correctly at registration, the falsifier would have fired on the
first run and E54 would never have claimed a floor. The claim was carried into
`HANDOFF.md`, the ecosystem status page and two session reports before the
repair exposed it; all are corrected.

The general lesson is the one this programme keeps re-learning from the other
direction: **an instrument defect can manufacture a POSITIVE result as easily as
a negative one.** F20 and E48's distractor check both guard against a defect
that makes a model look worse. This is the same failure making a model look
bounded when it is not, and nothing in the score could have shown it - only
repairing the question and re-running did.

### What survives, and it is sharper than what was lost

The comparative finding is not weakened by the falsification, it is
strengthened, because F1 is now a valid walked-period item and the split runs
over eight questions instead of seven:

- **The workhorse scores 0 of 8 walked periods.** Zero. It scores 6 of 7 on
  static analysis.
- **The 27B's single remaining miss in the entire tier is `N2`** - the
  composition question. It walks calendars at 7/8, computes statutory interest
  correctly, and fails only where two constraints must be applied together.

**So F80 as written is wrong for the top model and needs tiering, not
retracting.** "What the small models cannot do is walk a calendar" is exactly
right for the 30B MoE workhorse and false for Qwen3.8-27B. The correct
statement is that the failure moves up the tiers: the workhorse cannot count
time at all, the 8B counts it unreliably (5/8), and the 27B counts it well and
fails at **composing** two constraints it has each already satisfied. See the
F80 amendment.

### What this makes the next experiment

The 27B's frontier is now located at one question shape, and a fifteen-question
single-turn instrument reaching it once is not enough to characterise it.
**Multi-turn moves from "queued" to the obvious next build**: composition across
turns is the same capability under more pressure, and it is the one thing this
programme has never measured at all.
