## E60 (E59 stage 2): does MTP make the chat tier fast enough to remove routing? (2026-08-19, pre-registered before any run)

Four cells, chat tier only, 27B at `reasoning_effort: off`, n=4 fixed. E59
earned the right to drop n=2, other quants and other backends from this
experiment.

| MTP | profile | role |
|---|---|---|
| off | balanced | the existing **5.48s** control |
| n=4 | balanced | **primary architecture test** |
| off | performance | profile main effect |
| n=4 | performance | interaction / best attainable |

**Acceptance is measured again, not inherited.** E59's 83.4% is on held-out legal
reasoning at `low`. Acceptance depends on the draft sequence actually proposed,
and short conversational continuations are a different prediction problem, so
stage 2 measures its own.

### Three outcomes, kept separate

1. **Registered falsifier.** If `MTP n=4 + balanced` is **under 2.0s warm
   median**, the two-model recommendation is falsified by its original rule.
   **Balanced is the condition for this test** because that is where the 5.48s
   measurement and the rule were established - letting a profile change into the
   falsifier would move the goalposts silently.
2. **Operational decision, which is NOT the falsifier.** At 2.3-2.8s the
   falsifier stays unfired, and the separate question gets asked explicitly: is
   saving 1.5-2s per chat response worth a second resident model and a routing
   layer? That is engineering judgement, not an experimental result.
3. **Best-hardware result.** If only `performance + MTP` crosses 2s, it is
   recorded as its own claim: single-model chat latency is viable under the
   performance power policy but not under the historical balanced one.

### Predictions

1. **PRIMARY: `MTP n=4 + balanced` does NOT go below 2.0s.** Expected band
   **2.3-3.1s**, centre near 2.6s.
2. **`performance` helps less than MTP does**, and the two do not multiply
   cleanly.
3. **Chat/off MTP-4 acceptance >= 83%** - short conversational continuations
   should be easier to predict than long legal reasoning. **Falsified if under
   83%.**
4. **TOKEN-COUNT INVARIANCE: MTP does not change median generated tokens on
   chat/off by more than 10%.** If it does, the latency gain is a compound
   behavioural-plus-inference effect and must be reported as one rather than as
   a hardware optimisation.
5. **Decode speedup on chat is at least the 2.08x E59 measured on legal text**,
   given prediction 3.

### Decomposition recorded per cell

Warm median wall, TTFT/prompt-processing split (`timings.prompt_ms`), generated
tokens, measured generation tok/s, draft acceptance, accepted drafts per
verification, effective tokens per verification, and latency normalised to the
vanilla answer length. The normalisation exists to separate *MTP decoded faster*
from *MTP emitted fewer tokens*, which E59's `F1` proved is a real distinction.

**Note on execution:** `powerprofilesctl set` is polkit-denied for bench,
so the two `performance` cells require the owner to switch the profile. The two
`balanced` cells run now; the profile is RECORDED in every result file rather
than assumed.

## E60 RESULTS (2026-08-19): MTP is worth 2x on documents and 1.3x on chat, because ACCEPTANCE is a property of the workload

| chat tier, 27B `off` | balanced | performance | profile effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| MTP off | 5.34s | 5.31s | +0.6% |
| **MTP n=4** | **4.05s** | **4.04s** | +0.2% |
| MTP effect | **1.32x** | **1.31x** | - |

**The registered falsifier did not fire: 4.05s against 2.0s.** The two-model
recommendation stands, by a wider margin than the projection suggested.

**The power profile does nothing measurable.** 0.6% and 0.2%, inside run-to-run
noise, and the MTP effect is identical under both policies (1.32x vs 1.31x).
Prediction 2 held in its strongest form - not "helps less than MTP" but "does
not help". **That branch is closed**: there is no reason to revisit power policy
for inference on this box, and the fact that every previously published figure
was measured under `balanced` costs nothing.

### Predictions: two held, three falsified, and all three falsifications have ONE cause

| # | prediction | result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | MTP n=4 + balanced not below 2.0s, band 2.3-3.1s | direction right, **band FALSIFIED** at 4.05s |
| 2 | `performance` helps less than MTP, no clean multiplication | **HELD** |
| 3 | chat/off acceptance >= 83% | **FALSIFIED** at 30.2% |
| 4 | token-count invariance within 10% | **HELD exactly**, 57 tokens in all four cells |
| 5 | chat decode speedup >= E59's 2.08x | **FALSIFIED** at 1.32x |

**F85. Draft acceptance is a property of the WORKLOAD, not of the model, the
backend or the hardware.** Same GGUF, same build, same Vulkan device, same
`--spec-type draft-mtp --spec-draft-n-max 4`:

| workload | acceptance | accepted/verification | decode gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| held-out legal reasoning at `low` (E59) | **83.4%** | 3.34 | **2.08x** |
| short conversational prose at `off` (E60) | **30.2%** | 1.21 | **1.32x** |

The direction is the opposite of what was registered. Formulaic legal answers -
structured citations, templated phrasing, a fixed two-line response shape - are
easy for a draft head to predict. Free-form explanatory prose is not. **A
speculative-decoding speedup measured on one workload does not transfer to
another**, and 30.2% is below the 60% threshold at which speculation is
generally held to pay for itself at all.

The 30.2% reproduced to the decimal on the `performance` arm, an independent run
at a different power policy, so it is not a sampling artefact - though the chat
sample is 168 draft events against the legal conversation's 1,398, so the figure
should not be quoted to a decimal.

**This was the caveat that carried the experiment.** E59's acceptance was
deliberately not inherited. Inheriting 83.4% would have projected roughly 2.6s;
the measured answer is 4.05s. The architecture argument would have been wrong by
1.4 seconds.

### Token-count invariance held exactly, and it scopes the E59 concern

**57 generated tokens in all four cells**, a 0.0% change. So on chat the 1.32x
is pure inference gain with no behavioural component. The "quality change
wearing a performance costume" worry from E59 - where `F1` used 41% fewer tokens
for the same answer - appeared **only in the long legal conversation at
`reasoning_effort: low`**, not in short chat at `off`. That is a scope, not a
general property, and any future MTP decision should re-check it per workload
rather than assume either result.

### What this settles operationally

**Enable MTP for document work; on chat the gain is real but does not change the
routing decision.** 5.34s to 4.05s is a 24% latency reduction, which is not
nothing - it is simply far short of what a routing change would need. 2.08x with
answers unchanged on legal reasoning, against 1.32x with acceptance below the
threshold on chat. That is a routing conclusion in its own right, and it aligns
with the two-model design rather than cutting against it, because the 27B is
where document work already goes.

Machine facts unchanged from the E59 table. The `performance` arms were run by
the owner switching the profile (polkit denies bench), and the profile was
restored to `balanced` afterwards; every result file records the profile it ran
under rather than assuming it.
