## E33: executable coding eval - Qwen3.8-27B vs local coding incumbents (2026-08-15, pre-registered before any run)

**Why this exists:** the eval-pilot suite saturates - every 20B+ model scores
17/17 - so it discriminates nothing and must not be used to answer "is
Qwen3.8 better". There is also NO coding coverage anywhere in results/. This
adds an executable one: 15 Python tasks (2 easy / 7 medium / 6 hard), hidden
unit tests, graded by RUNNING the code. Execution grading also removes the
grader-artifact failure class that cost the eval pilot a point in 2026-07.

**Config.** `tools/run_coding_eval.py`, llama-server b10435, `-c 16384`,
`-np 1` (so -c is per-request, F20), temperature 0 (F59 determinism),
`max_tokens 3072`. Candidate code executes in a bounded subprocess (15s wall,
scratch cwd, no stdin). Rule 3 full-offload gate asserted by the harness and
FAILS on zero evidence. Relay down throughout.

**Arms.** Qwen3.8-27B-Q4_K_M (subject); Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B (the incumbent
local coding pick); Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 (the workhorse, recorded as
NOT suitable for agentic coding - included precisely to test whether that
verdict shows up here).

**Predictions (registered before the run):**

1. **Easy tier is a harness check, not a model test:** every arm scores 2/2.
   A failure there means the prompt or the harness is wrong, not the model,
   and invalidates that arm until fixed.
2. **Overall pass counts:** Qwen3.8 **10-13/15**; Qwen3-Coder-30B **9-12/15**;
   workhorse **6-10/15**.
3. **Hard tier (n=6):** Qwen3.8 **3-5**; Qwen3-Coder-30B **3-5**; workhorse
   **1-3**.
4. **Discriminating claim: Qwen3.8 >= Qwen3-Coder-30B on the HARD tier.** The
   model card claims substantial coding gains; if a 27B dense generalist does
   not match a dedicated coder model at the quant and stack we can actually
   run, that claim does not transfer to this box and the routing guide should
   not change.
5. **Truncation is its own outcome and is predicted LOW: <=2 tasks for
   Qwen3.8.** Thinking is on by default for this model, and F20's lesson is
   that a truncated answer silently recorded as a capability result is the
   trap. If truncations exceed 2, the budget is the finding - re-run at a
   larger `max_tokens` and declare the amendment here BEFORE the re-run.
6. **Cost:** Qwen3.8 wall time per task is **3-8x** the workhorse's, from the
   generation-rate gap (12.6 vs ~95 t/s) partly offset by the workhorse
   emitting no reasoning tokens.

**Not predicted:** which specific tasks fail. Naming them after seeing the
set would be theatre; the tier bands above are the falsifiable part.

**Amendments:** AMENDMENT 1 below.

### E33 AMENDMENT 1 (decided 02:45, BEFORE the affected run - but FILED LATE, and that is recorded)

**The amendment.** `max_tokens` is raised from the registered **3072 to
8192** for every arm. Owner instruction: reasoning models need budget, and
F20's lesson is that a truncated answer silently recorded as a capability
result is the trap. Prediction 5 registered truncations at **<=2 for
Qwen3.8** and explicitly said that if truncations exceed 2 the budget is the
finding and the re-run must be declared first - this raises the budget up
front instead, so prediction 5 is now resolved against the 8192 budget and
a truncation count above 2 becomes a much stronger result than it would
have been at 3072.

**No other prediction changes.** The tier bands and the discriminating claim
(4) stand exactly as written.

**The filing error, stated rather than smoothed over.** This amendment was
written into HANDOFF.md and committed at **02:45:12** (`78bf349`,
verifiable), and the affected run started at **02:59:18** - so the decision
genuinely predates the run by 14 minutes and the code carried it
(`--max-tokens` default 8192). But `results/experiments.md` is the registry
of record, and the amendment was not transcribed here until 03:06, seven
minutes AFTER the run began. Nothing about the experiment is compromised;
what failed is the filing, and a reader of the registry alone would have
seen a run executed at 8192 against a registration that said 3072 with no
amendment.

**This is the fourth pre-registration slip of the night and the first of its
kind.** The other three (F65) were specification errors - the registration
named the wrong thing. This one specifies correctly and files late, in the
wrong document. The lesson is narrower than F65's and worth keeping separate:
**an amendment is declared when it lands in the REGISTRY, not when it is
decided or when it reaches a bookmark file.** HANDOFF.md is a resume aid, not
a pre-registration.

### E33 AMENDMENT 2 (declared 2026-08-15 08:39, BEFORE the affected re-run)

**The trigger fired.** Prediction 5 registered truncations at **<=2 for
Qwen3.8** and said in terms that if truncations exceed 2 the budget is the
finding and the re-run must be declared here first. The 8192-budget run
produced **5 truncations in 6 failures** (9/15 pass). This is that
declaration.

**The amendment.** `max_tokens` 8192 -> **24576**, `-c` 16384 -> **32768**,
**Qwen3.8 arm only.** The other two arms recorded ZERO truncations at 8192,
so their scores are budget-independent and re-running them would change
nothing while costing hours of GPU. Asymmetric budgets are legitimate here
precisely because the budget is not the variable under test - the pass count
is - and an arm that never hit the cap cannot be affected by raising it.
This is recorded because it is the sort of asymmetry that looks like
favouritism if it is not argued.

**Why 24576 and not 12288.** The spend is bimodal, and that is the whole
reason to re-run rather than nudge. Passing tasks used **217-2,547**
completion tokens; every truncation used **exactly 8192** and emitted
21K-33K characters of reasoning with **no code at all**. Nothing sits in
between. A budget just above the observed pass ceiling would not distinguish
"needed a little more room" from "entered a reasoning spiral that no finite
budget ends" - 3x the current cap does, because a spiral will still hit it.

**Registered prediction for the re-run, and it is falsifiable in both
directions.** Of the 5 truncated tasks, **at least 3 complete and emit code**
at 24576. If 3 or more truncate AGAIN at 24576, the finding is NOT a budget
finding: it is that this model enters non-terminating reasoning on a
identifiable class of task, which is a materially worse property for an
interactive coding assistant than a low pass rate, and the coding verdict
must say so. Recorded field: `outcome == "truncated"` count in the re-run
JSON, over the SAME 5 task ids.

**Prediction 4 (the discriminating claim, Qwen3.8 >= Qwen3-Coder-30B on the
hard tier) is resolved against the RE-RUN**, not the 8192 run. The 9/15 is
not quotable as a capability result and must not appear in any verdict.

llama-bench-comparable quantity is `mean_stream_gen_tps`. Measured:
aggregate 9.99 (outside the band), per-stream 12.42 (inside, and 1.48% from
the triplet mean of 12.607 - just within F15). The harnesses agree; my

**This is the THIRD pre-registration specification error in one night** (the

### E32 AMENDMENT 2 (declared 2026-08-15 02:22, BEFORE the affected re-run)

The 45s sweep shows the Qwen3.8 arm still RISING at 8 slots (capacity 1.83 ->
2.65 -> 2.94x, decelerating but not flat) while the MoE control has
effectively plateaued (1.48 -> 1.97 -> 2.25x, and its recorded historical
figure is 2.1x at SIXTEEN slots). A sweep that stops where one arm is still
climbing cannot locate its knee.

**Amendment:** extend both arms to `-np 16` and re-run the whole sweep at
`--duration 180` (Amendment 1). Registered addition: **Qwen3.8 @16 exceeds
its own @8 by at least 5% on capacity** - i.e. the knee is NOT at 8. If it
does not, 8 is the knee and the deceleration seen at 45s was the plateau
arriving, not sampling noise.

KV headroom checked before committing to 16: this model puts KV on only 1
layer in 4 (`full_attention_interval = 4`), 4 KV heads x 256 head_dim x f16
= ~64 KiB/token, so 16 x 4096 = 65,536 tokens costs ~4 GiB on top of 15.65
GiB of weights. Comfortable on 128 GB.

**THE REGISTERED FALSIFIER FIRED, and is resolved rather than waived.** The
falsifier reads: *if slots=1 disagrees with the triplet by more than F15's
+/-1.5%, the harness and llama-bench are measuring different things and NO
scaling number from this run may be quoted until that is resolved.* Measured
`mean_stream_gen_tps` at slots=1 is **12.40 against the triplet's 12.607, a
-1.64% disagreement** - outside the band. It is not waived as "close
enough"; here is the resolution.

The disagreement is **systematic, not noise**. The 45s sweep measured 12.42
and the 180s sweep 12.40 on the same config - the two serve_bench runs agree
with each other to **0.16%**, which is also the llama-bench triplet's own
spread. An instrument that repeats to 0.16% and sits 1.6% below another
instrument is not noisy; it is measuring a slightly different thing. The
difference is the serving path: serve_bench generates over HTTP through
llama-server with real request handling, tokenisation and sampling per
stream, where llama-bench generates in-process. **~1.6% is the cost of the
HTTP serving path**, and it is a defensible number to carry rather than a
defect to chase.

**Why the scaling numbers survive it.** Every multiplier in the table is a
ratio taken *within one arm on one instrument*, normalised to that arm's own
slots=1 measured by serve_bench. A constant multiplicative offset cancels
exactly in such a ratio. The falsifier was written to catch the case where
the two harnesses disagree about *what they are measuring* in a way that
would corrupt the scaling claims; the offset is real but common-mode, so it
does not. **Absolute t/s from this sweep must be quoted as serve_bench
numbers and are ~1.6% below the llama-bench figure for the same config** -
that is the standing caveat this resolution buys.

**Prediction 3, the discriminating claim: CONFIRMED, and reproduced.**
Qwen3.8's @8 multiplier exceeds the workhorse's on both metrics - 2.84 vs
2.20 aggregate, 2.94 vs 2.24 capacity - and it holds at 16 (3.12 vs 2.58
capacity). The 180s figures reproduce the 45s figures to within 0.4%, which
retires any suspicion that the 45s result was a sampling artefact. Dense
models do batch relatively better than this MoE, as bandwidth amortisation
predicted.

**Prediction 2, the magnitude bands: MISSES LOW, unchanged by better
sampling.** Registered Qwen3.8 @8 at 3.5-6.0x; measured 2.84x aggregate /
2.94x capacity. @4 registered 2.6-4.0x, measured 2.10x agg / 2.65x capacity
(capacity scrapes into the band, aggregate does not). @2 registered 1.6-2.0x,
measured 1.59x agg / 1.83x capacity. The control's @8 landed at the very top
of its registered 1.5-2.2x band (2.20 agg, 2.24 capacity - capacity a hair
over). So the DIRECTION of the bandwidth-amortisation argument survives and
its SIZE does not, at both durations. A naive weights-read-once-per-batch
model overstates the gain by roughly a factor of two.

**Amendment 2's registered addition: CONFIRMED, narrowly.** Qwen3.8 @16
exceeds its own @8 by **+6.37% on capacity** against a registered threshold
of 5%, so 8 is NOT the knee and the 45s deceleration was sampling, not

**Amendment 2's PREMISE is falsified, and that is the more useful result.**
The amendment was justified by the claim that the MoE control had
"effectively plateaued" while Qwen3.8 was still climbing. Extending both arms
to 16 shows the opposite: from 8 to 16 the control gains **+14.88% capacity
(+21.00% aggregate)** while Qwen3.8 gains **+6.37% (+9.16%)**. The control
has more than twice the remaining headroom at 16. Reading a plateau off an
arm that was itself under-sampled at 45s was the error; the arm that looked
finished was the one still climbing. This does not disturb Prediction 3 -
Qwen3.8's multipliers remain higher at every point - but it does retire the
"MoE is out of headroom at 8" reading that Amendment 2 was built on.

**The number that actually decides the routing question is none of the
above.** Relative scaling flatters Qwen3.8 because its base is so low. In
absolute terms the MoE workhorse delivers **7.18x** the aggregate throughput
at 1 slot, **5.54x** at 8 and **6.14x** at 16. Qwen3.8 never reaches at
SIXTEEN slots (30.97 t/s) even a HALF of what the workhorse delivers at ONE
(71.63 t/s). For the workhorse slot, a better scaling *curve* on a 6x lower
*base* is not a reason to switch.

**TTFT confirms and worsens the interactive picture.** 1651 ms at one slot
against the workhorse's 306 ms (5.4x), and **10,259 ms vs 1710 ms at
sixteen** (6.0x). A ten-second time-to-first-token makes the 16-slot
configuration unusable interactively regardless of its capacity gain, so
Qwen3.8's "still climbing at 16" headroom is only reachable in batch work.

not move the three routing verdicts, and the two things it did establish -
the discriminating claim, and the 6x absolute deficit - are both settled.
