## E51: does the same question get the same ADVICE twice? (2026-08-17, pre-registered before any run)

**Why this exists.** F70 established that a byte-identical prompt at three
different request positions produces three different answers at temperature 0,
because KV prefix reuse changes how prefill is chunked. That is a
reproducibility finding. It is not yet a **reliability** finding, and the
commercial question is the second one: **if you ask the same matter twice in a
session, do you get the same advice?**

**A preliminary reading that made this worth doing properly.** The three E46
answers for `m12` were compared by hand on 2026-08-17 and are **substantively
identical** - EUR 156,000, EUR 195,000, EUR 700, "no, rule 5", "rule 3" in all
three. Only the prose differs. That is one matter, at n=1, compared by eye, and
it is exactly the kind of preliminary signal that should be measured rather
than reported.

**ONE VARIABLE CHANGES: request position.** All 13 matters, run at three
positions each - alone, early in a short sequence, and late in the full
thirteen - reusing E46's exact design and extending it from one matter to all
of them.

**Grading is on the SEALED KEY, not on similarity.** Two answers agree if they
score the same on the same elements, not if they read alike. A similarity
metric would report the prose difference F70 already established and tell us
nothing new.

### Predictions (registered before any arm runs). The FIELD is named in each.

1. **PRIMARY: `element_agreement` is 65/65** - every matter scores identically
   at all three positions. Registered at the ceiling deliberately: the m12
   reading says substance is stable, and a band that allowed slippage would let
   me call any result a success.
2. **`answer_sha` differs at all three positions for at least 10 of 13
   matters**, reproducing F70 rather than assuming it. If answers turn out to
   be byte-identical here, F70's mechanism does not generalise beyond m12 and
   that is its own finding.
3. **Where a matter scores below 5/5, it scores the SAME below-5 at all three
   positions.** A model that gets m09 3/5 at one position and 5/5 at another is
   a different and worse problem than one that is consistently imperfect.

**Falsifier: if any matter's score changes with request position, temperature-0
local serving is not reliable for advisory work without pinning the request
sequence**, and that becomes a deployment constraint that has to be written
into the routing guide - not a footnote.

**Cost.** 13 matters x 3 positions, and position 3 is the full 13-matter
sequence which E46 measured at 4,978s. Roughly two hours.

### E50 AMENDMENT 1 (declared 2026-08-17, BEFORE any E50 arm runs)

**The registration above mis-states the defect, and reading the thirteen task
definitions before building the instrument is what caught it.** The amendment
is declared rather than the registration silently rewritten.

**What I registered:** that the model fails "the branch where the correct
action is to do nothing", and that the open question is tool-call versus prose.

**What the E45 data actually shows.** Qwen3.8 at `medium` fails exactly two of
thirteen, and it passes four other negative branches:

| task | phrasing of the rule | branch | score |
|---|---|---|---|
| c1-urgent | "**If** balance over 5,000, put URGENT" | fires | 5/5 |
| **c2-not-urgent** | "**If** ... , put URGENT, **otherwise** keep normal" | must not fire | **2/5** |
| c4-date-before | "**If** completion before 1 Oct, put EXPEDITED" | fires | 4/5 |
| **c5-date-after** | "**If** ... , **otherwise** use a normal title" | must not fire | **0/5** |
| c10-compound-both | "Put URGENT **only if** balance over 5,000 AND litigation" | fires | 5/5 |
| **c11-compound-one** | "Put URGENT **only if** ... AND ..." | **must not fire** | **5/5** |
| c9-select-no-email | "**If** balance is zero, **also** email" | must not fire | 5/5 |
| c7-cat-probate | "**If** litigation ... ; if anything else, ROUTINE" | other branch | 5/5 |

**So "negative branches fail" is false.** c11 is a negative branch on the same
`no_urgent_in_title` check as c2 and scores **5/5 against c2's 2/5**. c9 and c7
are negative branches and score 5/5.

**The pattern that survives the data is PHRASING.** Both failures are phrased
*"If X, do A ... otherwise do B"*. Both clean negative-branch passes on a
marker-insertion task are phrased *"do A only if X"*. Under "if ... otherwise"
the model inserts the marker regardless of the condition; under "only if" it
withholds it correctly.

**E50 is therefore re-scoped to a 2x2, and it is a better experiment than the
one I registered.** Two factors, same underlying conditions throughout:

| | "If X, do A; otherwise B" | "Do A only if X" |
|---|---|---|
| **tool call** | c2, c5 (measured: 2/5, 0/5) | c11 (measured: 5/5) |
| **prose advice** | to build | to build |

**Revised predictions, replacing 1-5 above. The FIELD is named in each.**

1. **PRIMARY: restating c2 and c5 with "only if" phrasing raises
   `success_rate` to >= 4/5 each**, in the tool-call modality, with nothing else
   changed. This is the discriminating claim: if phrasing is the cause, the
   rewrite fixes it.
2. **The positive branches stay >= 4/5 under the rewrite.** A rewrite that
   fixed the negative branch by making the model never insert the marker would
   show up here, and would mean the rewrite broke the task rather than fixed
   the model.
3. **In prose, `negative_branch_correct` is >= 5/6 under BOTH phrasings.** The
   prose modality is predicted to be robust to phrasing, because E43's `m12`
   and L2's `Z1` both show reliable "this does not apply" reasoning in written
   answers.
4. **The workhorse shows the same phrasing sensitivity in tool calls.** If it
   does not, the effect is Qwen-specific rather than a property of the task.

**Falsifier: if the "only if" rewrite does NOT lift c2 and c5, phrasing is not
the mechanism** and the original modality question stands unanswered - in which
case the prose arm still runs and still separates the two.

**Why this matters more than the version I registered.** If phrasing is the
cause, the fix is free and immediately actionable: write conditionals as "do X
only if Y" and the defect largely disappears. That is a better outcome for a
reader than "do not automate conditional actions", and it is the kind of thing
the routing guide should carry.

### E51 REFINEMENT (declared 2026-08-17, BEFORE any E51 arm result is read)

**Prediction 1 named the wrong FIELD, and F65 says that makes it unfalsifiable
in the way that matters.** It registered `element_agreement` as "every matter
scores identically at all three positions". A score is not enough: two runs can
both score a matter **4/5 while missing DIFFERENT elements**, which is the same
number and different advice - exactly the case a score-only check waves through.

**Prediction 1 is therefore split into three fields, all of which must hold:**

1a. `score_identical` = 13/13 - the same hits at every position.
1b. `missed_set_identical` = 13/13 - where a matter is imperfect, it misses the
    **same elements** at every position.
1c. `traps_identical` = 13/13 - no trap reproduced at one position and not
    another. This is the worst version of the result, because traps are the
    specific wrong answers the corpus exists to catch.

The bands are unchanged in spirit - all three at the ceiling - so this tightens
the prediction rather than loosening it after the fact. Nothing has been read
from the E51 arms; arm 2 was still running when this was declared.

`spikes/closed-record/compare_positions.py` implements all three and was
validated before use against a known-different pair: E43 arm A against arm B
differ on exactly `m01-scope-variation` (4/5 -> 5/5), which is what the E43
result records, and the tool reports that one matter and no others.

### E50 AMENDMENT 2 (declared 2026-08-17, BEFORE any E50 arm runs)

**Reading the six prompts in full sharpens the hypothesis and kills one version
of it.** AMENDMENT 1 said the failures are phrased "If X, do A ... otherwise do
B" and the passes are "do A only if X". Half of that is wrong: **both** pairs
carry an else-clause.

| task | prompt shape | branch | score |
|---|---|---|---|
| c4-date-before | "**If** completion falls before 1 Oct, put EXPEDITED ...; **otherwise** use a normal title" | fires | 4/5 |
| c5-date-after | *identical wording*, later date | must not fire | **0/5** |
| c10-compound-both | "**Put** URGENT in the title **only if** balance > 5,000 AND litigation. **Otherwise** use a normal title." | fires | 5/5 |
| c11-compound-one | *identical wording*, conveyancing matter | must not fire | **5/5** |

So the presence of "otherwise" is NOT the variable. What differs is **clause
order**: condition-first (`If X, do A`) versus imperative-first with an "only
if" qualifier (`Do A only if X`). Under condition-first the negative branch
collapses to 0/5; under imperative-first it holds at 5/5.

**A confound in that comparison, named because it is real.** c10/c11 are
**compound** conditions (balance AND matter type) while c4/c5 are simple. The
phrasing reading could therefore be an artefact of compound-ness - a model that
evaluates a conjunction carefully might simply be more careful generally.

**Four tasks added to separate them.** `c1b`, `c2b`, `c4b`, `c5b` hold the
condition **simple** and change only the phrasing to imperative-first "only if",
keeping the else-clause so that is constant too. Each pair keeps both branches,
because a model that never writes the marker passes every negative branch and
fails every positive one.

| new task | condition | branch | phrasing |
|---|---|---|---|
| c1b-urgent-onlyif | balance 7,400 > 5,000 | fires | "Put URGENT ... only if ..." |
| c2b-not-urgent-onlyif | balance 300 | must not fire | same |
| c4b-date-before-onlyif | completion 15 Sep | fires | "Put EXPEDITED ... only if ..." |
| c5b-date-after-onlyif | completion 20 Nov | must not fire | same |

**Revised prediction 1, replacing AMENDMENT 1's:** `success_rate` for
**c2b >= 4/5 and c5b >= 4/5**, against c2's 2/5 and c5's 0/5. If both lift with
a simple condition, phrasing is the cause and compound-ness is not.

**Revised falsifier: if c2b and c5b score at or below their originals, clause
order is not the mechanism** and the effect in c10/c11 belongs to the compound
structure or to something else those two tasks share. The prose arm still runs
and still separates modality from reasoning.

**A harness defect fixed in the same pass, same class as four earlier ones.**
`score()` fell through to `return False` for an unrecognised `check` name. A
typo'd grader name would therefore have scored every run of that task 0/5 and
read as a capability failure, with nothing in the output saying otherwise -
F20 with a typo as the cause. It now raises. Verified: the guard fires on an
unknown name, all four new graders reject and accept the right titles, and
`validate_tooluse_conditional.py` still passes 20/20.

### E51 RESULT - the advice is STABLE across request position. Prose varies, substance does not.

Three arms, identical config (`thinking=low`, `max_tokens 24576`, temperature 0,
`-c 32768`), differing only in the order the 13 matters were asked in:
sorted (E43 arm A, already measured), reverse (m13 first), and a 7-rotation
chosen so no matter keeps a position it held in either of the others.

| field | result |
|---|---|
| `score_identical` | **13/13** |
| `missed_set_identical` | **13/13** |
| `traps_identical` | **13/13** |
| run totals | **59/65, 59/65, 59/65** |

**PREDICTIONS 1a, 1b and 1c ALL HELD at the ceiling.** Every matter scored the
same at every position; where a matter is imperfect it missed the **same
elements** each time (m01, m03, m06, m07 at 4/5 and m09 at 3/5, identically);
and no trap was reproduced at one position and not another.

**PREDICTION 3 HELD.** The five imperfect matters are imperfect in the same way
at all three positions.

**PREDICTION 2 IS FALSIFIED, and the direction is the interesting part.** I
registered that `answer_sha` would differ at all three positions for **at least
10 of 13** matters, on the assumption that F70's effect was general. Measured:

| distinct answers across the 3 orders | matters |
|---|---|
| 1 (byte-identical everywhere) | **6/13** |
| 2 | 6/13 |
| 3 (what I predicted for >=10) | **1/13** |

So **6 of 13 matters reproduce byte-for-byte regardless of where they sit in
the sequence**, and only one produced three different answers. F70's
byte-divergence is real - 7 of 13 vary somehow - but it is a minority effect,
not the general rule I inferred from a single matter. F70 was established on
m12, and m12 is one of the six that varies; generalising from it was the error.

**What this settles, and it is the point of the experiment.** F70 said
temperature-0 determinism is sequence-scoped. That remains true at the byte
level. But the commercial question was whether the ADVICE changes, and it does
not: **the wording moves on 7 of 13 matters and the answer moves on none of
them.** Recorded as F75.

**What it does NOT establish.** One model, one effort level, one corpus, three
orderings of thirteen matters. It does not show that no ordering anywhere can
change an answer - it shows that none of the three tested did, across 39
matter-runs. A stronger claim needs either more orderings or an argument about
why the mechanism cannot reach the answer, and I have neither.

### E50 RESULT - prediction 1 FALSIFIED, and the experiment turns out to be UNDERPOWERED

17 conditional tasks x 5 runs, `--jinja`, `-c 32768`, `max_tokens 4096`,
Qwen3.8 at `medium` and the workhorse. Zero format leaks in all 170 runs.

| task | qwen38 med | workhorse |
|---|---|---|
| c1-urgent ("If X, ...") | 5/5 | 5/5 |
| c1b-urgent-onlyif | 5/5 | 5/5 |
| **c2-not-urgent** ("If X ... otherwise") | **3/5** | 0/5 |
| **c2b-not-urgent-onlyif** | **3/5** | 0/5 |
| c4-date-before | 1/5 | 0/5 |
| c4b-date-before-onlyif | 1/5 | 0/5 |
| **c5-date-after** | **1/5** | 0/5 |
| **c5b-date-after-onlyif** | **2/5** | **5/5** |

**PREDICTION 1 FALSIFIED.** It registered c2b >= 4/5 and c5b >= 4/5 for
Qwen3.8. Measured **3/5 and 2/5**. The imperative-first "only if" rewrite did
not lift the negative branch on this model.

**But the experiment cannot support that conclusion either, and that is the
real result.** Re-running the THIRTEEN ORIGINAL tasks in the same session
exposed the problem:

| task | E45 | E50 | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| c4-date-before | 4/5 | **1/5** | **-3** |
| c11-compound-one | 5/5 | 4/5 | -1 |
| c2-not-urgent | 2/5 | 3/5 | +1 |
| c5-date-after | 0/5 | 1/5 | +1 |
| the other nine | 5/5 | 5/5 | 0 |

**Four of thirteen unchanged tasks moved between sessions, one of them by three
of five runs.** `c4-date-before` was the evidence that the positive branch is
reliable; it is 4/5 in one session and 1/5 in the next, same model, same effort,
same prompt.

**The cause is in the harness and was always visible: `temperature` is 0.2 and
`runs` is 5.** Both are FIXED deliberately so every E33-lineage number stays
comparable, and the comment in the file says so. At temperature 0.2 each run is
a sample, and five samples of an intermediate success rate has a standard error
of roughly one run. The four tasks that moved are exactly the four whose rate is
in the middle; the nine at 5/5 did not move at all, and **the workhorse - every
one of whose tasks sits at 0/5 or 4-5/5 - reproduced its mean EXACTLY, 68% to
68%, moving on none of thirteen.**

So the instrument is stable where the score is saturated and unstable precisely
where the interesting effects are.

**What survives.** The MEANS are far steadier than the per-task cells: over the
same 13 tasks, Qwen3.8 goes 86% -> **83%** and the workhorse 68% -> **68%**. The
ranking that drove yesterday's routing-guide correction (Qwen3.8 > workhorse >
Mistral) is unaffected.

**What does NOT survive, and it is mine to withdraw.** Yesterday I put
per-task figures into the routing guide and the task guide: that `c4-date-before`
is solved 4/5, that `c5-date-after` is 0/5, and that "nothing tested handles" it.
Those were single n=5 samples presented as properties of the model. `c5` has now
scored 0/5, 1/5 and 2/5 across three measurements, and the workhorse scored
**5/5** on `c5b`. Being corrected in both guides.

**E50 is therefore not evidence about phrasing.** It cannot separate "the
rewrite does not help" from "n=5 cannot see it". Answering the phrasing question
needs a powered design - more runs per task, or temperature 0, or both - and
that is registered as E53 rather than smuggled into this entry.
